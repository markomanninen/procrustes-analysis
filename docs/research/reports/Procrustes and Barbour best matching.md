# Best matching turns Procrustes into a connection

Procrustes analysis is the least-squares superimposition of one labelled point set on another after translation, rotation and, if wanted, scale and reflection are removed; it began as target rotation in factor analysis (Mosier 1939, Green 1952, named by Hurley and Cattell in 1962, solved by SVD by Schönemann in 1966, extended to many configurations by Gower in 1975), was found again as Wahba's satellite-attitude problem, the Kabsch algorithm and absolute orientation, and in 1984 became the metric of Kendall's shape spaces. Julian Barbour's best matching, introduced with Bruno Bertotti in 1982 as the "intrinsic derivative", is the same fit with masses as weights, fixed labels and proper motions only, but taken between infinitesimally close configurations, and that one change turns a distance into a connection: horizontal motions have zero total momentum and angular momentum (and, after Barbour's 2003 extension to scale, zero dilatational momentum), the quotient is Kendall's shape space (Edward Anderson, 2007), and chaining the fit around a closed loop of shapes rotates the configuration by half the enclosed solid angle on the shape sphere, the falling-cat holonomy that a one-shot Procrustes fit cannot see. General relativity applies the same logic twice: Christoffel symbols compensate for relabelling between neighbouring points, and Barbour's best-matching vector field, which is the ADM shift, compensates for relabelling between neighbouring instants and yields the momentum constraint. What no connection can absorb is a discrete relabelling (a vertex permutation, a mirror image, a large diffeomorphism), and that is exactly where the repository's triangle page jumps. With mirrors allowed its 12 candidates form one orbit of the reflection group D3h on Kendall's sphere, so the fitted triangle jumps only when the target ABC passes through an isosceles or flat shape, crossings by DEF only rename vertices, and the best candidate has a closed form, pairing vertices by the rank of the opposite side and mirroring if and only if the orientations differ, which matched exhaustive search in 40,000 of 40,000 random trials in this session. Each ingredient is published (Groth's 1986 star-matching labelling, Kendall's "spherical blackboard", the Coxeter chamber lemma), but the assembled least-squares statement, the asymmetry between target and source, and its failure without mirrors were not found in any source that could be read.

## Procrustes analysis was invented four times before it became shape geometry

### Factor rotation, satellite attitude, crystals and cameras posed one problem

The problem started in psychometrics. **Charles Mosier (1939)** asked for "the transformation which will transform the second matrix into the first" so that two factor studies could be compared "directly and quantitatively" ([Mosier 1939](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02288493)); his transformation was unconstrained, the later "oblique" Procrustes problem. **Bert Green (1952)** solved the orthogonal least-squares version for full-rank matrices ([Green 1952](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02288918)), and **Hurley and Cattell (1962)** are credited with the name, in "The Procrustes program: producing direct rotation to test a hypothesized factor structure" ([Hurley & Cattell 1962](https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830070216)). The name carried a warning from the start: Procrustes made his guests fit his bed, and a target rotation always makes data look like the hypothesis. **Peter Schönemann (1966)** gave the general SVD solution of AT = B + E with T′T = I, valid for matrices of less than full rank ([Schönemann 1966](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02289451)); Schönemann and Carroll (1970) added translation and "central dilation" ([Schönemann & Carroll 1970](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02291266)); and **John Gower (1975)** generalised the method to many configurations, "translating, rotating, reflecting and scaling" each towards a common centroid read as a consensus ([Gower 1975](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02291478)). Ten Berge (1977) showed that the published optimality conditions for more than two matrices were insufficient and gave the scaling step that generalized Procrustes analysis still uses ([Ten Berge 1977](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02294053)).

The same mathematics was found at least three more times, each community keeping its own name. **Grace Wahba** posed the best rotation between two sets of unit vectors as SIAM Problem 65-1 for satellite attitude ([Wahba 1965](https://doi.org/10.1137/1007077)); **Wolfgang Kabsch** solved it for crystallography in 1976, and his 1978 note is commonly cited for the improper-rotation case ([Kabsch 1976](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0567739476001873); [Kabsch 1978](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0567739478001680)); and metrology and computer vision produced Hanson and Norris (1981), Arun, Huang and Blostein (1987), Horn's unit-quaternion solution (1987) and Umeyama (1991), who added scale and fixed the tendency of earlier solutions to return "a reflection instead when the data is severely corrupted" ([Hanson & Norris 1981](https://doi.org/10.1137/0902029); [Horn 1987](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bkph/papers/Absolute_Orientation.pdf); [Umeyama 1991](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.88573)). A 2019 NIST paper calls the result the "Kabsch-Umeyama algorithm", "the algorithm of choice", which "is effectively no more than the computation of the singular value decomposition of a particular matrix" ([Lawrence, Bernal & Witzgall 2019](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/124/jres.124.028.pdf)). The split mattered for reflections: psychometrics allowed any orthogonal T, while the physical sciences needed det = +1, which is why the determinant correction carries Kabsch's and Umeyama's names.

The fourth discovery made Procrustes into geometry. **David Kendall** introduced a "theory of shape" in 1977 and in 1984 published "Shape manifolds, Procrustean metrics, and complex projective spaces", showing that the shapes of k labelled points in the plane form the complex projective space CP^{k−2} ([Kendall 1984](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/16.2.81); [Kendall 1989](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1177012582)); for triangles this is a sphere of radius ½. The theory grew partly out of an archaeological question: Kendall and Kendall (1980) counted nearly collinear ("ε-blunt") triads among random points to test whether standing stones and ley lines show more alignments than chance ([Kendall & Kendall 1980](https://doi.org/10.2307/1426603); [Broadbent 1980](https://doi.org/10.2307/2981985)), so **flat triangles, the equator of the shape sphere, were the first locus the theory studied**. Goodall, Kent, Dryden and Mardia then built the statistics on this geometry ([Goodall 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1991.tb01825.x); [Kent 1994](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1994.tb01978.x); [Dryden & Mardia 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119072492)). Kendall's 1984 text could not be read in this research, so the S²(½) statement rests on Montgomery, Anderson and Dryden's lecture notes, which agree.

### In the plane the whole fit is one complex division

In any dimension the recipe is the same. Centre both sets, since under isotropic least squares translation always separates; take the SVD of the cross-covariance and form the rotation from its singular vectors; if a proper rotation is required, multiply by diag(1, …, 1, det), which flips the direction of the smallest singular value and costs exactly twice that value in the fit; and take the scale as the corrected trace divided by the source's sum of squares ([Lawrence et al. 2019](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/124/jres.124.028.pdf); [Eigen's Umeyama implementation](https://eigen.googlesource.com/mirror/+/17af8c763d5aa58c40fc2c40ff6e8d6fc28a8d63/Eigen/src/Geometry/Umeyama.h)). The least-squares scale is asymmetric, since fitting A to B does not give the inverse of fitting B to A, which is why Horn preferred the ratio of root-mean-square sizes ([Horn 1987](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bkph/papers/Absolute_Orientation.pdf)).

Planar data need no SVD. Write centred configurations as complex vectors z (the target) and w (the configuration being fitted). Then **the optimal similarity is a complex regression without intercept, β̂e^{iθ̂} = w\*z / w\*w**, the residual is ‖z‖² − |w\*z|²/‖w‖², and with cos ρ = |w\*z|/(‖w‖‖z‖) the full and partial Procrustes distances are d_F = sin ρ and d_P = 2 sin(ρ/2), where ρ is the Riemannian distance in Kendall's shape space ([Dryden, lecture notes ch. 3](https://people.stat.sc.edu/dryden/STAT718A/notes/shape-chap3.pdf); [ch. 4](https://people.stat.sc.edu/dryden/STAT718A/notes/shape-chap4.pdf)). Dryden puts it plainly: "The full Procrustes fit of w onto y is actually obtained by complex linear regression of y on w." The repository's library is exactly this formula applied to each vertex pairing: its sum s = Σ z̄_i u_π(i) is z\*u, its turn is −arg s, and its scale, now named `beta` (formerly `rho`, which in Dryden and Mardia's notation is the shape distance), is |s|/Σ|u|² ([js/procrustes.js](../../../js/procrustes.js)). A mirror fit is complex conjugation: replacing u by ū makes the relevant correlation |uᵀz|, with no conjugate, and the reflection-allowed distance takes the larger of |w\*z| and |wᵀz|. A short derivation in this research shows that the determinant of the real 2×2 cross-covariance equals (|w\*z|² − |wᵀz|²)/4, so **in the plane the Kabsch correction fires exactly when the mirrored fit would beat the proper one**, the complex form of Dryden's remark that the last singular value is negative "in the cases where an orthogonal transformation (reflection and rotation) would produce a smaller sum of squares than just a rotation" ([Dryden ch. 5](https://people.stat.sc.edu/dryden/STAT718A/notes/shape-chap5.pdf)).

Two facts from this algebra organise the rest of the report. The turn must be the full argument of s: the GeoGebra construction on which the page was built used θ = atan(y/x), which cannot tell s from −s and so, whenever Re s < 0, turns DEF to the largest error instead of the smallest ([README](../../../README.md); [reflections.md §1](../../reflections.md)). And for triangles, |s|² = ‖z‖²‖u‖²(1 + n·n′)/2, where n and n′ are the two triangles' points on Kendall's sphere drawn at radius 1 ([reflections.md §4](../../reflections.md)); the left side divided by the sizes is cos²ρ, so the angle between two shape points on the page's sphere is 2ρ.

### Each field chooses its group, its reflection policy and its weak point

The variants sit on two independent axes ([Dryden ch. 5](https://people.stat.sc.edu/dryden/STAT718A/notes/shape-chap5.pdf)). One is how many configurations are fitted: ordinary Procrustes fits one onto another and is asymmetric, while generalized Procrustes (GPA) fits many onto their mean and is symmetric. The other is which transformations are allowed: partial Procrustes uses translation and rotation, full Procrustes adds isotropic scale, reflection Procrustes uses O(m) instead of SO(m), oblique Procrustes allows any linear map, and weighted, resistant and maximum-likelihood forms go further. GPA alternates ordinary fits against the mean of the others; its loss cannot increase, but it can stop at a non-global stationary point, and for planar data the full Procrustes mean is simply the leading eigenvector of a complex sum-of-products matrix ([Kent 1994](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1994.tb01978.x)). Resistant fits, generalised to many specimens by Rohlf and Slice, were the first robust alternative ([Rohlf & Slice 1990](https://doi.org/10.2307/2992207)).

**Geometric morphometrics** is the largest institutional user, and its practitioners say its development "is identified with the Procrustes paradigm" ([Adams, Rohlf & Slice 2013](https://doi.org/10.4404/hystrix-24.1-6283)). The standard pipeline digitises landmarks and sliding semilandmarks, runs GPA with unit centroid size, projects to a tangent space, and analyses with PCA and permutation-based Procrustes ANOVA ([Mitteroecker & Schaefer 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.24531); [Collyer & Adams 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13029)); MorphoJ's founding paper has about 3,400 Crossref citations, and geomorph reached version 4.1.1 in July 2026 ([Klingenberg 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1755-0998.2010.02924.x); [CRAN geomorph](https://cran.r-project.org/package=geomorph)). Reflections enter only on purpose, when a configuration is superimposed with its reflected copy to separate symmetric variation from asymmetry ([Klingenberg, Barluenga & Meyer 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0014-3820.2002.tb00117.x)). **Structural biology** uses the two-set, proper-rotation, no-scale problem and is the one field where solver speed drove a line of algorithms, from Kabsch through quaternions to Theobald's QCP, now the default in MDAnalysis ([Theobald 2005](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0108767305015266); [MDAnalysis docs](https://docs.mdanalysis.org/stable/documentation_pages/analysis/rms.html)); AlphaFold 3 reports protein–ligand success as the percentage of "pocket-aligned ligand r.m.s.d. < 2 Å" ([Abramson et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w)). **Vision, robotics and aerospace** use the same core as the inner step of ICP, whose founding paper by Besl and McKay has over 14,600 Crossref citations, and as an evaluation step that removes the unobservable frame (and, for monocular systems, scale) before error is measured, as in absolute trajectory error and Procrustes-aligned "Protocol #2" pose error ([Besl & McKay 1992](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.121791); [Martinez et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)).

The fields that compare configurations usually allow reflections, because their axes are arbitrary. Sensory science adopted Gower's GPA to reconcile assessors' private vocabularies in free-choice profiling ([Williams & Langron 1984](https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.2740350513)) and then had to test whether the consensus was real, since GPA "always produce[s] a matrix of maximal agreement" ([King & Arents 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-459X.1991.tb00500.x)). Psychometrics still uses target rotation to test factor replication: across **7,134** respondents in six languages, "when targeted rotations were used, the American factor structure was closely reproduced" ([McCrae & Costa 1997](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.52.5.509)). Ecology uses Jackson's PROTEST permutation test, implemented in vegan, which reports r = √(1 − ss) ([Jackson 1995](https://doi.org/10.1080/11956860.1995.11682297); [vegan reference](https://vegandevs.github.io/vegan/reference/procrustes.html)); population genetics compares genetic PCA maps with geography across 128 populations ([Wang, Zöllner & Rosenberg 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1002886)); and spatial transcriptomics aligns tissue slices with a Procrustes fit weighted by optimal-transport couplings ([Zeira et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01459-6)). Machine learning made orthogonal Procrustes a workhorse for aligning word embeddings across languages, where Smith et al. raised English–Italian precision@1 "from 34% to 43%" and MUSE refines adversarial maps with the closed form W\* = UVᵀ ([Smith et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03859); [Conneau et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087)), and it rehabilitated the Procrustes distance as a proper metric on neural representations ([Williams et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14739); [Ding, Denain & Steinhardt 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01661)). In fMRI, hyperalignment, an iterated GPA-like Procrustes alignment of subjects, raised between-subject classification of movie segments to **70.6% from 32.0%** ([Haxby et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.08.026)). Geodesy's seven-parameter Helmert datum transformation is a Procrustes problem that "requires neither initial starting values nor iteration" ([Paláncz et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.5047/eps.2010.10.004)).

The pitfalls recur because they come from least squares itself. Localised change is spread over all points (the morphometric "Pinocchio effect", or heteroscedastic error in structures, answered by maximum-likelihood superposition), the result depends on which points define the fit, the reflection policy must match the domain, and **correspondence is assumed known** ([von Cramon-Taubadel et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.20616); [Theobald & Wuttke 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0508445103)). The last is the deepest: where correspondence is unknown it becomes the hard part, from ICP's local minima to shape models for which manual landmarking is "almost impossible in three dimensions" ([Davies et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2002.1009388)). The users divide into two families, one using Procrustes to remove a gauge (structures, robotics, attitude, geodesy) and one using it to compare configurations (morphometrics, sensory panels, ecology, embeddings), and most practical mistakes come from carrying one family's defaults into the other's problem. Barbour belongs firmly to the first family; the triangle page sits on the second family's hardest problem, unknown correspondence.

## Barbour's best matching is a mass-weighted Procrustes fit taken to the infinitesimal limit

### Mach supplied the motive and Poincaré the test

Barbour's programme began with "relative-distance" theories that built kinetic energy directly from interparticle distances ([Barbour 1974](https://doi.org/10.1038/249328a0); [Barbour & Bertotti 1977](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02726208)). He later grouped them with Schrödinger's 1925 attempt and judged them dead, because they predict an anisotropic effective mass that the Hughes–Drever null experiments have "completely ruled out" ([Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)). The replacement was **Barbour and Bertotti (1982), "Mach's principle and the structure of dynamical theories"**, which proposed "intrinsic dynamics", recovered Newtonian dynamics "provided the angular momentum of the Universe is zero in the frame in which its momentum is zero", and fitted general relativity into the same pattern if the thin-sandwich conjecture holds ([Barbour & Bertotti 1982](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1982.0102)). Its device was the "intrinsic derivative"; Barbour renamed it **best matching in 1994** (CQG 11, 2853, p. 2862), as he notes in 2024 ([Barbour et al. 2024, fn. 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07480)). Mach supplied the motive ("The universe is not twice given, with an earth in rest and an earth in motion"), and Poincaré the precise test that Barbour calls the Mach–Poincaré principle: relational initial data, the separations and their rates of change, should fix the future ([Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368); [Mercati 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)). Newtonian mechanics fails that test because the separations carry no information about total angular momentum, so Newton's equations rewritten in them need third time derivatives, a point Barbour traces to Lagrange's 1772 three-body study ([Barbour 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)). The 1982 paper and the 1994 renaming could not be read in this research; their content is known from the abstract and from Barbour's later restatements.

### The definition is a Procrustes fit, and its optimality conditions are the constraints

Barbour's 2010 definition reads as a Procrustes recipe with masses: "Represent once and for all the first configuration by a vector X1 in some Cartesian frame of reference. Represent the second configuration in the same frame with its centre of mass in any arbitrary position, with any orientation and with any scale … find the necessarily unique position, orientation and scale for which this positive-definite quantity is minimized. It is readily seen that this 'best-matching' procedure, which reduces the 'incongruence' of the two figures to a minimum, brings the centres of mass to coincidence" ([Barbour 2010, §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)). His 2011 introduction draws it as a dashed triangle laid over a solid one and moved until "the two shapes, which are incongruent, have been brought as close as possible to congruence"; "Best matching brings the centres of mass to coincidence and reduces the net rotation to zero" ([Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)). Butterfield's gloss is the most Procrustean of all: put one overhead transparency of coloured dots on another so as "to minimize the weighted sum of the squared distances for red-to-red, green-to-green etc., with the weights given by a mass associated with each dot" ([Butterfield 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055)). For translations and rotations the finite step is therefore **mass-weighted ordinary Procrustes on labelled points**, and with scale it is mass-weighted full Procrustes. Barbour never says so, and a full-text search of his papers from 2003 to 2026 found no occurrence of "Procrustes", but the identification follows from his inner product ⟨a, b⟩ = Σ m_j a_j·b_j and the outcomes he states.

The dynamics uses the fit's first-order conditions. Barbour inserts the best-matched kinetic term into Jacobi's timeless action, I = 2∫√(W T_bm) dλ, so histories are geodesics on the quotient space weighted by the potential, and varying the auxiliary group variables gives **P = Σ p = 0, L = Σ x × p = 0 and, with dilatations, D = Σ x·p = 0**, conditions whose fulfilment "is precisely the indication that the best-matching position has been reached" ([Barbour 2012, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183); [Barbour 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)). These are the stationarity conditions of weighted Procrustes; molecular physicists know them as the Eckart conditions, and Kudin and Dymarsky showed that the RMSD-minimising Kabsch rotation satisfies them ([Kudin & Dymarsky 2005](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1929739)). Barbour names the split himself, calling directions inside group orbits "vertical" and the best-matched orthogonal ones "horizontal": "Readers familiar with fibre bundles will recognize this terminology." Placing each configuration on its predecessor in the best-matched position ("horizontal stacking") gives a frame in which particles "behave exactly as Newtonian particles in an inertial frame of reference with total momentum and angular momentum zero" ([Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)), and it answers Newton's problem of **equilocality**: "If a body is at these paired positions at the two instants, one can say it is at the same place." Consistency then restricts the potential to depend only on separations and, with dilatations, to be homogeneous of degree −2.

### Seven differences, of which the infinitesimal one matters most

Assembled from the definitions, since no source compares them systematically, best matching differs from statistical Procrustes in seven ways. Its weights are physical masses; its translation aligns centres of mass rather than unweighted centroids; it uses only proper motions, because the construction runs on continuous group generators; its correspondence is fixed by particle labels, so there is never a permutation search; it compares infinitesimally close configurations along a path; it handles scale either by normalising with the moment of inertia or by keeping it as a dynamical variable; and its output feeds a geodesic principle rather than a mean shape or a test ([Barbour 2003b](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0309089); [Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)). On handedness the presentations disagree. Butterfield's account of *The End of Time* defines the relative configuration space with the improper Euclidean group, "so as to identify oppositely handed polyhedra", while Barbour's technical shape sphere keeps mirror images apart in opposite hemispheres ([Butterfield 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055); [Barbour, Lostaglio & Mercati 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.6173)); in the page's terms, Butterfield's Platonia has mirrors on and Barbour's shape sphere has them off. An infinitesimal step never needs the mirror, because no small motion flips handedness. Symmetric configurations are trouble spots in both worlds: Barbour rejects principal axes as a frame because they lead "to problems if the configuration happens to be symmetric", and Mercati notes that the bundle structure degenerates at "collinear states or total collisions" ([Mercati 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)), so Barbour's "necessarily unique" holds only infinitesimally and away from symmetric shapes.

### Duration is the length of the best-matched residual

Barbour also removes time. Choosing the Jacobi parameter so that the equations take Newtonian form defines δt = √(Σ m_i δx_i·δx_i)/√(2(E − U)), which he identifies with the astronomers' ephemeris time: "Each particle 'advances time' in proportion to the square root of its mass and to its displacement" ([Barbour 2003, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)), and "The universe is its own clock" ([Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)). Read through Procrustes, **duration is the mass-weighted residual left after optimal superimposition, divided by √(E − V)**, so a purely rigid motion between snapshots contributes no time at all. The essay version won the FQXi prize in March 2009 ([Barbour 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3489)). Philosophers broadly accept intrinsic dynamics as a genuinely relational reading of Newtonian mechanics, which Pooley and Brown call "more explanatory than the conventional, substantival interpretation" ([Pooley & Brown 2002](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/53.2.183)), while contesting the timeless metaphysics; Gryb and Thébault answer that "duration is still regarded as relative, but temporal succession is taken to be absolute" ([Gryb & Thébault 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2691)).

## Removing scale turns best matching into shape dynamics and a Janus point

### The 2003 theory forbade size change; the 2014 theory made size the clock

In "Scale-invariant gravity: particle dynamics" Barbour extended best matching to dilatations as "a new and universal method for implementing scale invariance" ([Barbour 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)). The consequences are strong: the dilatational momentum D = Σ p·x must vanish, the energy and angular momentum of an "island universe" must be exactly zero, potentials must be homogeneous of degree −2, and so the moment of inertia cannot change. "There is no maximally predictive inertial dynamics on shape space." Newtonian forces are still recovered by dividing the Newton potential by √I, at the price of an extremely weak force like a cosmological constant and with G fixed by the matter distribution, and the abstract concludes that "steadily increasing inhomogeneity, not expansion of the universe, causes the Hubble red shift". To make the dilatational fit consistent, Barbour divides the kinetic metric by the centre-of-mass moment of inertia ([Barbour 2012, eq. 10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)), the mass-weighted analogue of Procrustes with unit centroid size, which is why its quotient reproduces Kendall's metric. Barbour cites Kendall here, once in all the texts checked, for the point that the total collision "Alpha is not a 'shape' and does not belong to shape space" ([Barbour 2003, §8](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)).

From 2013 the programme kept scale out of the observables but stopped forcing D = 0. Barbour, Koslowski and Mercati used the monotonic, dimensionless τ = D/D₀ as the independent variable, with log R canonically conjugate to D, and stressed that τ "has no relation to the readings of physical clocks" ([Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6264)). Sloan formalised the move as **dynamical similarity**, in which the scale-free variables evolve "as a contact system, in which the friction-like term describes evolution along the direction of similarity" ([Sloan 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04472)). The Procrustes comparison must keep these two senses of scale invariance apart: the 2003 theory is full Procrustes with scale optimised away, while the later theory works on Kendall's sphere and treats size as a separate, time-like coordinate.

### Shape dynamics trades refoliation for conformal invariance

For fields, best matching corrects the metric velocity by a Lie derivative along an auxiliary vector field and extremises over that field. In "Relativity without relativity", Barbour, Foster and Ó Murchadha showed that this gives the ADM momentum constraint, that a local square root in the action gives the Hamiltonian constraint, and that **only the Baierlein–Sharp–Wheeler action for general relativity propagates it consistently**; matter couplings are then forced onto the light cone, so "Einstein causality emerges" ([Barbour, Foster & Ó Murchadha 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089)). Adding best matching with respect to volume-preserving conformal transformations gives constant-mean-curvature (CMC) slicing and "all the equations used in York's conformal technique", which the authors read as showing that York's degrees of freedom "do not arise from a gauge fixing but from hitherto unrecognized fundamental symmetry principles" ([Anderson et al. 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0407104)). Gomes, Gryb and Koslowski then made shape dynamics a theory dual to general relativity, invariant under volume-preserving spatial conformal transformations, in which "local size is irrelevant"; a larger Linking Theory gives general relativity in CMC gauge under one gauge-fixing and shape dynamics under the other ([Gomes, Gryb & Koslowski 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.2481); [Mercati 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)). The duality breaks where CMC slicing fails: Gomes's Birkhoff theorem gives "the isotropic wormhole solution, in which no singularity is present, as opposed to maximally extended Schwarzschild" ([Gomes 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0310)), although Mercati's own collapse model found "no evidence" that the wormhole forms in a closed universe ([Mercati 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)).

### Shape complexity is the shape potential, minimal at the equilateral triangle

The N-body results tie directly to the page's sphere. For the Newtonian problem with E ≥ 0 and zero momentum and angular momentum, the Lagrange–Jacobi identity Ï = 4E − 2V_New > 0 makes D strictly monotonic, so **I is U-shaped with a unique minimum where D = 0, the Janus point**, except on a set of solutions of measure zero ([Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2014, PRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)). The scale-invariant **shape complexity** C_S = ℓ_rms/ℓ_mhl, the ratio of the root-mean-square separation to the mean harmonic separation, equals −V_New√I/m_tot^{5/2}, so up to sign and a constant it is the shape potential that drives motion on shape space. With scale acting as friction, C_S grows secularly on both sides of the Janus point, giving "one past … and two futures", and N = 1000 simulations show "clear linear growth" on either side ([PRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)). On the three-body shape sphere, complexity is minimal (the shape potential maximal) at the **equilateral triangle for all mass values**, the collinear Euler configurations are saddles, the three binary collisions on the equator are infinitely deep wells, and the equilateral triangle, "the most uniform shape", replaces the Big Bang ([Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)). The poles of the page's sphere are therefore the complexity minima of Barbour's landscape, and its equator, where the page's mirror fits tie, holds the collisions. For equal masses each isosceles great circle runs from pole to pole through one binary collision and, diametrically opposite, one Euler point ([Montgomery 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0841)).

### The theorem is accepted; its reading as time's arrow is not

Nobody disputes the Newtonian theorem; the argument is about what it explains. Carlip called it "an intriguing new viewpoint" but, asked whether it solves the arrow-of-time problem, answered "Probably not yet" ([Carlip 2014](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v7/111)). Zeh argued that it "does require a very improbable selection condition" and that a change of scale "cannot be meaningless", because phase-space volume is measured absolutely in units of Planck's constant ([Zeh 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02790)). Barbour, Koslowski and Mercati replied that the Janus point exists in all but a measure-zero set of solutions, that statistical intuitions built for confined systems fail for an unconfined universe, and that "any entropy-type quantity defined for an unconfined universe in its totality must decrease (not increase) in both directions away from the Janus point" ([Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03956)). Lazarovici and Reichert credit the relational theory with a normalisable measure under which Janus-point states are typically homogeneous, but call that measure "not entirely canonical" and the "entaxy" concept "somewhat ad hoc", and prove that entropy defined by scale-invariant macrovariables "relies on absolute scales and is thus not relational" ([Lazarovici & Reichert 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04646)). The programme continues in 2026 under a new name. Lourenço, Barbour and Lobo call the complexity function the **variety V**, and find that even slight deviations from its minimum lead to "filaments, loops, voids and other patterns reminiscent of the cosmic web" ([Lourenço, Barbour & Lobo 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11225)); a July 2026 paper concedes that the scalar does not fix morphology, since two N = 5000 central configurations whose varieties differ "by only 1.686%" have mean anisotropies differing "by 156.8%" ([Barbour, Lobo & Lourenço 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27526)); and Pure Shape Dynamics claims that Janus-point evolution through the Big Bang is generic in its inhomogeneous description, as applied to Bianchi IX ([Farokhi, Koslowski & Naranjo 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00996)). No result was found for inhomogeneous general relativity with realistic matter that yields the thermodynamic, radiative and quantum arrows.

## The shape sphere is common ground, and holonomy is what Procrustes misses

### Kendall's S²(½) and the three-body shape sphere are one object

In celestial mechanics the triangle shape sphere arrives by the same quotient. Montgomery removes translations with mass-weighted Jacobi coordinates Z ∈ C², removes rotations with the Hopf map (½(|Z₁|² − |Z₂|²), Re Z₁Z̄₂, Im Z₁Z̄₂), defines the distance between shapes as the infimum over rigid motions of the mass-metric distance (a Procrustes metric in all but name), and proves that oriented similarity classes form "the two-sphere of radius one-half", the base of the Hopf fibration S¹ → S³ → S²(½) ([Montgomery 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/dg-ga/9510005); [Montgomery 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0841)). This holds for any positive masses: collinear triangles always fill the equator, the height is proportional to the signed area, and reflection flips its sign. One caveat matters for Barbour's mass-weighted fit: **the poles are equilateral only when the masses are equal**. A derivation in this session from Montgomery's height formula puts an equilateral triangle at height √(3m₁m₂m₃M)/(m₁m₂ + m₂m₃ + m₃m₁), which reaches 1 only for equal masses; this is why Montgomery and Moeckel switch to "equal-mass" or "equilateral" coordinates that are only conformal to the mass metric ([Moeckel & Montgomery 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0972)). Kendall's unit weights are the equal-mass case, and so is the page. The mechanics literature also uses the 12 tiles the page needs. Zero-angular-momentum motions are exactly the horizontal ones (Montgomery's Lemma 1), and Chenciner and Montgomery assembled the figure-eight orbit from "twelve solution arcs each congruent to the original arc", one per tile cut out by the equator and the three isosceles circles ([Montgomery 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0841); [Chenciner & Montgomery 2000](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0011268)).

### Anderson proved the equivalence and named the difference

Edward Anderson made the connection between Barbour and Kendall. In June 2007 he showed that the relative configuration spaces of shapes are spheres in one dimension and complex projective spaces in two, built mechanics on them directly, and proved that it coincides with Barbour's best-matched theories after Routhian reduction: "Kendall's work is (a good start on) the configuration space study for Barbour's mechanics" ([Anderson 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3934)). His 2015 paper calls this the "Direct = Best-Matched Theorem", notes that the coincidence "was by no means guaranteed" because Bookstein's alternative metric would not have matched, and contains the only explicit comparison found anywhere: "Barbour's Best Matching is a procedure that bears much conceptual similarity to the Procrustes procedure of Shape Geometry; however these each involve extremizing a different object" ([Anderson 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1923)). An arXiv search for "best matching" together with "Procrustes" returned nothing relevant, and in the texts checked Barbour cites Kendall once and Procrustes never. Anderson also catalogued the discrete quotients of the triangle sphere: the full sphere, a hemisphere when mirror images are identified, a lune of width π/3 when labels are indistinguishable, and, with both identifications, "Kendall's spherical blackboard" ([Anderson 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10115)). His fractions for these regions vary between papers; the group-theoretic count of 12 tiles with angles 60°, 90° and 90° puts the blackboard at one twelfth of the sphere.

### Chained fits rotate by half the enclosed solid angle

Anderson's "different object" has a precise geometric meaning. The connection that declares zero-angular-momentum velocities horizontal, found by Guichardet, used by Iwai, rediscovered by Shapere and Wilczek and applied by Montgomery to the falling cat, is exactly Barbour's infinitesimal best-matching condition ([Montgomery 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/dg-ga/9510005); [Guichardet 1984](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPA_1984__40_3_329_0.pdf); [Shapere & Wilczek 1989](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.15986)). Gryb says the same from the physics side: the best-matching correction "is the covariant derivative of q with connection ω̇", the pull-back of a principal-bundle connection, and the corrected coordinates come from subtracting an "open–path holonomy" ([Gryb 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)). For planar triangles this is the Hopf connection ½ z dθ, and Montgomery's reconstruction formula splits a net rotation into a dynamic term ∫J/I dt and a geometric term given by the area enclosed on the shape sphere ([Montgomery 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/dg-ga/9510005)). So **a closed loop of shapes enclosing solid angle Ω on the unit sphere rotates a chain of best-matched configurations by Ω/2** in magnitude, while a one-shot Procrustes fit of the last frame to the first returns no rotation at all. The sign depends on conventions. The repository's library fixes them as Δθ = −Ω/2, with Ω the signed solid angle on its unit shape sphere, positive for a loop that runs counterclockwise seen from outside, and Δθ positive counterclockwise in the plane; on Kendall's S²(½) the magnitude is twice the enclosed area ([docs/reflections.md §6](../../reflections.md); `transport()` and `solidAngle()`, checked on 20 random loops in both directions to better than 10⁻⁴ at 1,500 steps). A session computation confirmed this by chaining exact weighted Procrustes rotations around a cap of polar radius 1.0:

| Steps around the loop | Net rotation (rad) |
|---|---|
| 12 | 1.41630 |
| 100 | 1.44379 |
| 2,000 | 1.44418 |
| Ω/2, predicted | 1.44418 |

The sign followed the loop's orientation, unequal masses gave the same result, and the one-shot fit returned about 10⁻¹⁷. In molecular language this is the difference between the Eckart frame, a one-shot Procrustes fit to a reference, and the zero-angular-momentum frame, which is parallel transport; Littlejohn and Reinsch treat the difference as gauge-field curvature ([Littlejohn & Reinsch 1997](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.69.213)), and Gomes and Gryb note that "curvature prohibits the existence of a horizontal section" ([Gomes & Gryb 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01693)). **Procrustes and best matching agree on the metric and on every single step; they disagree only around loops, and only by the holonomy.**

## General relativity needs one connection across space and another across time

### Christoffel symbols exist because coordinates are only labels

Einstein's 1916 paper demands laws "covariant for all possible transformations" and justifies the demand by observability: "All our well-substantiated space-time propositions amount to the determination of space-time coincidences", so general covariance "takes away the last remnants of physical objectivity from space and time" ([Einstein 1916, §3](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)). The tools came from a nineteenth-century question about relabelling. Christoffel's 1869 paper is titled, in translation, "On the transformation of homogeneous differential expressions of the second degree", meaning quadratic forms under a change of variables ([Christoffel 1869](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1869.70.46)); Ricci and Levi-Civita systematised the subject as the absolute differential calculus ([Ricci & Levi-Civita 1900](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01454201)); Grossmann brought it to Einstein, who introduced the three-index symbols "following Christoffel" and in §13 called the Γ's "the components of the gravitational field" ([Einstein 1916](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)). They are needed because a relabelling x → x′ varies from point to point, so the partial derivative of a vector picks up a term in ∂²x′/∂x∂x; the connection is "purposefully constructed to be non-tensorial, but in such a way that the combination (3.1) transforms as a tensor — the extra terms in the transformation of the partials and the Γ's exactly cancel" ([Carroll 1997, ch. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)). Because Γ is not a tensor, Riemann normal coordinates make it vanish at any one point, which is the equivalence principle, while curvature, the holonomy of parallel transport around a loop, cannot be removed; Levi-Civita's 1917 paper on parallelism gave curvature that geometric meaning ([Levi-Civita 1917](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03014898)). This is Barbour's pattern exactly: a compensating field that is pure gauge at a point and whose invariant content is its holonomy.

### The hole argument made the labels gauge

Einstein reached this view through a mistake. In 1913 he argued that generally covariant field equations could not be right, because relabelling the points inside a matter-free "hole" gives a second solution that agrees with the first outside it, an argument "created … as an act of desperation" ([SEP, The Hole Argument](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/)). On 3 January 1916 he wrote to Besso that "Everything in the hole argument was correct up to the final conclusion" ([Straumann 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0900)). The point-coincidence argument dissolved the problem, and the now-standard "Leibniz equivalence" holds that if two distributions of fields are related by a smooth transformation, "then they represent the same physical systems" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/); [Earman & Norton 1987](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/38.4.515)). Kretschmann showed in 1917 that any theory can be written covariantly, and Einstein conceded ([Kretschmann 1917](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19183581602)); Pooley sums up that general covariance per se has no physical content, but "GR does differ from SR in lacking a non-covariant formulation" ([Pooley 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03512)). Whether the hole argument even arises is still debated ([Weatherall 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axw012); [Halvorson & Manchak 2025](https://doi.org/10.1086/719193)). In Barbour's terms, a fixed identification of points across time is precisely such a background object, and best matching replaces it with one chosen by the dynamics without contradicting Leibniz equivalence: it picks a gauge, it does not make one physical.

### The ADM shift is the best-matching variable

Arnowitt, Deser and Misner wrote the spacetime metric as the spatial metric g_ij plus N and N_i, showed that these are Lagrange multipliers whose variation gives the constraints, and noted that a coordinate change "merely corresponds to a relabeling", so "the coordinate invariance plays a role similar to the gauge invariance of the Maxwell field" ([ADM 1962](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0405109)). The shift N^i is the lateral displacement between where the normal from x pierces the next slice and the point labelled x there ([Barbour 2012, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)). Barbour, Foster and Ó Murchadha observe that under λ-dependent relabelling the metric velocity "does not transform as a 3-tensor", replace it by k_ab = ∂g_ab/∂λ − ξ_a;b − ξ_b;a, minimise over the vector field ξ, and call the step what it is: "This process of adjusting for the gauge freedom is universal and is appropriately called best matching." Its variation gives "the standard ADM diffeomorphism constraint", a result that "has nothing to do with the square root or any other structure of the action" ([Barbour, Foster & Ó Murchadha 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089)). Gryb states the identification outright: "the Mach variation of ξ̇ is equivalent to the usual variation of the shift vector N^i" ([Gryb 2010, fn. 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)); Barbour calls the resulting equations G_0i = 0 the "local counterparts" of zero total angular momentum ([Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)). Labels behave differently in the two cases. Particles carry physical labels, so the correspondence is known and only six or seven frame parameters are fitted; the points of space carry none, so best matching must also construct the point-to-point correspondence: "Given an apple and a pear, there does not appear to be any way to establish a 1-to-1 pairing of all the points on the apple's surface with all those on the pear's. However, best matching does just that if the compared objects differ only infinitesimally" ([Barbour 2012, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)). The particle case is rigid Procrustes with known correspondence, the geometric case is closer to non-rigid registration with unknown correspondence, and the triangle page sits between them, with a finite, discrete set of correspondences to choose from.

### Large diffeomorphisms are the relabellings no connection removes

Wheeler's superspace, Riem(Σ)/Diff(Σ), metrics modulo relabelling of points, is the field-theory counterpart of Kendall's shape space. Giulini treats Riem as a principal bundle over it, with vertical directions ℒ_ξh and a horizontal subspace orthogonal to them in the DeWitt metric, and notes that superspace is stratified at metrics with Killing vectors, as shape space is at symmetric configurations ([Giulini 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)). Two of his points sharpen the analogy. The DeWitt metric of general relativity is indefinite, and "orthogonality does not imply transversality if the metric is indefinite", so the geometric best-matching equation can lose the uniqueness that the positive-definite particle problem enjoys. And since Riem is contractible, the fundamental group of superspace is the mapping class group, the diffeomorphisms not connected to the identity; the constraints see only the identity component, which leaves a θ-ambiguity like the Aharonov–Bohm effect and, for space built from identical pieces, "statistics sectors" that "correspond to the irreducible representations of the permutation subgroup". Friedman and Sorkin found that in some topologies a 2π rotation is such a large diffeomorphism, so pure quantum gravity contains half-integer spin ([Friedman & Sorkin 1980](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.44.1100)). **Large diffeomorphisms are to general relativity what the page's vertex permutations and mirror images are to triangles**: relabellings that no infinitesimal correction generates, which a theory must therefore choose among or sum over.

The table sets the three connections side by side. It is a synthesis from Carroll, Barbour, Barbour–Foster–Ó Murchadha, Gryb and Giulini; no source found presents them this way, and the superspace holonomy entry is an inference.

| | Christoffel connection (Einstein) | Best matching, particles (Barbour–Bertotti) | Best matching, geometry (BFÓ, shape dynamics) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compares | vectors at x and x + dx | configurations at λ and λ + dλ | 3-metrics at λ and λ + dλ |
| Label freedom | x → x′(x) | a Euclidean or similarity frame g(λ) | x → x′(x, λ) |
| Compensating field | Γ, transforming inhomogeneously | frame velocities (rotation, translation, dilatation) | the vector field ξ^i, i.e. the ADM shift |
| Fixed by | metric compatibility and zero torsion | minimising mass-weighted displacement | extremising the action over ξ |
| Yields | the covariant derivative ∇ | P = 0, L = 0 (and D = 0) | ∇_j p^{ij} = 0 |
| Removable | at a point (normal coordinates) | along a curve (horizontal stacking) | along a curve (zero shift) |
| Invariant remainder | Riemann curvature, holonomy of loops in space | falling-cat holonomy on shape space | holonomy on superspace |
| Discrete relabellings left over | none in the local derivative | permutations of identical particles, mirror images | large diffeomorphisms (mapping class group) |

## On the triangle page, discrete relabellings are the only source of jumps

### Twelve candidates are one orbit of the reflection group D3h

The page starts from a GeoGebra construction and corrects it in four places: the turn must use atan2, the best pairing is the one with the largest |s| rather than the smallest |θ|, the sixth pairing sum used u₁ twice, and reversing the vertex order does not mirror a triangle, since it changes which vertex goes where but not handedness, so a genuine mirror fit conjugates u and the candidates number 12 ([README](../../../README.md); [reflections.md §3](../../reflections.md)). It also keeps the current pairing until another is better by a margin (hysteresis), moves between old and new fits along the similarity that joins them, and shows a switch to a mirror fit as a half-turn in space ([reflections.md §2–3](../../reflections.md)). Because relabelling never changes Σ|u|², every candidate's error decreases with |s|, with or without scaling, and the identity |s|² = ‖z‖²‖u‖²(1 + n_ABC·n_DEF′)/2 turns the choice into geometry: **the best pairing is the image of DEF nearest to ABC on Kendall's sphere** ([reflections.md §4](../../reflections.md)). The twelve images form one orbit of a group, in which cyclic relabellings turn the sphere 120° about the poles, transpositions are half-turns about equatorial axes, and the mirror is reflection through the equator; together these form **D3h, generated by reflections in the equator and the three isosceles great circles**. For a group generated by reflections, the region nearest each orbit point is a chamber cut out by the mirrors, so the boundaries between pairings do not depend on DEF at all; the repository recorded this chamber argument on 27 October 2026 (commit bcb6c53). The chambers are 12 spherical triangles, each with a 60° corner at an equilateral pole and two right angles on the equator, the same tiles as Kendall's blackboard and the twelve arcs of the figure-eight orbit. Berge's maximum theorem explains why a jump is unavoidable: the minimum of continuous functions is continuous, but the set of minimisers is only upper hemicontinuous ([Maximum theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_theorem)), or in the page's words, "That jump is the correct answer changing" ([reflections.md §2](../../reflections.md)). The general fact behind it is that symmetric shapes are exactly where Procrustes matching has multiple solutions ([Dym & Lipman 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01548)).

### Only the target's crossing moves the fitted triangle

The chamber picture predicts an asymmetry that this session confirmed and that corrected an earlier account. The best candidate changes whenever ABC or DEF passes through an isosceles or flat shape, but **the fitted triangle jumps only when ABC crosses a wall**. When DEF is itself on a wall it equals its own relabelled mirror image, so the two tied images are the same point and both pairings place the same triangle with two vertex names swapped; folding DEF into ABC's chamber is continuous. Measured with the library's `analyse` and `placed`, the largest step of the fitted triangle was **0.002 when DEF crossed and 0.70 when ABC crossed** (session result); the repository's own test reports about 2 per frame against 0.002 ([reflections.md §4](../../reflections.md)), and the README and reflections document were corrected accordingly. An independent session scan moved ABC's apex along a horizontal line against a fixed scalene DEF. With mirrors on, the fit switched at x ≈ 0.529, 1.500 and 2.473, all three isosceles, with per-vertex jumps of 0.33, 0.12 and 0.33, and jumped 2.67 when ABC passed through a collinear shape. With mirrors off, the same scan switched at x ≈ 0.134 and 2.172, neither isosceles: the six proper relabellings form D3, which contains no reflections of the sphere, so the switching boundaries are perpendicular bisectors of DEF's six-point orbit and move with DEF. Allowing mirrors is what makes the jump locus "flat or isosceles, whatever DEF is".

| What happens | Mirrors on | Mirrors off |
|---|---|---|
| ABC passes through an isosceles or flat shape | candidate switches; fitted triangle jumps | not a switching locus in general |
| DEF passes through an isosceles or flat shape | candidate switches; only labels change (step about 0.002) | not a switching locus in general |
| ABC meets a perpendicular bisector of DEF's six-point orbit | not a switching locus | candidate switches; the locus moves with DEF |

A closed form describes the jump. For a wall with unit normal n, and x and y the unit shape vectors of ABC and of DEF's folded image, the reflection identity ⟨x, y⟩ − ⟨x, r_n y⟩ = 2⟨x, n⟩⟨y, n⟩ gives, for unit-size triangles, **Δ|s|² = ⟨x, n⟩⟨y, n⟩, the product of the two triangles' distances to the wall** (a derivation from this research, not a published result). The gap vanishes when either triangle lies on the wall, and the drawn jump grows with DEF's distance from it. A separate degeneracy afflicts single candidates: a candidate's turn is undefined when s = 0, which happens when that image of DEF is antipodal to ABC, the planar case of the degeneracies Coutsias, Seok and Dill enumerate ([Coutsias, Seok & Dill 2004](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20110)). The best candidate never has s = 0, but losing candidates can, which matters for any blend.

### Sort the sides, compare the orientations

The chamber of a labelled triangle has a plain description. Each isosceles great circle is where two labelled sides are equal, so the six lunes between the three circles are the six strict orderings of the side lengths, and the equator splits each lune by the sign of the area. Folding DEF into ABC's chamber therefore means **pairing the vertices by the rank of the opposite side length (equivalently, by the rank of the angle), and mirroring if and only if ABC and the relabelled DEF have opposite orientation**. In this session the rule matched the exhaustive 12-candidate search in **40,000 of 40,000 random trials, with and without scaling**, while with mirrors off, ranking alone matched only about 60% of trials (session result). The 60% has a simple reading: when the two triangles share an orientation, the rank-matched image already lies in ABC's chamber and is therefore also the best proper candidate, so the failures come from pairs with opposite orientation. The rule also implies an untested corollary in three dimensions. For triangles in space a proper rotation can turn the plane over, so the mirror comes free, plain rank pairing is optimal, and isosceles targets are the only walls.

### The soft fit is a posterior mean that flattens at ties

The soft fit weights every candidate by w_k ∝ exp(−(rms_k² − rms_best²)/σ²) and gives each vertex of ABC the weighted mean of its partners, so the result depends continuously on the vertices and returns to the hard fit as σ → 0. The repository credits the softassign Procrustes idea of Rangarajan, Chui and Bookstein and notes that lowering σ step by step is deterministic annealing ([reflections.md §5](../../reflections.md); [Rangarajan, Chui & Bookstein 1997](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-63046-5_3)); in its test, moving C across an isosceles shape made the hard fit leap by more than a unit while the soft fit with σ = 0.6 moved less than 0.15 per step. The weights have a Bayesian reading. With independent Gaussian vertex noise of variance τ², a uniform prior over the 12 labellings and the similarity set to its optimum, the posterior weight of candidate k is exp(−3 rms_k²/(2τ²)), the page's weight with σ² = 2τ²/3, so **the soft fit is the posterior mean and the hard fit the maximum a posteriori estimate**, the estimate Green and Mardia argue against for multimodal matching posteriors, where "there is no intrinsic attraction to the MAP estimate" ([Green & Mardia 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503712)). Integrating out the turn instead of optimising it multiplies each weight by roughly |s_k|^(−1/2), a von Mises normaliser, so the page's weights are a close approximation when σ is small. Unlike softassign, CPD and EM-ICP, which weight point pairs and then fit one rigid map ([Myronenko & Song 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2635)), the page averages twelve complete fits, so its output need not be congruent to DEF.

At an exact tie caused by a symmetry h of ABC, the 50/50 blend (P + hP)/2 is the projection onto the fixed set of h: **a collinear target yields a flat fit and an isosceles target an isosceles one**. A session run with a collinear ABC gave a blended area of 0.0000 against DEF's 3.08 for σ = 0.3, 1 and 3. Mixing proper and mirror maps produces w ↦ αw + βw̄ with determinant |α|² − |β|², which must pass through zero, the same obstruction that makes the page animate a mirror switch as a half-turn in space. The repository's verdict is the right one: "That is the picture of the ambiguity, not a better answer" ([reflections.md §5](../../reflections.md)). The literature suggests two refinements. σ is in absolute units, so normalising it by centroid size would make the blend scale-invariant; and CPD's argument that for rigid models σ should be estimated rather than annealed ([Myronenko & Song 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2635)) points to a self-tuning σ² ∝ Σ w_k rms_k². By the product formula above, the width of the transition band scales as σ² divided by DEF's distance from the wall.

## The jump-locus result is known in pieces and assembled nowhere searched

### Groth had the rule, Kendall the tiles, Coxeter theory the proof

Every ingredient is published. In star-field matching, **Groth (1986)** labels each triangle so that "the side between vertices 1 and 2 is the shortest side, the side between vertices 2 and 3 is the intermediate side, and the side between vertices 3 and 1 is the longest side", stores whether the vertices are "traversed in a clockwise or counterclockwise sense", and counts "six ways to match the vertices and two ways to match the orientations of a pair of triangles" ([Groth 1986](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1986AJ.....91.1244G)); Tabur notes that this reduces the candidates "by a factor of 6" ([Tabur 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3618)). That is the side-length rule as an algorithm, but Groth matches triangles within a tolerance in an invariant space of side ratios and angle cosines and makes no least-squares claim. Pál and Bakos observe that a side-ordered chiral triangle space "is not continuous", since "a small perturbation of an isosceles triangle can result in a new coordinate" ([Pál & Bakos 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0609658)), which is the wall seen as a hashing nuisance rather than as the place where a fit jumps. Kendall's blackboard supplies the 12-tile geometry; a tutorial extract describes the dividing planes as separating "clockwise and anticlockwise labelled hemispheres" and "left- and right-leaning hemispheres of scalene triangles" ([Behrend](https://cantab.net/users/michael.behrend/ley_stats/spher_black/pages/spher_black.html), extract only). In a different metric, Bowden and co-authors identify a tile whose boundary "is precisely the set of isosceles and degenerate triangles" and which "parametrizes triangles with side lengths written in ascending order", but they cut the sphere into 48 tiles under the group B₃ and use the tile to find the least symmetric triangle, not to match two ([Bowden et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01559)).

The mechanism is textbook: "the maximum … of the scalar product (wλ, µ) when w runs through the Weyl group W is achieved when wλ and µ lie in the same Weyl chamber" ([Kuznetsov & Polishchuk, Cor. 2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5607)), and coding theory decodes reflection-group codes by folding the received vector into the fundamental chamber ([Kim, Nation & Shepler 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.7038); [Eaton & Perlman 1977](https://www.projecteuclid.org/euclid.aop/1176995655)). D3h is the Weyl group W(A₂) × W(A₁), so in coding terms the page decodes a 12-word reflection-group code generated from DEF, with ABC as the received vector. Chemistry solves the same kind of minimum over relabellings by enumeration, Hungarian assignment or graph isomorphism, and in those terms the page computes a symmetry-corrected RMSD for a ring of three identical atoms with optional reflection ([Meli & Biggin 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7457508/)). For general n, the October 2026 Rubix paper solves planar unlabelled Procrustes globally with the same complex correlations and proves a sharp bound of n(n − 1) vertices on the "permutation polygon", six for triangles, matching the page's six pairings ([Bhattacharjee, Campbell & Shome 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10408)).

What no readable source states is the assembled result for triangle Procrustes: that rank plus orientation is the exact least-squares optimum among the 12 candidates for arbitrary, dissimilar triangles, with or without scaling; that the hard fit jumps only when the target crosses an isosceles or collinear shape while crossings of the source only relabel; and that both fail without mirrors because D3 is not a reflection group. The verdict is **known in pieces**. Each claim follows in a few lines from published ingredients, so it is best presented as an observation connecting Kendall's blackboard, Groth's labelling and reflection-group decoding ("we note that this classical labelling is in fact least-squares optimal") rather than as a discovery. The caveat is real: Kendall 1985, Kendall, Barden, Carne and Le (1999), Small (1996) and Mardia, Edwards and Puri (1977) could not be read, the quotient-metric form of the result plausibly appears there, and a library check is needed before any claim to priority. The parts most defensible as new are the asymmetry between target and source, the explicit failure with mirrors off, and the product formula for the gap across a wall.

### Its value is a closed form, a diagnostic and a certificate

The saving in computation is negligible, since twelve candidates are cheap; the value lies elsewhere. The rule gives a branch-light closed form for unlabelled triangle Procrustes (sort the sides, compare signed areas, then one complex division) that agrees with enumeration. It turns every visible jump into a diagnosis, since a jump means the target crossed a wall and its size measures the source's distance from that wall. It upgrades Groth-style labelling in astrometry from a convenient invariant to the residual-optimal correspondence under any noise, and explains why near-isosceles and near-flat triangles are where such labelling is fragile. It supplies canonical blackboard coordinates for statistics on unlabelled triangles, with the caution that folding a data cloud that straddles a wall biases means computed in the folded chart. And it connects a classroom construction to the geometry Barbour, Montgomery and Anderson use, since the walls of the page's chambers are the same isosceles meridians and collinear equator on which the collisions, Euler points and complexity minima sit.

### Open questions start where the reflection group ends

The result depends on D3h being a reflection group, and each generalisation tests that. For **four or more points** the planar shape space is CP^{n−2}, and each relabel-and-mirror involution fixes a real form of real dimension n − 2, which has codimension n − 2 and separates the space only when n = 3; there are then no chambers, and no sorting rule of this kind can hold in general (an inference from this research). Rubix's permutation polygon is the replacement, and collinear configurations, for which the rearrangement inequality gives sort-and-match with an optional flip, are a family that keeps the structure. **Unequal masses**, Barbour's case, break the S₃ isometry. With m₁ = m₂ ≠ m₃ the surviving group {id, (12)} × {proper, mirror} is still generated by reflections, and the rule should become "swap 1 and 2 by comparing r₁₃ with r₂₃, and mirror by orientation"; with all masses distinct only the mirror survives; and whether the remaining isosceles locus stays a great circle in mass-weighted coordinates was not checked. **Weighted or anisotropic** Procrustes raises the same question. For soft fits, the wall-gap identity would give the posterior over the twelve labellings in closed form, and whether it matches Green–Mardia posteriors for n = 3 is open. For three-dimensional configurations of four or more points, no closed-form rule is known. The Barbour connection also suggests a feature the page lacks: a chained mode that best-matches each frame to the previous one while a vertex is dragged around a loop, which would display the Ω/2 holonomy that one-shot Procrustes cannot show, and which would itself have to decide what to do when a chained step lands near a wall.

## Conclusion

The thread through all of this is the difference between continuous and discrete relabellings. Continuous ones (rotations, translations, scale, small diffeomorphisms) are absorbed by connections, the Christoffel symbols across space and best matching, or the shift, across time; the compensating field can be zeroed at a point or along a curve, and what survives is holonomy, as curvature in spacetime or as the falling cat in shape space. Discrete ones (vertex permutations, mirror images, large diffeomorphisms, exchanges of identical particles) cannot be generated infinitesimally, so a theory must choose among them (the hard fit, a gauge choice), sum over them (the soft fit, statistics sectors) or quotient them out (Kendall's blackboard). Barbour never meets the page's jumps because his particles carry labels and his steps are infinitesimal; general relativity meets them as large diffeomorphisms; the triangle page meets them in the smallest example where they can be seen whole. For three points with mirrors allowed, the discrete group happens to be generated by reflections, and that rare structure is what turns the choice into a fold with a sorting rule and lets only the target move the picture. Four or more points, unequal masses and weighted fits all destroy it, and there the jump locus will again depend on both configurations.

The page shows one half of the subject well: the finite fit and its discrete ambiguity, with the soft fit as an honest picture of that ambiguity rather than a cure. The other half, best matching proper, would be a chained mode that displays the holonomy on which Procrustes and best matching disagree. The sensible next steps are a library check of Kendall 1985, Kendall et al. 1999 and Small 1996 before claiming priority, adding the side-length rule to the library as a tested closed form, making the soft fit's σ size-normalised or self-tuning, and trying unequal masses, which are where Procrustes and Barbour's mechanics actually part ways and where the reflection-group structure begins to fail.

Since this report was written, the library has taken up three of these points (commits ff5249d and bb55886). The side-length rule is `trianglePairing()` in js/triangle.js, matching the exhaustive search in 80,000 of 80,000 trials, with the chamber proof in [docs/reflections.md §4](../../reflections.md). The chained mode is `transport()` with `solidAngle()` and the page's section "Carried round a loop" (§6). And `fit(..., { pairings: 'best' })` with `polygonPairings()` follows the Rubix permutation polygon for any number of points: it matched exhaustive search in 2,120 cases with n = 2 to 8, including symmetric polygons, with never more than n(n − 1) vertices ([Bhattacharjee, Campbell & Shome 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10408)).
