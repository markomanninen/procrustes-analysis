# Julian Barbour's best matching and relational particle mechanics: origins to the 2003 scale-invariant theory, and its relation to Procrustes superimposition

Scope note: these notes cover Barbour's particle-mechanics programme (1974 to 2003), its mathematics, its popular presentations and its critics. They also record whether Barbour connects to Procrustes or Kendall, and how best matching differs from statistical Procrustes. Shape dynamics for general relativity, the Janus-point cosmology and complexity get only brief pointers, because another researcher covers them.

Method: full texts were downloaded from arXiv and searched with `pdftotext`/`grep` (Barbour 2003, 2003b, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2024 and four 2026 papers; Mercati 2014; Butterfield 2001; Anderson 2007, 2008, 2010, 2013; Barbour–Lostaglio–Mercati 2013; Barbour–Koslowski–Mercati 2013; Sloan 2018). Bibliographic records came from the arXiv API and Crossref. Equation numbers below are those of the arXiv versions read (for gr-qc/0211021 this is v2, 2 May 2003).

## 1. Origins and timeline: Barbour 1974, Barbour & Bertotti 1977 and 1982; the roles of Mach and Poincaré

### Takeaway
Best matching was introduced by Barbour and Bertotti in 1982 (Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A 382, 295–306) under the name "intrinsic derivative", within a theory they called "intrinsic dynamics". Barbour renamed it "best matching" in 1994 (CQG 11, 2853, p. 2862) and extended it to dilatations in 2003. Two earlier works came first: Barbour 1974 (Nature 249, 328–329) and Barbour & Bertotti 1977 (Nuovo Cimento B 38, 1–27). These were "relative-distance" Machian theories. Barbour later groups this kind of theory with Schrödinger's 1925 approach, which predicts an anisotropic effective mass and is ruled out by experiment. Mach supplied the motivation: motion and time should be relational. Poincaré's *Science and Hypothesis* (1902; English 1905) supplied the precise criterion that best matching was built to satisfy: relational initial data (r_ij and their rates of change) should fix the evolution.

### Inferences
- The lineage runs in three steps. The relative-distance theories of 1974 and 1977 built kinetic terms directly from r_ij, which risks mass anisotropy. Best matching in 1982 keeps the isotropic Euclidean kinetic metric and quotients the redundancy variationally. In 2003 the same quotienting was extended to scale. The 1982 name ("intrinsic derivative/difference") stresses the derivative, that is, the connection on configuration space. The 1994 name ("best matching") stresses the finite picture of fitting one configuration onto another.
- Poincaré, not Mach, supplies the operational criterion: count the initial data needed in the quotient space. Mach supplies the motivation and the "whole universe" escape route that Poincaré did not take.

## 2. The mathematics of best matching: definition, Jacobi-type action, constraints, horizontal stacking, geodesics on shape space

### Takeaway
Take two neighbouring configurations. Hold one fixed and move the other along its group orbit (translations, rotations and, from 2003, dilatations) until the mass-weighted squared displacement Σ m_i |δx_i|² is minimal. This minimum is the "best-matched" distance. It defines a metric on the quotient (relative configuration space or shape space). The minimizing displacement is orthogonal ("horizontal") to the group orbits. Barbour inserts this best-matched kinetic term into Jacobi's timeless action I = 2∫√((E−V)T) dλ, or with a general potential factor W, I = 2∫√(W T_bm) dλ. Physical histories are geodesics of the resulting metric on the quotient space. Variation over the auxiliary group variables gives linear constraints: P = Σp_i = 0, L (or J) = Σ x_i × p_i = 0 and, with dilatations, D = Σ x_i·p_i = 0. Consistency forces the potential to be translation- and rotation-invariant and, with dilatations, homogeneous of degree −2.

### Cited Findings
**Barbour's verbal definition (2010, finite-difference form, with masses)**
- "Represent once and for all the first configuration by a vector X1 in some Cartesian frame of reference. Represent the second configuration in the same frame with its centre of mass in any arbitrary position, with any orientation and with any scale. This will give the vector X2. Choose an arbitrary 'time difference' δt and call V = (X2 − X1)/δt the velocity of the system. Then for all possible positions of the centre of mass, orientation and scale of the second configuration, calculate the scalar product ⟨V, V⟩ (note that this brings in a weighting with the masses) and find the necessarily unique position, orientation and scale for which this positive-definite quantity is minimized. It is readily seen that this 'best-matching' procedure, which reduces the 'incongruence' of the two figures to a minimum, brings the centres of mass to coincidence and ensures that for the extremalized quantities p_a = m_a δx_a/δt the relations (8) and (7) hold." — [Barbour 2010, §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- The inner product is eq. (5): ⟨a, b⟩ = Σ_j m_j a_j·b_j, with 2I = ⟨R, R⟩ and 2T = ⟨V, V⟩. The velocity splits uniquely and orthogonally as V = V_r + V_d + V_s (rotation, dilatation, shape; eq. 6, citing Saari). The pure-shape momenta satisfy Σ x_a × p_as = 0 (7) and Σ x_a·p_as = 0 (8). — [Barbour 2010, §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- Multiplying ⟨V,V⟩ by any function of the configuration (for example the potential) leaves the best-matched position unchanged. "Different choices of the measure will be significant in dynamics, but at any instant they merely change the magnitude of the intrinsic velocities, not their directions … the extremal V_s is a tangent vector on shape space." — [Barbour 2010, §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)

**Barbour's definition (2011, "Shape Dynamics. An Introduction"; equation numbers from arXiv:1105.0183)**
- Hold shape s1 fixed and move s2 around its orbit using translations and rotations. This changes dx_a = x2_a − x1_a and the trial distance (eq. 8): ds_trial := √((E − V) Σ_a (m_a/2) dx_a·dx_a). "Since (8) is positive definite … it will be possible to move shape s2 into the unique position in its orbit at which (8) is minimized … 1) Shape s2 has been moved to the position in which it most closely 'covers' s1, i.e., the two shapes, which are incongruent, have been brought as close as possible to congruence … This is the best-matched position. 2) The 3N-dimensional vector joining s1 and s2 in their orbits in Q^N is orthogonal to the orbits." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- The best-matched distance is eq. (9): ds_bm := min of √((E−V) Σ (m_a/2) dx_a·dx_a) between orbits. Fig. 4 shows "a) An arbitrary placing of the dashed triangle relative to the undashed triangle; b) the best-matched placing reached by translational and rotational minimization of (8); c) … The connecting 'strut' is orthogonal with respect to the supermetric … Best matching brings the centres of mass to coincidence and reduces the net rotation to zero." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.2, Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Equivariance: "the orthogonal separation (9) is the same at all points within the orbits … because the metric (8) on Q^N is equivariant … the translation and rotation group orbits are Killing vectors of the kinetic metric." Equivariance "is already lost if we attempt to include dilational best matching with respect to the kinetic metric." The fix is to "divide the kinetic metric by the square root of I_cms, the centre-of-mass moment of inertia" (eq. 10): ds_ssbm := min of √(I_cms⁻¹ Σ_a m_a dx_a·dx_a) between orbits. This quantity "is dimensionless and defines a metric on Q^N_ss". — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Terminology: "directions in Q^N that lie entirely in group orbits [are called] vertical and the best-matched orthogonal directions horizontal. Readers familiar with fibre bundles will recognize this terminology." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- The action, eq. (11): δI_bm = 0, I_bm = 2∫dλ √(W T_bm), T_bm = ½ Σ_a m_a (dx_a^bm/dλ)·(dx_a^bm/dλ). Construction of the horizontal curve: "We hold the initial point of segment 1–2 fixed and move the other end into the horizontal best-matched position on orbit 2. We then move the original 2–3 segment into the horizontal … all the way to the s_b orbit … we obtain an M-parameter family of horizontal best-matched curves [M = 7 for the similarity group] that all yield a common unique value for the action." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.3, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Constraints, eqs. (12)–(14): P = Σ m_a ẋ_a = 0; L = Σ m_a x_a × ẋ_a = 0; D = İ = 2Σ m_a x_a·ẋ_a = 0. "It is also intuitively obvious that the fulfilment of these conditions is precisely the indication that the best-matching position has been reached." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Horizontal stacking, from the Fig. 6 caption: "For the initial shape, one chooses an arbitrary position in Euclidean space. Each successive shape is placed on its predecessor in the best-matched position ('horizontal stacking'). The 'vertical' separation is chosen in accordance with distinguished curve parameter t … In the framework thus created, the particles behave exactly as Newtonian particles in an inertial frame of reference with total momentum and angular momentum zero." — [Barbour 2011/2012, Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Equilocality: "Best matching is the relational alternative to absolute space. For when one configuration has been placed relative to another in the best-matched position, every position in one configuration is uniquely paired with a position in the other. If a body is at these paired positions at the two instants, one can say it is at the same place. The two positions are equilocal." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Consistency conditions on the potential: "If (12) is to propagate, W must be a function of the coordinate differences x_a − x_b; if (13) is to propagate, W can depend on only the inter-particle separations r_ab … Propagation of (14) … requires W to be homogeneous with length dimension l⁻²." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)

**Formal machinery (2003, "Scale-invariant gravity: particle dynamics"; arXiv:gr-qc/0211021v2)**
- The Jacobi action, eq. (8): I_Jacobi = 2∫√(E − U) √T̃ dλ, with T̃ = Σ (m_i/2)(dx_i/dλ)·(dx_i/dλ). It is reparametrization invariant (eq. 9: λ → f(λ)). The timeless form is eq. (10): I = 2∫√(E−U)√T*, T* = Σ (m_i/2) dx_i·dx_i. The momenta (eq. 11) are homogeneous of degree zero in the velocities and satisfy the quadratic constraint Σ p_i·p_i/2m_i = E − U (eq. 12). — [Barbour 2003, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- The thin-sandwich (TS) formulation: "to first order we shall be minimizing I_TS = √(f_ij δq^i δq^j) (16) … stationary to first order under q^i(λ) → q^i(λ) + b(λ)e^i (17) for all choices of b(λ) … The thin-sandwich problem requires us to minimize (16) to first order w.r.t. b(0) and b′(0) … Provided the TSP has a unique solution, its limit as δλ → 0 will clearly define a metric … This process gets its name because the 'distance' is obtained when the configurations are best matched: brought as near to congruence, as measured by (16), as possible." — [Barbour 2003, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Auxiliary gauge variable a ∈ Γ (d = 7 for the full similarity group): the corrected coordinate is q̄^i = q^i + a e^i (eq. 19). It is invariant under the "banal transformation" q → q + b e, a → a − b (eq. 20), and the corrected velocity is v̄ = v + a′e (eq. 22). For dilatations: q → b q, a → a/b (eq. 23); q̄ = a q, v̄ = a v + q a′ (eq. 24). The general best-matched action is eq. (25): I_BM = ∫dλ 2√(B(q̄) T), T = ½ Σ m_i (v̄_i)². "Free-end-point" variation of a gives p_a = 0, so the primary constraints (27) become P_t = 0, D = 0 (eq. 28). — [Barbour 2003, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Footnote 4 on uniqueness of the thin-sandwich solution: "It will in the particle model, since positive-definite quantities are being minimized." Footnote 5: "The auxiliary space Γ is a fibre over the base Q, but the formalism of fibre bundles is not needed for translations and dilatations. It is for rotations … For a beautiful gauge fibre-bundle treatment of rotations in the N-body problem, see [Littlejohn & Reinsch 1997]." — [Barbour 2003, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021); [Littlejohn & Reinsch 1997, RMP 69, 213–276](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.69.213)
- The geometric reading: "(1)–(3) and the associated conditions on U and E are as purely geometrical in origin as the operators grad, curl and div of vector analysis, to which they exactly correspond." — [Barbour 2003, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Another version of the same action (Barbour 2003b) uses a conformal factor F and an explicit correction velocity. Eq. (10) is F Σ m_i dx_i·dx_i, and "the idea of best matching is to seek the minimum of (10) with respect to all possible placings and to declare this to be the metric distance between the configurations". Eq. (11) is I_BM = 2∫dλ √F √T, T = ½ Σ m_i (dx_i/dλ − c_i)·(dx_i/dλ − c_i), "where c_i … is the correction that arises from λ-dependent transformations … generated by translations, rotations and dilatations." — [Barbour 2003b, "Dynamics of pure shape, relativity and the problem of time", §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0309089)

### Inferences
- For translations and rotations, the finite best-matching step in Barbour 2010 is mathematically a mass-weighted ordinary Procrustes superimposition of labelled landmarks. With scale included, it is a mass-weighted full Procrustes fit. The optimal translation aligns the centres of mass, and the optimal rotation comes from the mass-weighted cross-covariance (the Kabsch/SVD solution). This is my identification. Barbour does not state it, but it follows directly from his eq. (5) inner product and his stated outcomes: centres of mass coincide, net rotation is zero. The first-order optimality conditions of that weighted Procrustes problem are exactly Barbour's (7) and (8) (zero mass-weighted "angular momentum" and "dilatational momentum" of the residual). In molecular physics these are the Eckart conditions. [Eckart 1935, Phys. Rev. 47, 552–558](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.47.552) is the classic source; the Eckart–RMSD equivalence was not verified here.
- The dynamical use of best matching is infinitesimal, not finite. The Procrustes step defines a connection (a horizontal/vertical split) and a quotient metric, and dynamics is a geodesic of that metric weighted by the potential. Because the horizontal lift is built segment by segment, a closed loop in shape space generally lifts to an open curve: there is a net rotation, the "falling cat" holonomy studied in the gauge-theory treatment of Littlejohn & Reinsch that Barbour cites. A pairwise Procrustes fit of the first and last frames is path-independent and does not reproduce this. This follows from the cited constructions; Barbour does not state it in the texts read.
- With dilatations included, Barbour's normalized metric (eq. 10, dividing by I_cms) is a mass-weighted analogue of Procrustes with size normalized to unit centroid size. That is why its quotient reproduces Kendall's shape-space metric (see section 6).

### Gaps
- BB82 itself (paywalled) was not read, so the original "intrinsic derivative" equations and their numbering are not quoted. The verified material is the 1982 abstract plus later re-statements (2003, 2010, 2011; Mercati; Butterfield).
- Barbour 1994 (CQG 11, 2853), where the name "best matching" first appears (p. 2862 per Barbour 2024), is paywalled and was not read.

## 3. Ephemeris time: duration "abstracted from change"

### Takeaway
In Barbour's scheme duration is not fundamental. Jacobi's action has no time. Choosing the parameter λ so that the equations take Newtonian form defines an emergent time whose increment is δt = √(Σ m_i δx_i·δx_i) / √(2(E − V)), with δx_i the best-matched displacements. Barbour identifies this with the astronomers' ephemeris time (Clemence 1957). Every particle "advances time" in proportion to √m_i times its displacement, and the universe is its own clock. In the 2009 FQXi essay the same formula appears (eq. 3) without explicit best matching. The 2003 and 2011 papers make the best-matched displacements explicit.

### Cited Findings
- 2003, eqs. (14)–(15): choosing λ so that T̃/(E−U) = 1 recovers Newton's second law. "However, (14), which is usually taken to express energy conservation, becomes the definition of time. Indeed, this emergent time, chosen to make the equations of motion take their simplest form [Poincaré 1898], is the astronomers' operational ephemeris time [Clemence 1957]. It is helpful to see how 'change creates time'. The increment δt generated by displacements δx_i is δt = √(Σ m_i δx_i·δx_i)/√(2(E − U)) ≡ δs/√(2(E−U)). Each particle 'advances time' in proportion to the square root of its mass and to its displacement … In a scale-invariant theory, E = 0, and … a given kinetic δs 'advances time' by an amount inversely proportional to |U|." — [Barbour 2003, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- 2011, eq. (7): δt = √(Σ_a m_a δx_a·δx_a)/√(2(E − V)). "This is the first example of the holism of relational dynamics: the time that we take to flow locally everywhere is a distillation of all the changes everywhere in the universe … The universe is its own clock. The definition of duration through (7) is unique (up to origin and unit) if clocks are to have any utility." With best matching, "There is also a distinguished curve parametrization (Sec. 3.1), uniquely fixed up to its origin and unit." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.1, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- The FQXi essay "The Nature of Time" (arXiv:0903.3489, March 2009). Its header reads: "This article won the first prize awarded on March 7th 2009 by the jury in the essay competition of the Foundational Questions Institute (fqxi.org) on The Nature of Time." Abstract: "A review of some basic facts of classical dynamics shows that time, or precisely duration, is redundant as a fundamental concept. Duration and the behaviour of clocks emerge from a timeless law that governs change." — [Barbour 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3489)
- In the essay, eq. (1) is V = −G Σ_{i<j} m_i m_j/r_ij and eq. (2) is Σ (m_i/2)(δd_i/δt)² + V = E. Astronomers "effectively … rearrange (2) and use it to define the increment δt of time between successive configurations", giving eq. (3): δt = √(Σ m_i (δd_i)²/(2(E − V))). "The time derived from (3) is called ephemeris time [Clemence 1957] … a chronometer: 'it is a mechanism for measuring time that is continually synchronized as nearly as may be with ephemeris time'." — [Barbour 2009, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3489)
- Also from the essay: "Speed of body i is not the ratio of its displacement to an abstract time increment but to (3), which involves the displacements of all the bodies in the system." "Time has no role to play as an independent element of reality. It can be abstracted from change. However, Mach could have been more specific – the definition of time must be based on an accurate description of the way naturally occurring motions are correlated." He quotes Mach (1883): "It is utterly beyond our power to measure the changes of things by time ... time is an abstraction at which we arrive by means of the changes of things." — [Barbour 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3489)
- A text search of the 2009 essay finds no occurrence of "best matching". The essay's δd_i are "the distance body i moves" in "snapshots" of the solar system. — [Barbour 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3489)
- Barbour 2010 separates the two Machian requirements: "Best matching resolves the problem of defining change in the presence of gauge redundancy … but not the representation of time (the second Mach's principle). For the stronger form of Mach's principle, an external time is eliminated by defining a geodesic principle on the considered relative configuration space." — [Barbour 2010, §9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- Exact record: Barbour, J. (2010). "The Definition of Mach's Principle". *Foundations of Physics* 40, 1263–1284. DOI 10.1007/s10701-010-9490-7. arXiv:1007.3368 — [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- Butterfield's account: choosing λ so that T = E − V "'vertically stacks' the configurations", which complements horizontal stacking. "This choice of λ, which recovers the Newtonian time, is made once-for-all for the entire system (the universe)." — [Butterfield 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055)
- Barbour cites Poincaré's "La mesure du temps", *Rev. Métaphys. Morale* 6, 1 (1898; English "The Measure of Time" in *The Value of Science*, 1913), and Clemence G M, "Astronomical time", *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 29, 2 (1957). — [Barbour 2009 refs](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3489); [Barbour 2003 refs 27–28](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Critical counterpoint: Gryb & Thébault argue that "duration is still regarded as relative, but temporal succession is taken to be absolute", so that "time, in a substantive sense, remains". — [Gryb & Thébault 2016, BJPS 67, 663–705](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2691). Gryb 2010 (PRD) studies how Jacobi's principle yields "the emergence of duration at the classical level" and timelessness after quantization. — [Gryb 2010, PRD 81, 044035](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2900)

### Inferences
- In best-matching terms, ephemeris time is the potential-weighted length of the best-matched (Procrustes-residual) displacement: δt ∝ (mass-weighted RMS residual after optimal superimposition) / √(E−V). A purely rigid motion between snapshots, whose Procrustes residual is zero, therefore contributes no duration. Barbour's horizontal + vertical stacking is "Procrustes alignment for space" plus "ephemeris weighting for time".

### Gaps
- Barbour's definition of the 2009 essay's δd_i relative to a best-matched frame was not checked. The essay's solar-system illustration implicitly uses an inertial/stellar frame.

## 5. Scale invariance (2003): dilatational best matching, its consequences, and the later move to dynamical similarity with D as a clock

### Takeaway
"Scale-invariant gravity: particle dynamics" (CQG 20 (2003) 1543–1570; gr-qc/0211021) extends best matching to dilatations. The consequences are D = Σ x_i·p_i = 0, E = 0, J = 0 (and P = 0), and potentials homogeneous of degree −2. By the Lagrange–Jacobi relation, the moment of inertia I is then constant. Newtonian gravity is recovered by dividing the Newton potential by √I, which also produces a weak cosmological-constant-like force, and G is fixed by the matter distribution. From about 2011–2013 Barbour and collaborators relaxed D = 0 (the "weak" Mach–Poincaré principle). They used the monotonic D, or τ = D/D₀, as a dimensionless independent variable, justified by "dynamical similarity". Sloan (2018) and Gryb & Sloan (2021) generalized this.

### Cited Findings
- 2003 abstract: "A new and universal method for implementing scale invariance, called best matching, is presented … It leads to far stronger predictions than general Newtonian dynamics. The energy and angular momentum of an 'island universe' must be exactly zero and its size, measured by its moment of inertia, cannot change. This constancy is enforced because the scale invariance requires all potentials to be homogeneous of degree −2. It is remarkable that one can nevertheless exactly recover the standard observed Newtonian laws and forces, which are merely accompanied by an extremely weak universal force like the one due to Einstein's cosmological constant … both the gravitational constant G and the strength of the cosmological force are uniquely determined by the matter distribution of the universe … Best matching implements a dynamics of pure shape for which the action is a dimensionless number. If the universe obeys such scale-invariant law, steadily increasing inhomogeneity, not expansion of the universe, causes the Hubble red shift." — [Barbour 2003, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021); record: CQG 20 (2003) 1543–1570, DOI 10.1088/0264-9381/20/8/310 — [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Constraints, eqs. (1)–(3): P = Σ p_i = 0 (1). Rotational best matching gives "the non-trivial vanishing of the total angular momentum J: J = Σ x_i × p_i = 0 (2) and in conjunction with translational best matching enforces U = U(r_ij)". Then: "The extension of best matching to dilatations is new … It concerns the scalar quantity D = Σ p_i·x_i, which has not, so far as I know, been given a name … I shall call it the dilatational momentum. Dilatational best matching forces it to vanish: D = Σ p_i·x_i = 0 (3). In addition, U must be homogeneous of degree −2 in r_ij, and E must be zero. Thus pure inertial motion … is not allowed. It is in this sense that inertia violates scaling. There is no maximally predictive inertial dynamics on shape space." — [Barbour 2003, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Shape space: Q_RCS has dimension 3N − 6 (quotient by translations and rotations), and "quotienting by the dilatations, we arrive at the (3N − 7)-dimensional space Q0 of shapes of N-particle configurations: shape space." — [Barbour 2003, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Lagrange–Jacobi relation, §2: Ï = 2Σ m_i ẋ_i·ẋ_i + 2Σ m_i x_i·ẍ_i. For U homogeneous of degree k, Ï = 4(E − U) − 2kU (eq. 5). For k = −2, Ï = 4E (eq. 6). "Suppose E = 0. Then Ï = 0 … so that İ = 2Σ m_i ẋ_i·x_i = 2Σ p_i·x_i = constant. Thus, the dilatational momentum D = Σ p_i·x_i is conserved if both U is homogeneous of degree −2 and E = 0 … Obtained thus, conservation of D is a fluke. However, … dilatational best matching" enforces it. For k = −1 (Newtonian gravity), Ï = 4E − 2U, so E ≥ 0 implies Ï > 0 (Lagrange's instability result). — [Barbour 2003, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- How Newtonian forces are recovered: "It is in fact possible to recover Newtonian gravitational and electrostatic forces exactly from l⁻² potentials by dividing the l⁻¹ Newtonian potentials by the square root of the moment of inertia I_cms. This is because I_cms is dynamically conserved and is effectively absorbed into the gravitational constant G … However, the presence of I_cms in the action leads to an additional force that has the form of a time-dependent 'cosmological constant' and ensures that I_cms remains constant." — [Barbour 2011/2012, fn. 10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Scope: "The constraints and the condition E = 0 apply to the complete 'island universe' of N particles … and need not apply to subsystems like the solar system – only the sums over all subsystems need satisfy the constraints." — [Barbour 2003, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021); similarly [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Big Bang: Barbour 2003 cites Kendall 1984 for the point that the total-collision point "Alpha is not a 'shape' and does not belong to shape space Q0." In Q0 the distinguished configuration "Alpha0 … is the most uniform state, at which the (negative) scale-invariant potential has an absolute maximum. In the 3- and 4-body problems, this is at the equilateral triangle and quadrilateral … There cannot be anything like the Big-Bang singularity." — [Barbour 2003, §8 and ref. 37](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- Strong and weak forms: "different E and g_i values lead to different curves in shape space, but not to any differences that can be expressed through an initial point and direction … If the strong Poincaré principle holds, this unpredictability is eliminated. There may still be several different terms in V but they must all have the same homogeneity degree −2 … the constant E cannot be present … Nature may have reasons not to best match with respect to all conceivable symmetries." The weak principle (point plus tangent vector) is kept open because "Vectors are amenable to mathematical manipulation in a way that directions are not." — [Barbour 2011/2012, §3.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183); [Barbour 2010, §8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- D as the clock (Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati, CQG 31 (2014) 155001): "if in any dynamical system … E ≥ 0, then the function I_cm(t) is concave upward and D monotonic … We now introduce the monotonic and dimensionless independent variable τ = D/D₀ and obtain an unconstrained (true) Hamiltonian. The log of R is canonically conjugate to D, {D, log R} = 1. Therefore H = −log(R/R₀) + const can be used as the generator of τ-translations." Also: "We say 'independent variable' rather than 'time' because … τ has no relation to the readings of physical clocks", and "any definite monotonic function of D/D₀ can also be chosen as time." — [Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2014, §4 and fns. 4, 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6264)
- Dynamical similarity: in the GR case, "the LY equation … like the N-body equations, exhibits a form of dynamical similarity" (eqs. 23–24), "first recognized by Ó Murchadha … and led to our idea of introducing a dimensionless independent variable." — [Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2014, §5 and fn. 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6264)
- Sloan formalizes "dynamical similarities … symmetries of an intrinsically determined physical system under which observables remain unaffected." The invariants evolve "as a contact system, in which the friction-like term describes evolution along the direction of similarity". The paper gives a "natural extension of the results of Barbour et al to generic potentials". — [Sloan 2018, PRD 97, 123541](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04472)
- The gravitational arrow of time (Barbour, Koslowski & Mercati 2014, PRL 113, 181101) and the Janus point are not detailed here. — [arXiv 1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)

### Inferences
- There are two senses of "scale invariance" in Barbour, and the comparison with Procrustes should keep them apart. (i) 2003, strong: best matching in scale, so D = 0, I constant, E = 0 and degree −2 potentials. Procrustes analogue: full Procrustes with optimal scaling or unit-size normalization. (ii) 2013 onward: scale is not best-matched away. Its change D becomes the independent variable, and only the shape-space trajectory is physical. Procrustes analogue: work on Kendall's pre-shape/shape sphere, with size treated as a separate time-like coordinate.

### Gaps
- When the D = 0 requirement was formally abandoned (Barbour 2011 already keeps the weak form open; BKM 2013 uses D as the independent variable) is documented here only from these papers. *The Janus Point*'s presentation was not checked.
- Gryb & Sloan, "When scale is surplus", *Synthese* (2021), DOI 10.1007/s11229-021-03443-7, was seen only in search results. Volume and pages are not verified.

### Inferences
- Anderson's caveat that the two procedures "involve extremizing a different object" is accurate at the level of the action. Procrustes minimizes Σ m_i |x_i − g y_i|² for two finite configurations. Barbour minimizes the infinitesimal kinetic quadratic form (the alignment step), then extremizes the potential-weighted length of the whole path in shape space (the dynamical step). At the alignment step, for translations and rotations, the two are the same weighted least-squares problem. Barbour 2010 shows the best-matched position does not depend on the conformal factor (E−V). The local, infinitesimal Procrustes distance therefore induces the very metric that Anderson identifies with Kendall's. This is consistent with Kendall 1984's title pairing "shape manifolds" with "Procrustean metrics".
- Barbour knew Kendall's shape-space work by 2002–2003 (the citation, and Montgomery's input on shape spheres), but there is no evidence that he connected best matching to Procrustes. The Procrustes connection in the physics literature is due to Anderson.

## 7. How best matching differs from statistical Procrustes: mass weights, centre of mass vs centroid, rotations vs reflections, labelling

### Takeaway
At the alignment step, best matching is a mass-weighted ordinary or full Procrustes fit with labelled points, proper rotations only, and translation to the centre of mass. It differs from statistical Procrustes (GPA) in seven ways. (1) Weights are physical masses, not equal or variance-based weights. (2) The translation aligns centres of mass, not unweighted centroids. (3) Only proper rotations (SO(3), continuous group generators) are used, so mirror images stay distinct shapes, whereas orthogonal Procrustes may admit reflections. (4) Correspondence is fixed by particle labels, with no permutation search. (5) The comparison is between infinitesimally close configurations along a path, producing a connection and horizontal lifts, not a one-off finite fit. (6) Scale is handled either by I-normalization and D = 0, or kept as the dynamical variable D. (7) The result feeds a potential-weighted geodesic principle (dynamics), not a mean shape or a statistical test.

### Cited Findings
- **Mass weighting and centre of mass:** the inner product is ⟨a, b⟩ = Σ m_j a_j·b_j, and "this brings in a weighting with the masses"; best matching "brings the centres of mass to coincidence". — [Barbour 2010, eq. (5) and §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368). "Best matching brings the centres of mass to coincidence and reduces the net rotation to zero." — [Barbour 2011/2012, Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183). Butterfield: each squared difference "is weighted by the mass of the particle concerned". — [Butterfield 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055)
- Barbour explicitly rejects principal axes as a frame choice: "We could consider choosing the axes along the principal axes of inertia but, except in the case of rigid bodies, such a choice has no dynamical significance and, moreover, leads to problems if the configuration happens to be symmetric." — [Barbour 2010, fn. 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- Anderson's reductions use "mass-weighted relative Jacobi interparticle cluster separation coordinates". The configuration-space radius is "the square root of the moment of inertia, I". — [Anderson 2015, Fig. 3 caption](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1923)
- **Continuous group and chirality:** best matching uses "the Euclidean generators of translations, rotations and dilatations". — [Barbour 2003b, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0309089). Barbour 2010 says it "exploits the fundamental mathematics of Lie groups". — [Barbour 2010, §9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368). On the 3-body shape sphere, "points in the southern hemisphere represent triangles which are mirror images of the corresponding ones … on the northern hemisphere", so mirror images are distinct shapes. — [Barbour, Lostaglio & Mercati 2013, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.6173). Contrast Butterfield's RCS, which uses "the improper Euclidean group … so as to identify oppositely handed polyhedra". — [Butterfield 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055)
- **Labelling:** Butterfield's analogy matches "red-to-red, green-to-green" dots, and his RCS definition requires "the m_i-vertex in π(x) being transformed to the m_i-vertex in π(y)" (labelled particles). — [Butterfield 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055). Anderson contrasts the *labelled* shape sphere with Kendall's "spherical blackboard": "The space of all unlabelled triangles is 1/3 of a hemisphere: Kendall's spherical blackboard." — [Anderson 2015, Fig. 2 caption](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1923)
- **Infinitesimal vs finite:** the dynamically relevant best matching is the thin-sandwich, first-order problem for "infinitesimally differing A and B", whose δλ → 0 limit "will clearly define a metric". — [Barbour 2003, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021). The finite-difference picture is "easier to visualize", but "calculations are more readily done with continuous variations". — [Barbour 2003b, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0309089)
- **Uniqueness:** Barbour asserts that the best-matched position is "necessarily unique" (2010) and that the thin-sandwich problem has a unique solution "since positive-definite quantities are being minimized" (2003, fn. 4). — [Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368); [Barbour 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021). Mercati notes "there are symmetric configurations (e.g., collinear states or total collisions)" where the fibre-bundle structure degenerates. — [Mercati 2014, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **Scale:** dilatational best matching requires dividing the kinetic metric by √I_cms (eq. 10). — [Barbour 2011/2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183). The auxiliary-variable form is q̄ = a q (eq. 24). — [Barbour 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)

### Inferences
- Masses vs centroid: for equal masses, Barbour's best matching reduces to standard (unweighted) Procrustes on labelled landmarks, and the centre of mass equals the centroid. For unequal masses it is weighted Procrustes with weights m_i. Rescaling coordinates by √m_i (or using mass-weighted Jacobi vectors, as Anderson does) turns it into unweighted Procrustes on N−1 relative vectors.
- Reflections: Barbour's continuous, infinitesimal construction never needs to consider reflections, since an infinitesimal displacement can't flip handedness. Orthogonal Procrustes over O(3), by contrast, may return det R = −1 for nearly planar or mirror-related configurations, unless it is restricted to SO(3) (the Kabsch determinant correction). In physics terms, mirror-image triangles are distinct points (northern and southern hemispheres) on the shape sphere, and collinear triangles (the equator) are where finite proper-rotation fits can become ill-conditioned. The finite Procrustes rotation for a planar 2D pair is unique except where the cross-covariance degenerates (collinear or coincident configurations). Barbour's "necessarily unique" claim is safe only for the infinitesimal problem away from such symmetric configurations.
- Labelling: physical particles are labelled (each has its own mass and identity), so no permutation/assignment search is ever done. Statistical shape analysis also usually assumes labelled landmarks. Unlabelled matching (ICP or assignment problems) has no counterpart in Barbour's scheme. Anderson's unlabelled "spherical blackboard" is a further quotient by permutations.
- Path dependence: summing best-matched infinitesimal rotations along a path (horizontal stacking) gives a frame that can differ from the direct finite Procrustes fit of the endpoints. The difference is the holonomy of the rotational connection (Littlejohn & Reinsch). For a demonstration app, comparing "chained" frame-to-frame best matching against direct Procrustes over a closed loop would show this.
- Purpose: Procrustes/GPA estimates mean shapes and tests shape differences. Best matching defines equilocality, inertial frames and duration for an entire closed universe.

### Gaps
- No source found that systematically compares best matching and weighted/generalized Procrustes on these specific points. The comparisons above are my synthesis from the cited definitions.

## 8. Critical and philosophical discussions

### Takeaway
The main philosophical engagement is in the *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* around 2000–2002. Belot (2000) is on geometry and motion. Pooley & Brown (2002) argue that Barbour–Bertotti intrinsic dynamics gives "a genuinely relational interpretation" of Newtonian mechanics that is "more explanatory" than substantivalism. Butterfield (2002) is a long critical notice of *The End of Time*, sympathetic to the classical Machian programme and focused on time capsules and the quantum proposals. Later work includes Gryb (Jacobi's principle; background independence from best matching), Gryb & Thébault ("Time remains", 2016), and Anderson's problem-of-time programme. The SEP entry "Absolute and Relational Space and Motion: Classical Theories" (Hoefer, Huggett & Read, first published 19 July 2021) lists Barbour 1982 and Belot 2000 in its bibliography but does not discuss best matching in its main text.

### Cited Findings
- Pooley & Brown 2002, abstract: "The implications for the substantivalist–relationalist controversy of Barbour and Bertotti's successful implementation of a Machian approach to dynamics are investigated. It is argued that in the context of Newtonian mechanics, the Machian framework provides a genuinely relational interpretation of dynamics and that it is more explanatory than the conventional, substantival interpretation. In a companion paper (Pooley [2002a]), the viability of the Machian framework as an interpretation of relativistic physics is explored." Sections include "Dynamics on the relative configuration space" and "Intrinsic particle dynamics". Record: *BJPS* 53 (2), 183–204, DOI 10.1093/bjps/53.2.183. — [DOI / OpenAlex abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/53.2.183)
- Belot, G. (2000). "Geometry and motion". *BJPS* 51 (4), 561–595. DOI 10.1093/bjps/51.4.561 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/51.4.561)
- Butterfield's arXiv abstract: "I discuss J. Barbour's Machian theories of dynamics, and his proposal that a Machian perspective enables one to solve the problem of time in quantum geometrodynamics (by saying that there is no time). I concentrate on his recent book 'The End of Time' (1999)." He criticizes the popular formulations (pp. 29–30, 266–267) because they "blur the difference" between a modest claim and radical metaphysics, "so that the plausibility of the former accrues invalidly to the latter." He also says Barbour "has yet to tell us how his proposals, e.g. about time capsules," work in detail. — [Butterfield 2001 (BJPS 2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055)
- Gryb & Thébault: "On one popular view, the general covariance of gravity implies that change is relational in a strong sense … leads to a 'fundamentally timeless' formalism for quantum gravity. Here, we will show how one may avoid this acute 'problem of time'. Under our view, duration is still regarded as relative, but temporal succession is taken to be absolute." Record: *BJPS* 67 (3), 663–705 (2016), DOI 10.1093/bjps/axv009. — [arXiv 1408.2691](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2691)
- Gryb 2010 (CQG): a definition of background independence that "draws from Barbour's best-matching framework"; best-matching actions "must have a local gauge symmetry". — [arXiv 1003.1973](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)
- Gryb 2010 (PRD): Jacobi's principle "is known to lead to a problem of time … using Jacobi's principle for non-relativistic particles is equivalent classically to Newton's theory but leads to a time-independent Schrödinger equation upon Dirac quantization." — [arXiv 0804.2900](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2900)
- Anderson's relational particle mechanics as a problem-of-time arena: "RPM's are analogous to various significant formulations of GR". Scaled RPM's E and L are analogous to the GR Hamiltonian and momentum constraints, and pure-shape RPM's D to the maximal-slicing condition p = 0. — [Anderson 2015, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1923); see also [Anderson 2011, arXiv:1111.1472](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.1472)
- SEP: "Absolute and Relational Space and Motion: Classical Theories" (Carl Hoefer, Nick Huggett, James Read; first published Mon 19 July 2021). Its bibliography ("Notable Philosophical Discussions") lists Barbour 1982 (BJPS 33: 251–274), Belot 2000 (BJPS 51: 561–595) and Pooley 2013, but the main text does not discuss Barbour or best matching (checked this session). — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-theories-classical/)
- The philosophical precedent for "minimize motion" identification of places across time is Peacocke 1979 (pro) and Forbes 1987 (con), per Butterfield. — [Butterfield 2001, fn. 15](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103055)

### Inferences
- Philosophers broadly accept that intrinsic dynamics/best matching is a genuinely relational reformulation of Newtonian N-body theory: it recovers the subset with P = L = 0, plus E = 0 and D = 0 in the scale-invariant version. The disputes concern (a) whether the extra predictive strength (L = 0 for the universe) is an empirical advantage or an ad hoc restriction, (b) relativity and field theory (Pooley's companion paper), and (c) Barbour's timeless quantum metaphysics (Butterfield; Gryb & Thébault).

- The SEP post-Newtonian companion entry was not checked.
- Lynden-Bell's independent "relative Newtonian mechanics" (in Barbour & Pfister (eds), *Mach's Principle*, Birkhäuser 1995) is cited by Anderson 2007 ("D. Lynden-Bell, in [Barbour & Pfister]") but was not read.
