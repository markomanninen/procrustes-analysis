# Procrustes alignment with unknown correspondence, and the discontinuities ("jumps") of the best fit

Scope: aligning point sets when the vertex pairing (a permutation), and possibly a reflection, must be chosen along with the rotation, as in this repository's triangle page. The page tries all 6 pairings × {proper, mirror} = 12 candidates, takes the best, and optionally blends all 12 with weights e^(−(rms² − rms²_best)/σ²) ([js/procrustes.js](../../../../js/procrustes.js), `softFit`; [index.html](../../../../index.html)). These notes cover the theory of why the hard choice jumps, and the soft and probabilistic alternatives in statistics, computer vision and chemistry. Literature status as of 28 October 2026.

Notation used below. z = centred vertices of ABC (the target), u = centred vertices of DEF (the fitted triangle), both as complex numbers. For a pairing π, s_π = Σ z̄_i u_π(i). For a mirror candidate, u is replaced by ū. The fit error at turn φ is E(φ) = Σ|z|² + Σ|u|² − 2 Re(e^{iφ} s), which is smallest at φ = −arg s ([docs/reflections.md](../../../../docs/reflections.md)).

## Q1. Unlabelled shape analysis: quotienting by permutations, and solving permutation × rotation jointly

### Takeaway
Unlabelled (or "unregistered") shape analysis treats the matching as an unknown: a matching matrix or labelling sits beside the rotation, translation and scale. It is estimated by Bayesian MCMC (Green & Mardia 2006; Dryden, Hirst & Melville 2007; Kenobi & Dryden 2012; Dryden & Mardia 2016, ch. 14), or optimised by alternating Hungarian assignment with SVD/Kabsch, by convex (SDP or Birkhoff) relaxations, or by exact or certified-global methods. These include Maron et al.'s PM‑SDP, which is exact for asymmetric or bilaterally symmetric isometric shapes, and, for the planar problem, the October 2026 "Rubix" permutation-polygon algorithm. Rubix uses the same complex correlation s_π = Σ x̄_i y_π(i) as the triangle page. For n = 3 the full enumeration of 3! (×2 with mirrors) candidates is exact and cheap. The theory says that the solution set is non-unique precisely at configurations with symmetry.

### Cited Findings
**Unlabelled shape in the statistical shape-analysis canon**
- The 2nd edition of Dryden & Mardia's *Statistical Shape Analysis, with Applications in R* (Wiley 2016) has a dedicated chapter, Ch. 14 "Unlabelled size-and-shape and shape analysis", pp. 341–352 (DOI 10.1002/9781119072492.ch14). — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119072492.ch14)
- Per a publisher table of contents (search summary; full text not seen), the chapter's sections are 14.1 the Green–Mardia model, 14.2 the Procrustes model, and 14.3 related methods. The chapter turns from labelled landmarks to Bayesian matching of unlabelled point sets, where correspondence must be estimated, not assumed. — [O'Reilly TOC page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/statistical-shape-analysis/9780470699621/c14.xhtml)
- Kendall's labelled shape space for k planar points is a complex projective space, and for triangles it is a sphere (Kendall 1984, Bull. London Math. Soc. 16:81–121). The Riemannian structure of general shape spaces Σ^k_m was given by Le & Kendall (1993). Le & Kendall's abstract notes that Kendall had treated only the "atypically simple" cases m = 1, 2. — [Kendall 1984](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/16.2.81); [Le & Kendall 1993, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176349259)

**Bayesian unlabelled matching**
- Green & Mardia (2006, Biometrika 93:235–254) introduce hierarchical models for matching configurations "in which the points … are either unlabelled or have at most a partial labelling constraining the matching, and in which some points may only appear in one of the configurations". They make "simultaneous inference about the matching and the transformation", with a Poisson process for hidden true locations, EM and MCMC. — [Biometrika / OpenAlex abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/93.2.235); [arXiv math/0503712](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503712)
- In Green & Mardia, the matching is a matching matrix M with M_jk indicating whether x_j and y_k derive from the same hidden point. The rotation prior is the matrix Fisher distribution, or the von Mises in 2D, which is conditionally conjugate. In 2D the full conditional for θ stays von Mises with κ cos ν updated to (κ cos ν + S11 + S22) and κ sin ν to (κ sin ν − S12 + S21). — [arXiv math/0503712, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0503712)
- Green & Mardia argue against reporting the MAP matching. "The posterior is multi-modal, and different modes may have different 'widths'… So there is no intrinsic attraction to the MAP estimate". Instead they derive a loss-function-based point estimate of M "that depends only on a single parameter of the family". — [arXiv math/0503712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0503712)
- Dryden, Hirst & Melville (2007, Biometrics 63:237–251) compare unlabelled point sets (steroid molecules) with "a type of mixture model … the parameters of the distribution are a labeling matrix (indicating which pairs of points match) and a concentration parameter". The likelihood is invariant under rotations and translations. — [Biometrics abstract via Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1541-0420.2006.00622.x)
- Kenobi & Dryden (2012, Bayesian Analysis 7:547–566) compare two likelihoods for unlabelled matching, "based on the Procrustes size-and-shape and the full configuration", using MCMC. They propose "occasional large jumps in the burn-in period" to improve convergence of the Procrustes algorithm, applied to protein binding sites. — [Bayesian Analysis abstract](https://doi.org/10.1214/12-BA718)

**Joint optimisation over permutation × rotation**
- Maron, Dym, Kezurer, Kovalsky & Lipman (2016, ACM TOG 35(4)) name "the problem of simultaneously aligning and labelling two point clouds … the Procrustes matching (PM) problem". They note that PM problems are non-convex and "can only be solved locally using … ICP … Good initialization is crucial", and introduce a convex SDP relaxation that is "guaranteed to return a correct global solution … when matching two isometric shapes which are either asymmetric or bilaterally symmetric". — [ACM TOG abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/2897824.2925913)
- Dym & Lipman (2017, SIAM J. Optim. 27:1513–1530) show "Exact PM to be computationally equivalent to the graph isomorphism problem". They find that "for symmetric shapes, PM has multiple solutions. The non-convex set of optimal solutions of PM is strictly contained in the convex set of optimal solutions of PM-SDP". The PM solutions are the extreme points of that set, and a randomised algorithm "returns all solutions of PM with equal probability". — [arXiv 1606.01548](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01548)
- Grave, Joulin & Berthet ("Wasserstein Procrustes", AISTATS 2019) jointly estimate an orthogonal Q and a permutation P. "The problem is not jointly convex in Q and P but … there are exact solutions for each optimization problem if the other variable is fixed. Naively alternating … quickly converges to bad local minima even on small problems". The matching for a fixed Q costs O(n³), or O(n²) up to log factors via Sinkhorn. They initialise with the Gold & Rangarajan Birkhoff-polytope relaxation solved by Frank–Wolfe. — [arXiv 1805.11222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.11222)
- Alvarez-Melis, Jegelka & Jaakkola (AISTATS 2019) cast "optimal transport in the presence of latent global transformations", such as reflection or rotation, as a joint optimisation over the transport plan and the transformation. — [arXiv 1806.09277](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.09277)
- Even, Ganassali, Maier & Massoulié (2024) study the "Procrustes-Wasserstein problem", where Y is a noisy copy of X "up to an orthogonal transformation and a relabeling". They give information-theoretic thresholds and a "Ping-Pong algorithm, alternatively estimating the orthogonal transformation and the relabeling". — [arXiv 2405.14532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14532)
- Niu, Schramm & Xu (July 2026) give a polynomial-time algorithm for exact recovery of the permutation in high-dimensional Procrustes matching at constant correlation, by "weighted counts of a specially chosen family of 'wide' trees". — [arXiv 2607.08538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08538)
- **Directly relevant to the triangle page.** Bhattacharjee, Campbell & Shome, "Rubix" (arXiv, 7 Oct 2026), solve "the equally weighted planar problem globally under squared Euclidean loss". In their words, "Each matching σ of two centered n-point sets defines a complex correlation z_σ = Σ_i x̄_i y_σ(i). Their convex hull is the permutation polygon: supporting vertices give optimal matchings at fixed rotations, and the farthest vertex gives the global alignment." They "prove the sharp bound of n(n−1) vertices", answering "Rote's rotation-assignment open problem", with O(n⁵) assignment queries. — [arXiv 2610.10408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10408)
- The page's own code computes exactly this quantity, s = Σ z̄_i u_perm(i), with best turn −arg s. It ranks the pairings by error, and Σ|z|² + ρ²Σ|u|² − 2ρ|s| shows that ranking them is the same as ranking |s|. — [js/procrustes.js, `candidate`](../../../../js/procrustes.js)
- The linear assignment step is classically solved by the Hungarian method. Kuhn (1955) poses the assignment problem and solves it, and Munkres (1957) gives the algorithmic version. — [Kuhn 1955](https://doi.org/10.1002/nav.3800020109); [Munkres 1957](https://doi.org/10.1137/0105003)
- The rotation step for a fixed pairing is the orthogonal Procrustes / Wahba / Kabsch problem: Schönemann 1966, Wahba 1965, Kabsch 1976/1978, Arun et al. 1987, Horn 1987 (quaternions), Umeyama 1991 (similarity with det correction). — [Schönemann 1966](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02289451); [Wahba 1965](https://doi.org/10.1137/1007077); [Kabsch 1976](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0567739476001873); [Umeyama 1991](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.88573)

### Inferences
- **The triangle page solves the unlabelled problem exactly by enumeration.** With n = 3 there are 3! = 6 pairings, which matches the Rubix bound n(n−1) = 6 on the vertices of the permutation polygon. With mirrors, the six conj-correlations Σ z̄_i ū_π(i) are added. The best candidate is the s of largest modulus, i.e. the farthest point of the 12-point set {s_π, s′_π} from the origin. The hard fit is therefore an argmax over a finite set of continuous functions, and nothing more elaborate (Hungarian, SDP) is needed.
- **The geometry of the 12 candidates on Kendall's shape sphere (triangle case).** The page states that |s|²/(‖z‖²‖u‖²) = (1 + n_ABC · n_DEF′)/2, where n are unit shape-sphere vectors ([js/procrustes.js](../../../../js/procrustes.js)). So rms is a monotone function of the great-circle angle between ABC's shape point and the image of DEF, and the best candidate is the nearest of DEF's 12 images. Montgomery's account of the shape sphere identifies the group acting on those images (see Q3).
- **Unlabelled shape space is an orbifold.** Quotienting Kendall's (reflection-)shape space by S_k (and O(m)) gives a space whose singular points are configurations with a non-trivial stabiliser, i.e. symmetric configurations. These are exactly the configurations where the Procrustes-matching argmin is non-unique (Dym & Lipman's "multiple solutions" for symmetric shapes). The jump phenomenon of the triangle page is the planar, n = 3 instance of this general fact.
- Alternation (ICP-style: Hungarian with rotation fixed, then Kabsch with pairing fixed) is the default heuristic. It converges to local minima (Grave et al.). Exact global methods exist for small n (enumeration), for the plane (Rubix), and for 3D via branch-and-bound or SDP relaxations (Maron et al.; Go-ICP-type bounds in Q2; Go-PERMDIST in Q5).

## Q2. Registration with unknown correspondence: ICP, softassign/RPM, EM‑ICP, CPD, GMM, global methods, and the link to e^(−Δ²/σ²)

### Takeaway
All the soft methods replace ICP's hard nearest-neighbour or Hungarian assignment with Gaussian (Boltzmann) weights exp(−‖x − T y‖²/(2σ²)) over correspondences. They let the width σ (a temperature) shrink, either by a deterministic-annealing schedule (softassign/RPM, TPS‑RPM, multi-scale EM‑ICP) or by EM re-estimation (CPD). As σ → 0 they recover the hard assignment, and entropic OT/Sinkhorn makes this limit exact (Mena et al. 2018). The triangle page's e^(−(rms²−rms²_best)/σ²) is the same Boltzmann/softmin construction. It applies to whole global hypotheses (12 pairing × chirality candidates) rather than to individual point pairs, which makes it a Bayesian model average over a discrete latent variable.

### Cited Findings
**Hard-assignment baseline**
- ICP "solves the rigid point cloud registration problem iteratively in two steps: (1) make hard assignments of spatially closest point correspondences, and then (2) find the least-squares rigid transformation". Hard assignments "are sensitive to the initial rigid transformation and noisy/outlier points, which often cause ICP to converge to wrong local minima". This description is RPM‑Net's summary of Besl & McKay 1992. — [Yew & Lee 2020, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13479); [Besl & McKay 1992 record](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.121791)
- ICP, "being based on local iterative optimization … is known to be susceptible to local minima … only local optimality is guaranteed". — [Go-ICP abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.03344)

**Softassign / RPM and deterministic annealing**
- Gold, Rangarajan, Lu, Pappu & Mjolsness (1998, Pattern Recognition 31:1019–1031) solve pose and correspondence jointly "using a combination of optimization techniques such as deterministic annealing and the softassign". The method works on noisy 2D/3D sets "of unequal sizes", with "over thirty thousand experiments". — [Pattern Recognition abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0031-3203(98)80010-1)
- Rangarajan, Chui & Bookstein (IPMI 1997, LNCS pp. 29–42) is the "softassign Procrustes matching algorithm", which the triangle page's comment cites as the source of its soft fit. The bibliographic record was verified; I could not access the abstract. — [Springer record](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-63046-5_3); [js/procrustes.js comment](../../../../js/procrustes.js)
- Softassign is Sinkhorn normalisation of a Boltzmann matrix. Mena et al. prove (Theorem 1) that the Sinkhorn operator satisfies S(X/τ) = argmax_{P ∈ Birkhoff} ⟨P, X⟩_F + τ h(P), where h is the entropy, and that as τ → 0 it converges to the hard matching M(X) = argmax_{P ∈ permutations} ⟨P, X⟩_F. The analogue for a single categorical choice is softmax_τ, which converges "to a vertex of the simplex, a one-hot vector" as τ → 0. — [Mena, Belanger, Linderman & Snoek, ICLR 2018, arXiv 1802.08665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08665)
- Cuturi (2013) smooths the OT problem "with an entropic regularization term", computable "through Sinkhorn-Knopp's matrix scaling algorithm" orders of magnitude faster. — [arXiv 1306.0895](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0895); see also [Peyré & Cuturi 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00567)
- Yuille & Kosowsky (1994) connect mean-field / statistical-physics algorithms to barrier-function and interior-point methods, with the linear assignment problem and deterministic annealing as the worked example. — [Neural Computation abstract](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1994.6.3.341)
- Rose (1998) is the standard review of deterministic annealing for clustering and related optimisation. — [Proc. IEEE record](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.726788)
- RPM‑Net (CVPR 2020) "uses the differentiable Sinkhorn layer and annealing to get soft assignments", with a secondary network predicting "optimal annealing parameters". Deep Closest Point (ICCV 2019) uses attention to "approximate combinatorial matching" plus "a differentiable singular value decomposition (SVD) layer". — [RPM-Net arXiv 2003.13479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13479); [DCP arXiv 1905.03304](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03304)

**EM / Gaussian-mixture view**
- CPD (Myronenko & Song 2010, TPAMI 32:2262–2275) treats registration "as a probability density estimation problem", fitting GMM centroids (one set) to data (the other). It uses equal isotropic covariance σ², equal mixing weights 1/M, and an extra uniform component of weight w for outliers. — [arXiv 0905.2635](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2635)
- CPD's E-step correspondence posterior (rigid, Fig. 2) is
  p_mn = exp(−‖x_n − (sRy_m + t)‖²/(2σ²)) / [Σ_k exp(−‖x_n − (sRy_k + t)‖²/(2σ²)) + (2πσ²)^{D/2} (w/(1−w)) (M/N)].
  σ² is initialised to (1/(DNM)) Σ_n Σ_m ‖x_n − y_m‖² and re-estimated in each M-step as σ² = (1/(N_P D))(tr(X̂ᵀ d(Pᵀ1) X̂) − s tr(AᵀR)). — [CPD, arXiv PDF, §4 and Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2635)
- CPD on annealing: "RPM also uses deterministic annealing on σ², which requires to set the starting and stopping criteria as well as the annealing rate … We argue that it is preferable to estimate σ² instead of using deterministic annealing … for the rigid point set registration problem the rigid parametrization is a strong constraint that alleviates the advantages of the deterministic annealing." — [CPD §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2635)
- CPD reports that "in RPM alternating soft-assignment of correspondences and transformation is equivalent to the Expectation Maximization (EM) algorithm for GMM", and that probabilistic methods commonly add an outlier term and use "deterministic annealing on the Gaussian width to avoid poor local minima". CPD also states that TPS‑RPM (Chui & Rangarajan 2003) uses "deterministic annealing and alternate updates for soft-assignment and TPS parameters". — [CPD §1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2635); [Chui & Rangarajan 2003 record](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1077-3142(03)00009-2)
- Jian & Vemuri (2011, TPAMI 33:1633–1645) represent both point sets as GMMs and minimise a discrepancy between the mixtures, instantiated as the closed-form L2 distance. They show that "ICP … and several existing point set registration methods … can be reinterpreted meaningfully in our general framework". — [TPAMI abstract (OpenAlex)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2010.223)
- Multi-scale EM‑ICP (Granger & Pennec, ECCV 2002, pp. 418–432): bibliographic record verified (Crossref; HAL inria‑00615911). I did not see the abstract or text this session. — [Springer record](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-47979-1_28); [HAL](https://inria.hal.science/inria-00615911v1)

**Global and certifiable methods**
- Go‑ICP (Yang, Li, Campbell & Jia 2016, TPAMI 38:2241–2254) is "the first globally optimal algorithm … for Euclidean (rigid) registration of two 3D point-sets under the L2 error metric defined in ICP". It uses branch-and-bound over SE(3) with novel upper and lower bounds, and local ICP integrated. — [arXiv 1605.03344](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.03344)
- TEASER (Yang, Shi & Carlone 2021, T‑RO 37:314–333) addresses "large amounts of outlier correspondences" with a truncated least squares cost and decouples scale, rotation and translation. Its "TLS rotation estimation can be relaxed to a semidefinite program (SDP) and the relaxation is tight", and the algorithm is certifiable. It assumes putative correspondences (with outliers) rather than none. — [arXiv 2001.07715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07715)
- Recent invariant OT distances avoid explicit alignment. Example: RISWIE (Oct 2025) "matches optimal signed permutations along axes" to get rigid invariance with near-linear complexity. — [arXiv 2510.10233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10233)

### Inferences
- **The page's weight as a posterior over 12 discrete hypotheses.** Take the model z_i = a·u_π(i) + ε_i (or a·ū_π(i)), ε_i ~ N(0, τ²I₂) i.i.d., a uniform prior over the 12 candidates, and the similarity a profiled out (set to its optimum). The posterior weight of candidate k is ∝ exp(−E_k/(2τ²)) = exp(−3·rms_k²/(2τ²)). This is the page's weight with σ² = 2τ²/3. Subtracting rms²_best only normalises. The soft fit (weighted mean of the partner positions) is then the posterior mean of the fitted vertex positions, i.e. the Bayes estimate under squared-error loss. The hard fit is the MAP estimate, which Green & Mardia argue is unattractive for multimodal posteriors.
- **Profiling versus marginalising the rotation.** Integrating the turn φ out with a uniform prior (ρ = 1) instead gives the weight ∝ exp(−(Σ|z|²+Σ|u|²)/(2τ²)) · I₀(|s_k|/τ²), a von Mises normaliser, the same structure as Green & Mardia's conditionally conjugate von Mises update. Since I₀(x) ≈ eˣ/√(2πx), this is the page's weight times a mild factor |s_k|^(−1/2). The page's profile-likelihood weights are therefore a close approximation to the fully Bayesian ones when σ is small relative to the triangle size.
- **Softmin and free energy.** F_σ = −σ² log Σ_k exp(−rms_k²/σ²) is a smooth surrogate for min_k rms_k², with min − σ² log 12 ≤ F_σ ≤ min. Its gradient is Σ_k w_k ∇rms_k², with exactly the page's weights. Lowering σ step by step, as the page's comment notes, is deterministic annealing. The CPD argument suggests that with a strongly constrained (rigid or similarity) model, σ can instead be estimated from the residual. One option is the EM-type estimate σ² ∝ Σ_k w_k rms_k², which would make σ self-tuning rather than a slider.
- **Pairwise versus global soft assignment.** Softassign, CPD and EM‑ICP put Boltzmann weights on individual point pairs and then fit one rotation to the soft cross-covariance, so their output is always a rigid or similarity image of the source. The page instead weights 12 complete fits and averages their outputs. The induced soft correspondence matrix M_ij = Σ_k w_k [π_k(i) = j] is a convex combination of permutation matrices, hence doubly stochastic: the same Birkhoff-polytope object that softassign/Sinkhorn produce. But the averaged output is not, in general, congruent to DEF (see Q3). A softassign-style alternative for the page would put a single turn on the weighted correlation Σ_k w_k s_k, over the proper candidates only, since proper and mirror maps cannot be averaged into one rigid map.
- **Units.** The page's σ is in absolute coordinate units (slider 0–3, [index.html](../../../../index.html)), so the same σ blurs a small triangle more than a large one. Normalising by centroid size, i.e. using Kendall's full Procrustes distance in the exponent, would make the blending scale-invariant.

## Q3. Why the best fit is discontinuous; instability near symmetric or degenerate configurations; averaging rotations

### Takeaway
The minimum error over candidates is continuous (Berge's maximum theorem), but the minimiser is only upper hemicontinuous. It jumps wherever two candidates tie. For planar triangles with mirrors allowed, the 12 candidates are the orbit of a finite reflection group (order 12) acting on Kendall's shape sphere. Its mirrors are the three great circles of isosceles triangles and the equator of collinear ones (Montgomery 2015). The hard fit jumps exactly when the target triangle crosses one of these mirrors. I confirmed this numerically with the page's own code. Separately, each candidate's rotation is ill-conditioned when its correlation s → 0, the 2D case of the polar factor or SVD degeneracy (coinciding singular values, Coutsias et al. 2004). Blending candidates by averaging rotations or positions is itself delicate: arithmetic means shrink, quaternion signs and antipodal ties break naive means, and Karcher means are unique only within balls of bounded radius (Moakher; Markley et al.; Hartley et al.; Afsari).

### Cited Findings
**Continuity of the value versus the argmin**
- Berge's maximum theorem: for a continuous objective and a continuous, compact-valued constraint correspondence, "the value function f* is continuous, and the set of maximizers C* is upper-hemicontinuous with nonempty and compact values". The theorem guarantees only "some, but not all, of that continuity" of the solutions. Original: Berge, *Topological Spaces*, 1963, ch. 6 §3. — [Wikipedia, "Maximum theorem"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_theorem)
- The page's own analysis reaches the same conclusion: "a minimum over 12 smooth functions of the vertex positions … is continuous, but the argument of the minimum is not … That jump is the correct answer changing". — [docs/reflections.md §2](../../../../docs/reflections.md)

**The triangle shape sphere and its symmetry group**
- Montgomery (2015, Amer. Math. Monthly 122:299–321) describes the shape sphere as "the space of oriented similarity classes of planar triangles". In shape space, w₃ is the signed area, and "the collinear triangle locus corresponds to the plane w₃ = 0". Two triangles are congruent iff π(q₁) = π(q₂) or π(q₁) = σ(π(q₂)), where σ is reflection across the collinear plane. Congruence classes (reflections allowed) are therefore the closed half-space w₃ ≥ 0. — [arXiv 1402.0841, Thm/§7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0841)
- Montgomery again: "The space of isosceles triangles forms three great circles on the shape sphere, each circle passing through the two Lagrange points [equilateral triangles] which are represented as the North and South Poles". Each isosceles circle meets the collinear equator at an Euler point and a binary-collision point. A transposition of two (equal-mass) labels acts on the shape sphere as "a half-twist about the line connecting EUL₂ and B₂". Composing it with the reflection about the triangle's symmetry axis gives an isometry "whose effect on shape space is that of reflection about the plane ISOSC₂". — [arXiv 1402.0841, proof of Thm 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0841)
- The page uses the same sphere (equal-mass Jacobi coordinates). "The poles are the two equilateral triangles, the equator the collinear ones, and the height is (4/√3)·area / Σ|p − K|² … the best pairing is the image of DEF nearest to ABC, and two pairings tie on the great circle halfway between their images". — [js/procrustes.js](../../../../js/procrustes.js)

**Instability of the Procrustes rotation at degenerate configurations**
- Coutsias, Seok & Dill (2004) prove the quaternion method equivalent to Kabsch. They "give a complete enumeration of the special cases in terms of the arrangement of the eigenvalues of a traceless, 4 × 4 symmetric matrix". The optimal rotation is non-unique when the top eigenvalue is degenerate. — [J. Comput. Chem. abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20110)
- Markley et al. make the same point for averaging: the quaternion average "is unique if and only if the two largest eigenvalues of M are not equal". — [NASA NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20070017872/downloads/20070017872.pdf)
- Differentiating the SVD or eigendecomposition gives a matrix K̃ with off-diagonal entries 1/(σ_i − σ_j) (Ionescu, Vantzos & Sminchisescu, eqs. 34 and 37 of the arXiv version), which diverge when two values coincide. Wang et al. (2019) note that backpropagating eigendecomposition "tends to be numerically unstable". Papadopoulo & Lourakis (ECCV 2000) derive the SVD Jacobian for vision applications. — [arXiv 1509.07838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.07838); [arXiv 1906.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09023); [Papadopoulo & Lourakis record](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-45054-8_36)
- The Procrustes rotation in O(m) is the orthogonal polar factor of the cross-covariance matrix, which has a "best approximation property" (Higham 1986). Perturbation bounds for the polar factor are in Higham (1986) and R.-C. Li (1995), who asks "How much may Q change if A is perturbed?". — [Higham 1986 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1137/0907079); [Li 1995 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479893256359)
- Söderkvist (1993, BIT 33:687–694) gives a perturbation analysis of the orthogonal Procrustes problem. Söderkvist & Wedin (1993, J. Biomech. 26:1473–1477) treat "well-configured markers" for skeletal motion. Wedin (1972) gives SVD perturbation bounds. All three records verified; text not seen. — [Söderkvist 1993](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01990543); [Söderkvist & Wedin 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9290(93)90098-Y); [Wedin 1972](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01932678)
- Symmetry-aware metrics are fragile in a stronger sense. Sverdlov, Rosen & Dym (Nov 2025) show that the Procrustes Matching metric and hard Gromov–Wasserstein distances "are not bi-Lipschitz equivalent", and that "popular invariant networks for point clouds are not bi-Lipschitz with respect to the PM metric". — [arXiv 2511.11735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11735)

**Averaging rotations and why naive averaging fails**
- Moakher (2002) defines invariant means on SO(3). The Frobenius (chordal) mean is "the closest special orthogonal matrix to the usual arithmetic mean of the given rotation matrices", while the intrinsic-metric mean is the Riemannian centre of mass (Karcher mean). — [SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. abstract](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479801383877); [Karcher 1977](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160300502)
- Markley, Cheng, Crassidis & Oshman (2007): naive quaternion averaging Σ w_i q_i "has two flaws": the result is not a unit quaternion, and since "q and −q represent the same rotation … changing the sign of any q_i should not change the average, but … Eq. (1) does not have this property". The fix is the eigenvector of M = Σ w_i q_i q_iᵀ with the largest eigenvalue. Uniqueness is guaranteed if all estimates lie within a rotation angle "less than π/2" of some reference. — [NASA NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20070017872/downloads/20070017872.pdf)
- Hartley, Trumpf, Dai & Li (2013, IJCV 103:267–305): for the L2 geodesic or quaternion metrics each term d²(R, R_i) "is strictly convex … on an open ball B̊(R_i, π)". The total cost is strictly convex on each connected component of the intersection of these balls, which "in general … consists of several components". — [author PDF](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~hartley/Papers/PDF/Hartley-Trumpf:Rotation-averaging:IJCV.pdf)
- Afsari (2011) gives a radius bound, in terms of injectivity radius and curvature, on a ball containing the data that "ensures existence and uniqueness of the global Riemannian Lᵖ center of mass". — [Proc. AMS abstract / OA PDF](https://www.ams.org/proc/2011-139-02/S0002-9939-2010-10541-5/S0002-9939-2010-10541-5.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Why the jump locus is "isosceles or flat".** By Montgomery's description, the 12 candidate operations form a group of order 12 acting on the triangle sphere:
  - the 3-cycles are rotations by ±120° about the polar (equilateral) axis;
  - the transpositions are half-turns about equatorial axes;
  - the reflection is the mirror in the equatorial (collinear) plane;
  - each transposition composed with the reflection is the mirror in an isosceles plane.

  This is the point group D₃h. It is a finite reflection group generated by four mirrors: the equator and the three isosceles great circles. Together they cut the sphere into 12 congruent spherical triangles, each with a 60° corner at an equilateral pole and two 90° corners on the equator.

  For a finite reflection group, the orbit point nearest a given point is the one in the same closed chamber, a standard fact about Coxeter groups, e.g. ⟨x, wy⟩ ≤ ⟨x, y⟩ for x, y in one chamber. So the hard best fit is "the image of DEF lying in ABC's chamber". It changes, and the drawn fit jumps, exactly when ABC crosses a mirror, i.e. passes through an isosceles or collinear shape. The jump size is twice the distance of DEF's image from that mirror, so it vanishes if DEF is itself near-symmetric.
- **Numerical confirmation (run this session on the page's `analyse`, mirrors on).** I moved ABC's apex along y = 1.7 with A = (0,0), B = (3,0) and a fixed scalene DEF. The hard fit switched at x ≈ 0.529, 1.500 and 2.473, and all three are isosceles: |BC| = |AB|, |CA| = |CB|, and |AC| = |AB| respectively. The per-vertex jumps were 0.33, 0.12 and 0.33. ABC passing through a collinear shape switched candidate 6 → 0 with a jump of 2.67.
- **DEF crossing a mirror gives no positional jump.** When DEF crossed an isosceles or collinear shape instead, the selected candidate index changed (9 → 2, 3 → 9), but the fitted partner positions moved by 0.0000. Folding DEF's point into ABC's chamber is a continuous map, so only the vertex labels switch. I infer that the visible jumps come from the target crossing mirrors, while a mirror crossing of the fitted triangle only relabels.
- **Without mirrors the jump locus depends on DEF.** With mirrors off, the six candidates form the rotation group D₃, which is not a reflection group. The switch points in the same scan were at x ≈ 0.134 and 2.172, which are not isosceles. The jump locus is then the Voronoi (perpendicular-bisector) boundary of DEF's 6-point orbit and moves with DEF.
- **Per-candidate degeneracy (s = 0).** For a single pairing, φ = −arg s, with |dφ| = |ds|/|s|. The turn is undefined when s = 0, which happens iff DEF's image is antipodal to ABC on the sphere (the pair at maximal Procrustes distance ρ = π/2). An example is ABC equilateral against a proper copy of the oppositely oriented equilateral DEF. This is the planar instance of the polar-factor / Kabsch degeneracy. In 2D, the proper rotation is non-unique iff the cross-covariance H has σ₁ = σ₂ and det H < 0, or H = 0. The mirror fit is non-unique iff σ₁ = σ₂ and det H > 0. This is my derivation from s and its conjugate counterpart; it matches Coutsias et al.'s "special cases".
  - The best candidate never has s = 0, because the group orbit always contains a non-antipodal image. Non-best candidates can, and in the soft blend they contribute w_k times an undefined turn.
  - With scaling on, the page's map a = ρe^{iφ} with ρ = |s|/Σ|u|² equals s̄/Σ|u|², a polynomial in the coordinates, so that candidate collapses continuously to the centroid. With scaling off (ρ = 1), a = s̄/|s| is discontinuous at s = 0.
- **Collinear configurations.** In 2D a collinear ABC makes H rank 1 (σ₂ = 0), but the proper turn stays unique. What becomes degenerate is the choice of chirality: for real z, the mirror correlation is the complex conjugate of the proper one, so |s′| = |s| and the proper and mirror fits tie. In 3D the analogue is a coplanar (rank-2) configuration, for which Kabsch's det-sign correction costs nothing. A collinear 3D configuration (rank 1) leaves the rotation about its line undetermined.
- **The soft fit flattens at a chirality tie.** For a collinear ABC, every proper candidate ties with its mirror partner. The equal-weight mean of a fit P and its reflection across ABC's line is the projection of P onto that line. Numerically, with the page's `softFit` and a collinear ABC, the blended triangle had area 0.0000 (DEF's area is 3.08) for every σ tried (0.3, 1, 3). Generally, at a tie caused by a symmetry h of ABC, the 50/50 blend (P + hP)/2 is the projection onto h's fixed set. A collinear target gives a flat fit; an isosceles target gives an isosceles fit. This is the soft-fit counterpart of the page's §3 observation that turns and mirrors cannot be joined without passing a degenerate map.
- **Averaging positions versus transforms.** The page averages partner positions, which amounts to averaging the complex maps a_k (and their mirror versions). Like Moakher's arithmetic mean of rotation matrices, |Σ w_k a_k| < 1 shrinks the result. Mixing proper and mirror maps gives a general real-linear map w ↦ αw + βw̄ with det = |α|² − |β|², which must pass through 0 between pure turns and pure mirrors.
  - In 2D the analogue of Markley's eigenvector rule is the weighted circular mean arg Σ w_k e^{iφ_k}. It is undefined when the resultant vanishes, e.g. two equal weights half a turn apart, the 2D version of Markley's equal-eigenvalue case.
  - Any averaging scheme that keeps the output rigid must either fail at such ties or break the symmetry arbitrarily (hysteresis, as the page does).
- **No free lunch.** A fit that is continuous in both triangles and always optimal cannot exist across a tie, since Berge gives only upper hemicontinuity. The soft fit buys continuity by being suboptimal, and non-rigid, inside a band around each mirror. Since w_g/(w_g + w_h) = 1/(1 + exp((rms_g² − rms_h²)/σ²)), the band's width in configuration space scales like σ² divided by the rate at which the two candidates' errors separate.

### Gaps
- I did not read Söderkvist 1993 or Li 1995 in full. The sharp form of the polar-factor bound, which I recall as ‖ΔQ‖ ≤ 2‖ΔA‖/(σ_{n−1} + σ_n) for real square A, is from memory and unverified.
- I found no paper that analyses specifically the 12-chamber (D₃h) structure of the triangle shape sphere as the jump locus of unlabelled, reflection-allowed Procrustes fitting. The statement above is my synthesis of Montgomery's geometry, standard reflection-group facts, and the numerical test, so it should be treated as derived, not cited.

## Q4. Reflections: det = −1 solutions, chirality of planar triangles, and when to allow the mirror fit

### Takeaway
Orthogonal Procrustes over O(m) may return a reflection. Restricting to SO(m) flips the sign of the singular direction with the smallest singular value: Kabsch 1978, Umeyama 1991, and C = diag(1, …, 1, det(UVᵀ)) in CPD. For planar triangles, chirality is the sign of the signed area, i.e. the hemisphere of Kendall's sphere. Allowing reflections quotients by the equatorial mirror (Montgomery: congruence classes are the half-space w₃ ≥ 0), and this adds the collinear equator to the jump locus. Allow the mirror only when handedness is not meaningful for the objects. For planar figures that can be flipped over in 3D, a mirror fit is a proper 3D motion, the half-turn that the page animates.

### Cited Findings
- Umeyama (1991): the SVD solutions of Arun et al. (1987) and Horn et al. (1987) "sometimes fail to give a correct rotation matrix and give a reflection instead when the data is severely corrupted". The proposed theorem "always gives the correct transformation parameters even when the data is corrupted". — [IEEE TPAMI abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.88573); [Arun et al. 1987](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.1987.4767965)
- CPD's Lemma 1 (citing Myronenko & Song's reference [35]): the rotation maximising tr(AᵀR) is R = UCVᵀ with C = d(1, 1, …, 1, det(UVᵀ)), which enforces det(R) = 1. — [CPD arXiv PDF §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2635)
- Kabsch's 1978 note is "A discussion of the solution for the best rotation to relate two sets of vectors". It is commonly cited for the improper-rotation case; record verified, text not seen. — [Acta Cryst. A 34:827–828](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0567739478001680)
- In Montgomery's coordinates, w₃ is the signed area and reflection acts as (w₁, w₂, w₃) ↦ (w₁, w₂, −w₃). "Oriented congruence exclude[s] reflections". Allowing reflections identifies the two hemispheres, with the collinear triangles (w₃ = 0) as the boundary. — [arXiv 1402.0841](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0841)
- The page implements the mirror candidate by conjugating u: "A mirror pairing replaces u by its conjugate ū before summing" ([index.html](../../../../index.html)). It notes that reversing vertex order (the GeoGebra approach) "changes which vertex goes where. The triangle's shape is unchanged, and so is its handedness. A genuine mirror image is fitted only by conjugating u". Every mirror fit z ↦ a·z̄ "is a single reflection about the line through the centroid at angle arg(a)/2", animated as a half-turn in space. — [docs/reflections.md §3](../../../../docs/reflections.md)
- In equivariant generative modelling of molecules, Klein, Krämer & Noé (2023) define the symmetry-aware cost as a minimum over "all possible combinations of rotations, reflections, and permutations", r ∈ O(D), s ∈ S(N). They approximate it by optimising the permutation first and then a rotation in SO(D). — [arXiv 2306.15030, eqs. 15–16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15030)
- IRA (Gunde et al. 2021) is "able to find rigid rotations, reflections, translations, and permutations between structures", so reflection is an explicit option in atomistic shape matching. — [arXiv 2111.00939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00939)

### Inferences
- **When to allow the mirror fit.**
  - Allow it when the objects are achiral by nature or by convention: a cut-out paper triangle that can be turned over, unoriented landmark sets, or an energy invariant under O(D), as for Klein et al.
  - Disallow it when chirality is data: enantiomers in chemistry, left and right anatomical structures, or oriented (cyclic-order) labelled data. The reflection-free choice is also the only one for which Kendall's oriented shape sphere is the right space.
  - For planar triangles, the reflected 2D fit equals a proper 3D rigid motion (a half-turn about an in-plane line). So "reflection allowed in 2D" is the same as "proper motions in 3D of a flat object", which is the page's animation.
- **Cost of allowing mirrors.** The equator (collinear targets) becomes an additional jump locus. There, the tie between a fit and its mirror is exact for every DEF, and the soft blend flattens the fit (Q3). Without mirrors, a near-collinear target is unremarkable.
- **Interaction with permutations.** For isosceles triangles, a transposition composed with a reflection is a symmetry. With mirrors allowed, isosceles targets become exact tie loci independent of DEF (Q3 numerics). Without mirrors they are not. So enabling reflections is what makes the jump locus "flat or isosceles".
- **The SO(m) restriction itself creates degeneracies.** The 2D condition (σ₁ = σ₂ with det H < 0) and the 3D condition (σ₂ = σ₃ with det H < 0) are where the det-corrected Kabsch rotation becomes non-unique. These cases arise only because reflections are forbidden; over O(m) the solution UVᵀ is unique whenever H is nonsingular.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the specific treatment of "reflection shape" and "reflection size-and-shape" in Dryden & Mardia 2016 this session. They are standard terms there, from memory.
- I did not check whether RDKit's `AlignMol` exposes a `reflect` flag. Its `GetBestRMS` and `CalcRMS` signatures, as documented, have none (see Q5).

## Q5. Molecular analogues: symmetry-corrected RMSD, Hungarian vs graph-automorphism mappings, cyclic/dihedral symmetry

### Takeaway
Chemistry faces the same "minimum over relabellings" problem for symmetry-equivalent atoms. The tools differ in how they enumerate the relabellings:
- **Hungarian assignment** per element (Allen & Rizzo 2014; ArbAlign 2017). It is polynomial but can break the bond graph.
- **Graph-automorphism enumeration** (DockRMSD 2019; spyrmsd 2020; RDKit `GetBestRMS`). It is exact for symmetry but can explode combinatorially.
- **Global branch-and-bound** (Go‑PERMDIST, Griffiths, Niblett & Wales 2017) and iterative rotations-and-assignments (IRA 2021).

Recent ML work (equivariant flow matching) uses Hungarian-then-Kabsch as an approximation to the joint minimum. The triangle page is exactly a symmetry-corrected RMSD for a 3-atom ring of identical atoms, whose automorphism group is S₃ ≅ D₃, with an optional mirror.

### Cited Findings
- Allen & Rizzo (2014, J. Chem. Inf. Model. 54:518–529) implement "a symmetry-corrected root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) method into the program DOCK based on the Hungarian algorithm … which dynamically assigns atom correspondence in molecules with symmetry". It adds "only a trivial amount of computation time" and raises reported docking success by about 5% over 1043 receptor–ligand systems, up to 16.7% for some families. — [PMC3958141](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3958141/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/ci400534h)
- spyrmsd (Meli & Biggin 2020, J. Cheminform. 12):
  - It computes "All possible graph isomorphisms … using the VF2 algorithm and the lowest RMSD among all isomorphisms is retained".
  - It provides a Hungarian `hrmsd` function only for comparison, warning that it "could result in unphysical assignments which break the molecular graph and result in artificially low RMSD values". In one example a ring oxygen is mapped to a nearby oxygen, giving 1.00 Å instead of the correct 2.46 Å.
  - QCP minimisation can be enabled "with and without symmetry-corrections".
  - Because graph isomorphism is hard, symmetry-corrected calculations "are only suited for small to medium sized molecules".

  — [PMC7457508](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7457508/)
- DockRMSD (Bell & Zhang 2019): several symmetry-correcting RMSD tools "do not take into account the bonding structure of molecules and can therefore result in non-physical atomic mapping". DockRMSD "converts the symmetry correction to a graph isomorphism searching problem" with an exhaustive search over isomorphisms. — [J. Cheminform. abstract (PMC6556049)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6556049/)
- RDKit `rdMolAlign.GetBestRMS` returns "the optimal RMS for aligning two molecules, taking symmetry into account". It "will attempt to align all permutations of matching atom orders in both molecules", taken from substructure matches (`maxMatches` default 1,000,000), and warns of "combinatorial explosion" especially with hydrogens. `symmetrizeConjugatedTerminalGroups` (default True) treats groups such as nitro or carboxylate symmetrically. — [RDKit docs](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.Chem.rdMolAlign.html)
- ArbAlign (Temelso et al. 2017): when atoms are ordered differently, "it is necessary to find the best ordering of the atoms and check for optimal axes before calculating a meaningful pairwise RMSD". The Kuhn–Munkres algorithm reduces the cost "from factorial to polynomial scaling". — [J. Chem. Inf. Model. abstract](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6b00546)
- Griffiths, Niblett & Wales (2017): "Most current algorithms for aligning structures are stochastic, scale exponentially … Go-PERMDIST, a branch and bound algorithm, locates the global minimum RMSD deterministically in polynomial time", for clusters and periodic supercells. — [J. Chem. Theory Comput. abstract](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.7b00543)
- IRA (Gunde et al. 2021) solves shape matching "when atomic assignments are not known a priori". It iterates rotated atom-centred frames and assignments, picks the frame minimising a permutation-invariant Hausdorff distance, then applies SVD for distorted structures, with its own constrained shortest-distance assignment algorithm. — [arXiv 2111.00939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00939)
- Equivariant flow matching (Klein, Krämer & Noé 2023): the exact minimum over rotations, reflections and permutations "is computationally infeasible in practice". They "first compute the optimal permutation with the Hungarian algorithm and then align the two samples through rotation with the Kabsch algorithm", noting that "the potential energy is invariant under permutations of atoms of the same chemical element". — [arXiv 2306.15030 §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15030)
- Fast RMSD kernels used inside these tools include QCP (Theobald 2005) and the quaternion method (Coutsias, Seok & Dill 2004). — [Theobald 2005](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0108767305015266); [Coutsias et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20110)

### Inferences
- **The page as a 3-atom symmetric RMSD.** Three identical atoms in a ring (e.g. a C₃ ring or H₃⁺) have bond-graph automorphism group S₃ ≅ D₃, of order 6. These are the page's 6 pairings: 3 cyclic, which are proper rotations of the labels, and 3 transpositions, which reverse the cyclic order. Adding the mirror gives the 12-element group of Q3. The page's hard fit is "symmetry-corrected RMSD with optional reflection". Its jumps are the same phenomenon that makes symmetric RMSD non-differentiable: a minimum over automorphisms is continuous, but its active branch switches.
- **Cyclic and dihedral symmetry in molecules.**
  - For a methyl group, the bond graph's automorphisms permute the three H's freely (S₃), but only the cyclic subgroup C₃ is realised by proper rotations of the rigid group.
  - For benzene, the ring graph has automorphism group D₆ (order 12), where again half the elements reverse the cyclic order.
  - Graph-automorphism enumeration therefore includes "orientation-reversing" relabellings. Combined with a proper Kabsch rotation, these act like the page's transposition candidates. This is why the jump structure for symmetric molecules resembles the triangle case, with ties at configurations where the geometric symmetry matches a graph automorphism.
- **Hungarian versus automorphism enumeration** is the "unconstrained versus constrained" choice. The Hungarian method searches all permutations within each element type (the full S_n, as in the triangle page, where all labels are equivalent). Graph-isomorphism tools search only bond-preserving permutations. For three identical, mutually bonded atoms the two coincide, since every permutation is an automorphism of the triangle graph.
- **Sequential approximations reintroduce ICP-like local optimality.** Klein et al. do Hungarian then Kabsch, and ArbAlign does axis checks then Kuhn–Munkres. The Hungarian step on unaligned coordinates depends on the initial orientation. ArbAlign handles this by checking axis swaps and reflections of principal axes, which are themselves ambiguous when inertia eigenvalues coincide. That is another symmetric-configuration instability, though I found no paper quantifying it.

### Gaps
- **"Allen & Castleman".** I found no symmetry-corrected-RMSD paper by these authors. The matching work is Allen, W. J. & Rizzo, R. C. (2014), J. Chem. Inf. Model. 54:518–529, which spyrmsd cites as the Hungarian approach. The brief's "Allen & Castleman" appears to be a mix-up.
- **Not covered.** I did not verify AlphaFold-style "symmetric atom renaming" and chain-permutation alignment in structure-prediction losses (a hard argmin over symmetric labellings inside a training loss).
- **Not quantified.** I found no source that quantifies how often symmetric-RMSD optimal mappings switch along MD trajectories (the molecular analogue of the page's jumps).
