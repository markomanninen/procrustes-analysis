# Labelling, covariance and the Christoffel connection in general relativity, and the link to Barbour's best matching

Scope: how coordinates serve as labels of points in GR, why relabelling forces the Christoffel connection and covariant derivatives, how the hole and point-coincidence arguments make labels gauge, general covariance versus diffeomorphism invariance, the ADM shift as the best-matching variable, superspace as Riem/Diff (the field-theory analogue of Kendall's shape space), large diffeomorphisms as the analogue of discrete relabellings, and the Levi-Civita connection set beside the best-matching connection. Shape-dynamics field equations (BFÓ 2002, GGK 2011) are covered in `shape_dynamics_janus_point.md` and are cited here only where the link needs them. The falling-cat holonomy for particles is covered in `shape_sphere_bridge.md`.

Notation: Greek indices are spacetime and Latin indices are spatial; $\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita covariant derivative; $\mathcal L_\xi$ is the Lie derivative; a semicolon or $|$ also denotes the covariant derivative, as in the quoted sources.

## Q0. The user's question: how has labelling been used in Einstein's theory, where Christoffel symbols are used in tensors, to form the basis of covariance?

### Takeaway
In GR, coordinates are only labels of manifold points, and any smooth relabelling $x\to x'(x)$ is allowed. Comparing quantities at *neighbouring* points then needs a compensating object, the Christoffel symbols $\Gamma$, whose inhomogeneous transformation cancels the label-dependence of ordinary derivatives. The hole and point-coincidence arguments go further: the labels carry no physical meaning at all, so relabellings are gauge. Barbour's best matching is the same move made along *time* instead of across space. Particles carry physical labels, so only the 6 (or 7) Euclidean/similarity parameters are best-matched. The points of space carry no labels, so best matching must also decide which point is "the same place" at the next instant. The quantity that does this is the vector field $\xi^i$, whose velocity plays the role of the ADM shift $N^i$.

### Cited Findings
- Einstein's general principle of relativity (1916, §3): "Laws of Nature are expressed by means of equations which are valid for all co-ordinate systems … which are covariant for all possible transformations." — [Einstein 1916, Bose transl., §3 (Wikisource)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- Einstein's justification is that only coincidences are observable: "All our well-substantiated space-time propositions amount to the determination of space-time coincidences", and general covariance "takes away the last remnants of physical objectivity from space and time" (§3). — [Einstein 1916, §3](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- Einstein's machinery is Christoffel's and Ricci–Levi-Civita's: the introduction credits "the researches of Gauss, Riemann and Christoffel on the non-Euclidean manifold". §5 says: "In accordance with Ricci and Levi-Civita, the contravariants and covariants are designated by the over and under indices". §9 introduces the three-index symbols with "Here we have put, following Christoffel". In §13 he calls the $\Gamma$'s (defined in eq. 45 as minus the Christoffel symbols of the second kind) "the components of the gravitational field". — [Einstein 1916](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- ADM state the labelling view directly. A general coordinate transformation of $g_{\mu\nu}$ "does not involve any observable changes in the physics, since it merely corresponds to a relabeling under which the theory is invariant". So "the coordinate invariance plays a role similar to the gauge invariance of the Maxwell field" (§1). — [Arnowitt, Deser & Misner 1962, arXiv:gr-qc/0405109](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0405109)
- Barbour makes the particle/GR analogy explicit. Many 3-metrics "express identical distance relationships on the manifold that are simply expressed by means of different coordinates, or labels … the 3-diffeomorphisms form a structure group that will play a role analogous to the Euclidean group in particle dynamics" (§4.1). — [Barbour 2012, "Shape Dynamics. An Introduction", arXiv:1105.0183](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Barbour on the GR correspondence problem: "because we are considering intrinsically different 3-geometries, mere identity of the coordinate values $x^i$ does not mean that they specify 'the same point' in the two different 3-geometries … Given an apple and a pear, there does not appear to be any way to establish a 1-to-1 pairing of all the points on the apple's surface with all those on the pear's. However, best matching does just that if the compared objects differ only infinitesimally" (§4.3). — [Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- "In best matching wrt 3-diffeomorphisms, we are in effect looking at all possible ways in which all points on one 3-geometry can be mapped bijectively to the points of an intrinsically different 3-geometry and selecting the bijection that extremalizes the quantity chosen to measure the incongruence of the two" (§4.4). — [Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- Particles versus geometry: "Whereas application of Machian principles to particles in Euclidean space leads to global conditions [vanishing total angular momentum and dilatational momentum] … in the theories with dynamical geometry local conditions are obtained … the equations $G_{0i}=0$ arise from best matching and are local counterparts of the condition (7) [zero total angular momentum]." — [Barbour 2010, "The definition of Mach's principle", arXiv:1007.3368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)

### Inferences
- The answer to the user's question can be stated in one line. *Covariance is the requirement that physics not depend on the labelling, and the Christoffel symbols are the price of comparing labelled quantities at different labels.* Best matching pays the same price for comparing configurations at different instants.
- The particle and GR cases differ in what the labels do. In Barbour's particle mechanics the labels $a=1,\dots,N$ are physical: they solve the correspondence problem in advance, and best matching only removes a finite-dimensional frame freedom. This is ordinary Procrustes with known correspondence. In GR the manifold labels are pure gauge (Q2), so best matching must *also* produce the point-to-point correspondence, a bijection parametrised by a whole vector field $\xi^i(x)$. That is closer to non-rigid (diffeomorphic) registration than to rigid Procrustes.

### Gaps
- I did not find a single sentence in which Barbour himself uses the word "labelling" for both the particle labels and the manifold coordinates in one comparison. The closest is Barbour 2012 §4.1 ("coordinates, or labels" and "analogous to the Euclidean group in particle dynamics").

## Q1. History: Christoffel 1869 → Ricci & Levi-Civita 1900 → Grossmann & Einstein 1913 → Einstein 1916 → Levi-Civita 1917

### Takeaway
The tools Einstein used to make physics independent of labelling came from nineteenth-century work on how quadratic differential forms change under a change of variables. Christoffel (1869) introduced them, Ricci and Levi-Civita (1900) systematised them as the "absolute differential calculus", and Grossmann brought them to Einstein (Entwurf, 1913). Einstein then built GR on them (1916) and read the Christoffel symbols as the gravitational field itself. Levi-Civita (1917) gave the geometric meaning, parallel transport, which ties curvature to holonomy.

### Cited Findings
- **Christoffel 1869.** E. B. Christoffel, "Ueber die Transformation der homogenen Differentialausdrücke zweiten Grades", *J. reine angew. Math.* 70 (1869) 46–70, DOI 10.1515/crll.1869.70.46 (the Crossref record has no author field, but title and pages match). The title is literally "On the transformation of homogeneous differential expressions of the second degree", i.e. of quadratic forms $g_{ij}dx^idx^j$ under change of variables (relabelling). — [Crossref/De Gruyter record](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1869.70.46)
- **Ricci & Levi-Civita 1900.** M. M. G. Ricci and T. Levi-Civita, "Méthodes de calcul différentiel absolu et leurs applications", *Math. Ann.* 54(1–2) (1900) 125–201, DOI 10.1007/BF01454201. — [Crossref/Springer record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01454201)
- **Grossmann & Einstein 1913 (Entwurf).** "Entwurf einer verallgemeinerten Relativitätstheorie und einer Theorie der Gravitation" (Einstein wrote the physical part, Grossmann the mathematical part). It was reprinted in *Z. Math. Phys.* 62 (1914), listed by Straumann as pp. 225–259, and appears as CPAE Vol. 4, Doc. 13. — [Straumann 2011, ref. 12, arXiv:1106.0900](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0900). An AMS review of the separatum appeared in Feb. 1914. — [Bull. AMS 20(5):273–274, DOI 10.1090/s0002-9904-1914-02486-3](https://doi.org/10.1090/s0002-9904-1914-02486-3)
- In the 1916 paper Einstein thanks Grossmann, "by whose help I was not only spared the study of the mathematical literature" (introduction), and attributes the three-index symbols to Christoffel (§9, eq. 23). — [Einstein 1916](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- **Einstein 1916.** "Die Grundlage der allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie", *Ann. Phys.* (4) 49 (= vol. 354 in Wiley's running numbering), no. 7, 769–822, DOI 10.1002/andp.19163540702. — [Crossref/Wiley record](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19163540702)
- In §13 (equation of motion of a point), after eq. (45) defines $\Gamma^\tau_{\mu\nu}=-\{\mu\nu,\tau\}$ (note the minus sign relative to the modern convention), Einstein writes: "They are the components of the gravitational field." — [Einstein 1916, Bose transl. §13](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- **Levi-Civita 1917.** T. Levi-Civita, "Nozione di parallelismo in una varietà qualunque e conseguente specificazione geometrica della curvatura riemanniana", *Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo* 42(1), 173–204 (Crossref pages; often cited as 173–205), issue dated Dec. 1916, usually cited as 1917, DOI 10.1007/BF03014898. The title, "Notion of parallelism in an arbitrary manifold and the consequent geometric specification of Riemannian curvature", states the result: parallel transport gives curvature its geometric meaning. — [Crossref/Springer record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03014898)
- Modern statement of that result: "parallel transport of a vector around a closed loop in a curved space will lead to a transformation of the vector. The resulting transformation depends on the total curvature enclosed by the loop". This transformation "is known as the 'holonomy' of the loop". — [Carroll, Lecture Notes on GR, ch. 3, arXiv:gr-qc/9712019](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)

### Inferences
- The chain runs: Christoffel's problem (when are two quadratic forms the same form in different variables?) → Ricci–Levi-Civita's calculus of objects whose equations keep their form under any change of variables → Einstein's demand that the laws keep their form under any relabelling. Christoffel's *equivalence problem* is the field-theory ancestor of asking when two configurations are "the same shape". Kendall's shape space and Wheeler's superspace (Q5) are both answers to it.

## Q2. Why are Christoffel symbols needed? Non-tensorial derivatives, the inhomogeneous transformation of Γ, normal coordinates and the equivalence principle, curvature as holonomy

### Takeaway
Under a relabelling $x\to x'$, the partial derivative of a vector picks up a term in $\partial^2x'/\partial x\,\partial x$, because the transformation matrix itself varies from point to point. $\Gamma$ is built to transform with exactly the opposite inhomogeneous term, so $\nabla_\mu V^\nu=\partial_\mu V^\nu+\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda$ is a tensor. Because $\Gamma$ is not a tensor, a choice of labels (Riemann normal coordinates) can make it vanish at any one point, and this is the equivalence principle. Curvature (the Riemann tensor, i.e. the holonomy of parallel transport around a loop) is a tensor, so no relabelling removes it.

### Cited Findings
- **Transformation law of Γ** (Carroll eq. 3.6):
  $$\Gamma^{\nu'}_{\mu'\lambda'}=\frac{\partial x^{\mu}}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^{\lambda}}{\partial x^{\lambda'}}\frac{\partial x^{\nu'}}{\partial x^{\nu}}\Gamma^{\nu}_{\mu\lambda}-\frac{\partial x^{\mu}}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^{\lambda}}{\partial x^{\lambda'}}\frac{\partial^2 x^{\nu'}}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial x^{\lambda}}.$$
  "This is not, of course, the tensor transformation law; the second term on the right spoils it. That's okay, because the connection coefficients are not the components of a tensor. They are purposefully constructed to be non-tensorial, but in such a way that the combination (3.1) transforms as a tensor — the extra terms in the transformation of the partials and the Γ's exactly cancel." — [Carroll, arXiv:gr-qc/9712019, ch. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
- The non-tensorial term in the partial derivative is
  $\partial_{\mu'}V^{\nu'}=\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial x^{\nu'}}{\partial x^\nu}\partial_\mu V^\nu+\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial x^{\mu'}}\frac{\partial^2x^{\nu'}}{\partial x^\mu\partial x^\nu}V^\nu$. The covariant derivative is (3.1) $\nabla_\mu V^\nu=\partial_\mu V^\nu+\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda$. — [Carroll ch. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
- **Christoffel (Levi-Civita) connection.** The unique torsion-free, metric-compatible connection is $\Gamma^\sigma_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12g^{\sigma\rho}(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho}+\partial_\nu g_{\rho\mu}-\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$, which is "sometimes the Christoffel connection, sometimes the Levi-Civita connection … sometimes called Christoffel symbols". — [Carroll ch. 3, eq. 3.21](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
- **Γ can be removed at a point by relabelling.** "at any point p there exists a coordinate system in which $g_{\mu\nu}$ takes its canonical form and the first derivatives $\partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu}$ all vanish (while the second derivatives $\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu}$ cannot be made to all vanish). Such coordinates are known as Riemann normal coordinates, and the associated basis vectors constitute a local Lorentz frame." Hence "even in a curved space it is still possible to make the Christoffel symbols vanish at any one point … Of course this can only be established at a point, not in some neighborhood of the point." — [Carroll ch. 2 and ch. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
- **Curvature cannot be removed, and it is holonomy.** For a flat connection, "parallel transport around a closed loop leaves a vector unchanged, … covariant derivatives of tensors commute". In curved space the loop gives a transformation that "depends on the total curvature enclosed by the loop". The Riemann tensor is the infinitesimal version: $\delta V^\rho\propto R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma A^\mu B^\nu$ for a loop spanned by $A,B$, and $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$. "If you know the holonomy of every possible loop, that turns out to be equivalent to knowing the metric." — [Carroll ch. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)
- In Einstein's own reading the $\Gamma$'s are the gravitational field (§13), so "the field can be transformed away at a point" is the coordinate form of the equivalence principle. — [Einstein 1916 §13](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- **Textbook loci** (standard; chapter-level, from memory): Wald 1984, *General Relativity*, ch. 3 (derivative operators, parallel transport, curvature) and App. E (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian formulations, ADM). Misner–Thorne–Wheeler 1973, *Gravitation*, chs. 8–11 (connection coefficients, parallel transport, Riemann curvature as loop holonomy) and ch. 21 (lapse, shift, ADM). Carroll 2004, *Spacetime and Geometry*, ch. 3. — not verified this session (see Gaps)

### Inferences
- The structure is a gauge-compensator pattern: a derivative that is not covariant because the frame varies (the $\partial^2x'$ term), plus a compensating field that shifts inhomogeneously (Γ), gives a covariant derivative. The compensator is locally pure gauge (zero at a point in a suitable gauge), and the invariant content is in its curvature (holonomy). Best matching has the same structure along the time direction (Q4, Q6).
- The analogue of Riemann normal coordinates in best matching is the "best-matched" or "distinguished" representation. There one chooses frames (or spatial coordinates) along a *curve* in configuration space so that the correction term vanishes, $\dot\xi=0$ or $N^i=0$ (zero shift). As with Fermi coordinates along a worldline, the compensator can be zeroed along a whole curve but not around a closed loop.

- Fermi normal coordinates (Γ = 0 along a geodesic; Manasse & Misner 1963) are from memory and were not checked.

## Q3. The hole argument (1913–14) and the point-coincidence argument (1915–16): labels of manifold points are gauge

### Takeaway
In 1913 Einstein argued that generally covariant field equations cannot be right. Relabelling points inside a matter-free "hole" gives a second solution that agrees outside, so the theory would be indeterministic. In late 1915 to early 1916 he dissolved the problem with the point-coincidence argument: only coincidences are physical, so the two solutions are the same physical world. Earman and Norton (1987) recast this as a dilemma for manifold substantivalism. The now-standard view, "Leibniz equivalence", says that diffeomorphism-related models represent the same physical situation, so manifold-point labels, and even the bare points themselves, are gauge.

### Cited Findings
- **Discovery and first publication.** Einstein found the argument in August 1913. It was first published in January 1914 in a supplement to the Z. Math. Phys. reprint of the Entwurf (Einstein 1914d in the Collected Papers), with a fuller treatment in a 1914 paper replying to Mie. — [summary of Princeton CPAE editorial notes via web search; the CPAE pages were not directly readable](https://einsteinpapers.press.princeton.edu/vol4-doc/319) (see Gaps)
- In a September 1913 lecture Einstein stated: "It is possible to demonstrate by a general argument that equations that completely determine the gravitational field cannot be generally covariant with respect to arbitrary substitutions" (CPAE Vol. 4, Doc. 16). — [Straumann 2011, arXiv:1106.0900](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0900)
- Straumann's modern restatement: take a hole $D$ with $T_{\mu\nu}=0$, apply a diffeomorphism $\varphi$ that is the identity outside $D$, and "Clearly, g and $\varphi^*g$ are different solutions of the field equations that agree outside D." "It took a long time until Einstein understood that this non-uniqueness is an expression of what we now call gauge invariance". On January 3, 1916 Einstein wrote to Besso: "Everything in the hole argument was correct up to the final conclusion". — [Straumann 2011, Ann. Phys. (Berlin) 523:488–500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0900)
- Point-coincidence argument in print (1916, §3): "All our well-substantiated space-time propositions amount to the determination of space-time coincidences." — [Einstein 1916](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_the_Generalised_Theory_of_Relativity)
- SEP (Norton, Pooley & Read; first published 1 Feb 1999, substantive revision 6 Jun 2023): the argument was "created by Albert Einstein late in 1913 as an act of desperation". Einstein "urges that the physical content of a theory is exhausted by the catalog of the spacetime coincidences it licenses" (citing Einstein 1916, p. 117 of the Dover translation). Earman and Norton (1987) "recast" it as one "that explicitly targets spacetime substantivalism". Stachel (1980) recognised "its highly non-trivial character". — [SEP, "The Hole Argument"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/)
- **Leibniz equivalence** (SEP): "If two distributions of fields are related by a smooth transformation, then they represent the same physical systems." Denying it makes the theory one that "fails to determine uniquely how the universe will evolve to the future". — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/)
- SEP distinguishes *passive* general covariance (arbitrary coordinates) from *active* (redistributing fields over the manifold while preserving solutionhood), and says the two are not equivalent. Its §10.3 treats the hole argument as a template for gauge freedoms, needing "some physical grounds" (verifiability, determinism) to declare a freedom gauge. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/)
- Earman & Norton, "What price spacetime substantivalism? The hole story", *BJPS* 38(4) (1987) 515–525, DOI 10.1093/bjps/38.4.515. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/38.4.515)
- Stachel's later review: "The Hole Argument and Some Physical and Philosophical Implications", *Living Rev. Relativ.* 17 (2014) 1, DOI 10.12942/lrr-2014-1. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2014-1)
- **Recent debate, 2018–2025.** Weatherall argues that isomorphic models are the same structure, so the argument dissolves ("Regarding the 'Hole Argument'", *BJPS* 69(2) 2018, 329–350). Pooley & Read reply that "mathematical structuralism" is insufficient (*BJPS* 76(1) 2025, 21–43; online 2021). Halvorson & Manchak argue that the unique isometry between the solutions blocks the argument ("Closing the Hole Argument", *BJPS* 76(2) 2025, 295–318; online 2021). Gomes & Butterfield give an updated counterpart-theoretic treatment (*J. Phys.: Conf. Ser.* 2533, 2023, 012002 and 012003). — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spacetime-holearg/); records: [Weatherall](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axw012), [Pooley & Read](https://doi.org/10.1086/718274), [Halvorson & Manchak](https://doi.org/10.1086/719193), [Gomes & Butterfield I](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/2533/1/012002), [II](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/2533/1/012003)
- ADM present the same point in canonical form: $g_{\mu\nu}$ "may be modified at any later time simply by carrying out a general coordinate transformation", which "merely corresponds to a relabeling", so "it is necessary that the metric field be separated into the parts carrying the true dynamical information and those parts characterizing the coordinate system". — [ADM 1962 §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0405109)

### Inferences
- In 3+1 language the hole freedom is exactly the freedom of the lapse and shift. Choosing $N^i(x,t)$ differently inside a region relabels the points of later slices differently, giving different $g_{ij}(x,t)$ that agree outside the region (with $N$ adding refoliation). The momentum constraint is the generator of the spatial part of that freedom. Best matching is a rule that *picks* one representative, the one with minimal incongruence. So best matching does not contradict Leibniz equivalence: it chooses a gauge, it does not make one physical.
- Particles contrast sharply. Barbour's particles carry labels that are *not* gauge (particle 1 at $t$ is particle 1 at $t+dt$), so the hole-type freedom is absent and only the finite Euclidean group is gauge. In GR, Leibniz equivalence says the manifold points have no transtemporal identity of their own, and equilocality must be *constructed*, which is Barbour's claim (Q4).
- Gomes's recent work (2023–2026; see sibling note `shape_dynamics_janus_point.md`) has moved toward the hole argument and reference frames. A link between best matching and "dressing" or field-space connections is a natural next step, but I did not verify any paper making it.

- The letter to Ehrenfest of 26 Dec 1915 (CPAE Vol. 8, Doc. 173) is, from memory, the first statement of the point-coincidence argument ("The physically real in the world of events … consists in spatiotemporal coincidences"). I could not retrieve its text, so it is unverified. The Besso letter of 3 Jan 1916 is verified via Straumann.

## Q4. General covariance vs. diffeomorphism invariance: Kretschmann 1917, Anderson's absolute objects, Norton 1993, Pooley; passive vs. active

### Takeaway
Kretschmann (1917) showed that any theory can be written in generally covariant form, so passive general covariance, the freedom to relabel, has no physical content by itself, and Einstein conceded the point (1918). The substantive content of GR is that it has *no absolute (background) objects* (J. L. Anderson 1964/1967): every field, including the metric, is dynamical, so active diffeomorphisms are a gauge symmetry of the solution space. Pooley (2017) argues that even "diffeomorphism invariance" can be had by background-dependent theories, and that coordinates in GR, as in SR, are mere labels. In best-matching terms, what matters is not that labels can be changed but that no fixed structure tells you which point is which across time.

### Cited Findings
- Kretschmann, E., "Über den physikalischen Sinn der Relativitätspostulate, A. Einsteins neue und seine ursprüngliche Relativitätstheorie", *Ann. Phys.* (4) 53 (= vol. 358), no. 16, 575–614 (cited as 1917; Crossref issue date Jan. 1918), DOI 10.1002/andp.19183581602. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19183581602)
- Einstein's reply: "Prinzipielles zur allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie", *Ann. Phys.* (4) 55 (= vol. 360), no. 4 (1918) 241–244, DOI 10.1002/andp.19183600402. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19183600402)
- Pooley: "that any theory can be given a generally covariant formulation — was put to Einstein by Kretschmann (1917). In his response, Einstein conceded the basic point (Einstein, 1918). He identified three principles … (a) the (general) principle of relativity; (b) the equivalence principle; and (c) Mach's principle." Relativity "had simply become the claim that the laws of nature are statements only about spatiotemporal coincidences". — [Pooley 2017, arXiv:1506.03512, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03512)
- Pooley's summary: "General covariance per se has no physical content: the essence of Kretschmann's objection to Einstein is that any sensible theory can be formulated in a generally covariant manner. It follows that GR does not differ from SR in virtue of having a generally covariant formulation. However, GR does differ from SR in lacking a non-covariant formulation." — [Pooley 2017, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03512)
- Einstein (1920, reply to Reichenbächer, quoted by Pooley): "the coordinate system is only a means of description and in itself has nothing to do with the objects to be described". Pooley comments: "Einstein's idea seems to be that coordinates should not have a function beyond the mere labelling of physical entities". — [Pooley 2017, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03512)
- Active vs. passive (Rovelli 2001, quoted by Pooley): "A field theory is formulated in [a] manner invariant under passive diffs (or change of co-ordinates), if we can change the coordinates of the manifold, re-express all the geometric quantities (dynamical and non-dynamical) in the new coordinates, and the form of the equations of motion does not change. A theory is invariant under active diffs, when a smooth displacement of the dynamical fields (the dynamical fields alone) over the manifold, sends solutions of the equations of motion into solutions of the equations of motion." Pooley also notes that "Norton … distinguishes 'active' and 'passive' general covariance" and that "Earman distinguishes merely 'formal' from 'substantive' general covariance". — [Pooley 2017, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03512)
- Anderson's absolute objects (as summarised by Pooley): "Anderson's formal definition of absolute objects does not characterise them directly. Instead he defines them in terms of conditions intended to determine when a subset of the dynamical variables of a theory constitute the components of the theory's absolute objects (Anderson, 1967, 83). Friedman (1983, 56–60) later advocated a coordinate-free characterisation". References: Anderson 1964, "Relativity principles and the role of coordinates in physics", in Chiu & Hoffmann (eds), *Gravitation and Relativity*, Benjamin, 175–194; Anderson 1967, *Principles of Relativity Physics*, Academic Press. — [Pooley 2017, §7 and bibliography](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03512)
- Norton, J. D., "General covariance and the foundations of general relativity: eight decades of dispute", *Rep. Prog. Phys.* 56(7) (1993) 791–858, DOI 10.1088/0034-4885/56/7/001. — [Crossref record (title field empty; title from Pooley's bibliography)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0034-4885/56/7/001); author's scan: [Pitt](https://sites.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/papers/decades.pdf)
- Barbour on what "gauge" should mean: "In my view the decisive thing is the fact that one starts with instantaneous spatial configurations on which a group of motions acts. It generates gauge transformations of the configurations. A dynamical theory of gauge type arises from this fact" (footnote 7). — [Barbour 2010, arXiv:1007.3368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)

### Inferences
- Barbour's position is a Machian version of the Anderson/no-background view, applied to *instants*. A fixed equilocality relation (absolute space) is exactly an absolute object in Anderson's sense, and best matching replaces it with a dynamically determined one. This lines up with the Kretschmann lesson: passive relabelling freedom is trivial, and the content lies in *not* having a fixed identification of points across time.
- Active/passive maps onto best matching like this. A λ-independent relabelling of $g_{ij}$ is the passive (trivial) part, handled by using covariant spatial derivatives. A λ-*dependent* relabelling is the non-trivial part, handled by the best-matching correction $-\mathcal L_\xi g_{ij}$ (BFÓ 2002; Q5).

- I did not read Kretschmann 1917, Anderson 1967 or Norton 1993 directly. Their content is quoted here via Pooley (2017) and SEP, which are secondary sources.

## Q5. ADM lapse and shift, extrinsic curvature, momentum constraint, and the shift as the best-matching variable that fixes equilocality

### Takeaway
ADM split $g_{\mu\nu}$ into $g_{ij}$, $N=(-{}^4g^{00})^{-1/2}$ and $N_i={}^4g_{0i}$, and showed that $N$ and $N_i$ are Lagrange multipliers whose variation gives the constraints, including $\mathcal R^i\equiv-2\pi^{ij}{}_{|j}=0$. The shift says how spatial labels are carried from one slice to the next. Barbour, Foster and Ó Murchadha replace the shift by an auxiliary vector $\xi^i$ whose velocity enters only through the best-matching correction $\partial_\lambda g_{ij}-\xi_{i;j}-\xi_{j;i}$, and extremise over it. Variation gives the ADM momentum constraint. Gryb states the equivalence outright: varying $\dot\xi$ is equivalent to varying the shift. Barbour describes the outcome as fixing which point is "the same point" (equilocality) on successive 3-geometries.

### Cited Findings
- **ADM definitions** (§3-2, eqs. 3.9–3.14): $g_{ij}\equiv{}^4g_{ij}$, $N\equiv(-{}^4g^{00})^{-1/2}$, $N_i\equiv{}^4g_{0i}$; ${}^4g_{00}=-(N^2-N_iN^i)$; $\sqrt{-{}^4g}=N\sqrt g$. The Lagrangian is $\mathcal L=-g_{ij}\partial_t\pi^{ij}-N\mathcal R^0-N_i\mathcal R^i+$ (divergence), with $\mathcal R^0\equiv-\sqrt g\,[{}^3R+g^{-1}(\tfrac12\pi^2-\pi^{ij}\pi_{ij})]$ and $\mathcal R^i\equiv-2\pi^{ij}{}_{|j}$, where "$|$ indicates the covariant derivative using this metric". "That the N and $N_i$ are truly Lagrange multipliers follows from the fact that they do not appear in the pq′ … part of L. Their variation yields the four constraint equations". — [ADM 1962, arXiv:gr-qc/0405109](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0405109)
- ADM's text does not use the words "lapse" or "shift" (a full-text search of the reprint finds no hits). They write only $N$ and $N_i$. — [ADM reprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0405109)
- **Geometric meaning of the shift.** "the coordinates on successive 3-geometries will be chosen arbitrarily, so that the point with coordinate x on hypersurface t+δt will not lie at the point at which the normal erected at point x on hypersurface t pierces hypersurface t+δt. There will be a lateral displacement of magnitude $\delta x^i=N^i\delta t$. The vector $N^i$ is called the shift. The lapse and shift encode the $g_{00}$ and $g_{0i}$ components respectively of the 4-metric: $g_{00}=N_iN^i-N^2$, $g_{0i}=N_i$" (§4.1). — [Barbour 2012, arXiv:1105.0183](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183). Mercati (App. on 3+1): "Starting from the point $(x_0,t+\delta t)$, one has to move 'horizontally', on the spatial hypersurface, by an amount $N^i(x_0,t+\delta t)$ to reach the point with coordinates $(x,t+\delta t)$". — [Mercati, Shape Dynamics Tutorial v2, arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **Extrinsic curvature and its covariant derivatives.** BFÓ: "$K_{ij}=-(1/2N)(\partial g_{ij}/\partial t-N_{i;j}-N_{j;i})$ is the extrinsic curvature" (their sign convention; many texts write $K_{ij}=\frac1{2N}(\dot g_{ij}-\nabla_iN_j-\nabla_jN_i)$). The semicolon is the covariant derivative built from the Christoffel symbols of $g_{ij}$. BSW "replaced $K_{ij}$ in the action by $k_{ij}=\partial g_{ij}/\partial t-N_{i;j}-N_{j;i}$". The shift "determines how the coordinates are laid down on the successive 3-geometries". — [Barbour, Foster & Ó Murchadha 2002, §1, arXiv:gr-qc/0012089](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089)
- **Why a correction is needed (relabelling along λ).** Under λ-dependent transformations $x^i\to x'^i(x^j,\lambda)$,
  $\frac{\partial g'_{ij}}{\partial\lambda}=\frac{\partial x^a}{\partial x'^i}\frac{\partial x^b}{\partial x'^j}\Big(\frac{\partial g_{ab}}{\partial\lambda}-\xi_{a;b}-\xi_{b;a}\Big)$ with $\xi^a=-\partial x^a/\partial\lambda$, "so the λ derivative does not transform as a 3-tensor. This is a general problem that arises whenever the 'points' of a configuration space have an internal (gauge) symmetry … In the present case, the 'gauge' corresponds to coordinate transformations on space. We overcome this problem by having the metric velocity always appear as part of the combination $k_{ab}=\partial g_{ab}/\partial\lambda-\xi_{a;b}-\xi_{b;a}$, where we regard $\xi^i$ as an arbitrary 3-vector field and minimize the action with respect to it … This process of adjusting for the gauge freedom is universal and is appropriately called best matching." Also: "$\xi^i$ is actually a velocity", and $\xi_{a;b}+\xi_{b;a}$ "is the Killing form of the vector $\xi^i$. It is also the Lie derivative of $g_{ij}$ along $\xi^i$." — [BFÓ 2002 §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089) (formula reconstructed from the PDF text extraction)
- **Momentum constraint from best matching.** "The variation of A w.r.t. ξ is the variation w.r.t. $k_{ij}$ (which is just $p^{ij}$), times the variation of $k_{ij}$ w.r.t. ξ, which is just the gradient of the variation of ξ. We integrate once and get the standard ADM diffeomorphism constraint, $p^{ij}{}_{;i}=0$. This result has nothing to do with the square root or any other structure of the action except the use of 'best matching' to implement diffeomorphism invariance." — [BFÓ 2002 §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089). Barbour 2012 §4.3: "constraints arise through the best matching wrt diffeomorphisms. This is implemented by variation of (25) with respect to $\xi_i$, treated as a Lagrange multiplier … $p^{ij}{}_{;j}=0$ (28). These linear constraints are closely analogous to the linear constraints (12) and (13) in particle dynamics [total momentum and angular momentum]." — [Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- **Labels and the finite best-matching distance.** "We take coordinates with the same range, so that we can identify points on the two manifolds with the same coordinate values … This is a distance on Riem. To get a distance on superspace, we make different coordinate transformations on each of the two manifolds … and, in the key step, find the minimum of Δ over all possible transformation pairs … we only need to make transformations on the second manifold" (§3, eqs. 3.4–3.6). — [BFÓ 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089)
- **Barbour links the shift and best matching through the same phrase, "how coordinates are laid down".** BFÓ: the shift "determines how the coordinates are laid down on the successive 3-geometries". Barbour 2012 §4.3: "$g'_{ij}=dg_{ij}/d\lambda$ mixes information about the intrinsic change of the described 3-geometry with arbitrary information about the way in which the coordinates are laid down as the 3-geometry changes. There is an equivalence class of velocities $\{g'_{ij}-\xi'_{(i;j)}\}$ that all represent the same intrinsic change. The task of best matching is to select a unique one among them that can be said to measure the true change … To fix the way the coordinates are then laid down, we consider the effect of λ-dependent diffeomorphisms". — [BFÓ 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089); [Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- **Explicit identification shift ↔ $\dot\xi$ (Gryb).** "Equivalence is achieved because the Mach variation of $\dot\xi$ is equivalent to the usual variation of the shift vector $N^i$" (footnote 14). Recovering ADM amounts to "replacing the lapse N with $\dot\tau_0$ and the shift $N_a$ with $\dot\xi_a$". In finite-dimensional models: "the lapse, N, and shift, $N^\alpha$, are just Lagrange multipliers … these Lagrange multipliers play the same role as the lapse and shift in general relativity. The Mach variation implies $\dot\omega^\alpha=\{\omega^\alpha,H_T\}=N^\alpha$". For translations, "The shift is the velocity of the center of mass". — [Gryb 2010, CQG 27:215018, arXiv:1003.1973](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)
- Mercati writes the best-matching differential as $Dg_{ij}=dg_{ij}+\mathcal L_{d\xi}g_{ij}=dg_{ij}+\nabla_id\xi_j+\nabla_jd\xi_i$ (before eq. 129). Elsewhere he calls $\xi^i$ "the shift vector" directly (spherically symmetric ansatz, eq. 434 context). — [Mercati, arXiv:1409.0105 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **Equilocality.** Barbour 2010: Newton "saw the need for the definition of what may be called equilocality: the identification of points that can be said to have the same place at different times." Also: "physically (as opposed to gauge related) different configurations do not come with equilocality markings or preassigned time differences between them". — [Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368). For particles: "If a body is at these paired positions at the two instants, one can say it is at the same place. The two positions are equilocal" (§3.4). — [Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183) (also quoted in sibling note `barbour_best_matching.md`). Mercati: best matching "allows one to establish a notion of equilocality – to say when two points are at the same position at different instants of time when only relational data are available". — [Mercati 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- Barbour on lapse and shift as "fitting together": "One is not supposed to think that the Riemannian 3-geometry on the leaves of a 3+1 foliation is more fundamental than the lapse and shift, which tell one how the 3-geometries on the leaves 'fit together'" (§4.3). — [Barbour 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0183)
- For the field-equation side (conformal best matching, CMC slicing and symmetry trading), see Barbour–Foster–Ó Murchadha 2002 and Gomes–Gryb–Koslowski 2011 (*CQG* 28:045005, DOI 10.1088/0264-9381/28/4/045005), which are covered in `shape_dynamics_janus_point.md`.

### Inferences
- The chain of identifications is: ADM shift $N^i$ (relabelling velocity between slices) = $\dot\xi^i$ (best-matching velocity) = the field that fixes equilocality. In ADM the shift is arbitrary (gauge) and its variation imposes $\nabla_j\pi^{ij}=0$. In best matching the *same* variation is read as "extremise the incongruence over all point-to-point identifications". The solution $\xi$ (given $g,\dot g$) is the best-matched shift, the field analogue of the optimal Procrustes rotation, and the point $x$ on slice $t$ is "the same place" as the point $x+\dot\xi\,dt$ on slice $t+dt$.
- In the particle case the analogue is $\dot\omega^\alpha=N^\alpha$ for the 6 Euclidean generators (Gryb). The best-matching conditions are 6 *global* constraints ($P=0$, $L=0$). In GR, $\xi^i(x)$ is a field, so the constraint is *local* (3 per point), exactly as Barbour 2010 says.
- The "same point" in GR is fixed only infinitesimally and depends on the path (Q6): chaining best-matched identifications around a closed loop of 3-geometries need not return the identity map.

### Gaps
- I found no sentence in Barbour 2010, Barbour 2012 or BFÓ 2002 that literally says "the shift vector *is* the best-matching variable". The explicit equivalence sentence is Gryb's (2010, fn. 14). Barbour's link is through the shared phrase "how the coordinates are laid down". Barbour 1994 (*CQG* 11:2853, "The timelessness of quantum gravity I") and *The End of Time* (1999) may contain a more explicit statement but were not searched.
- I did not verify who coined "lapse" and "shift". From memory, the terms are Wheeler's (c. 1964) and are used in MTW ch. 21.
- The thin-sandwich problem (existence and uniqueness of the best-matching $\xi$ for given $g,\dot g$) is the GR analogue of uniqueness of the Procrustes fit. It is noted in Barbour & Bertotti 1982 (see `barbour_best_matching.md`), but its current mathematical status (Bartnik–Fodor results etc.) was not researched here.

## Q6. Superspace = Riem/Diff as the analogue of Kendall's shape space; large diffeomorphisms (mapping class group) as the analogue of discrete relabellings

### Takeaway
Wheeler's superspace is the quotient $\mathcal S(\Sigma)=\mathrm{Riem}(\Sigma)/\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma)$: metrics modulo relabelling of points. It is the field-theory counterpart of Kendall's shape space (configurations modulo similarity, with particle labels kept). Giulini treats $\mathrm{Riem}\to\mathcal S_F$ as a principal fibre bundle whose vertical directions are $\mathcal L_\xi h$, with a horizontal (best-matching) subspace fixed by the DeWitt metric. Because Riem is contractible, $\pi_1$ of superspace equals the mapping class group $\pi_0\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma)$, the "large" relabellings not connected to the identity. These are not generated by the constraints, and they can give $\theta$-sectors and even statistics sectors that permute identical "prime" pieces of space. That is the GR analogue of permuting identical particle labels.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition.** "Superspace $\mathcal S(\Sigma)$ is the set of all Riemannian geometries on the 3-manifold Σ. Here 'geometries' means 'metrics up to diffeomorphisms' … $\mathcal S(\Sigma):=\mathrm{Riem}(\Sigma)/\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma)$" (eq. 1). — [Giulini 2009, "The superspace of geometrodynamics", GRG 41:785–815, arXiv:0902.3923](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- Barbour's version: "The configuration space with redundancy is called Riem … The space of gauge-invariant 3-geometries is obtained by quotienting by 3-diffeomorphisms and is called superspace: Superspace := Riem / 3-diffeomorphisms", with conformal superspace as a further quotient that "has precisely two (local) degrees of freedom per space point". — [Barbour 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3368)
- **Singularities, as at collinear/coincident points in shape space.** "$\mathcal S(\Sigma)$ … is not a manifold if Σ allows for metrics with nontrivial isometry groups … the quotient … has the structure of a stratified manifold with nested sets of strata ordered according to the dimension of the isometry groups". Giulini resolves this with frame-fixing diffeomorphisms: $\mathcal S_F(\Sigma):=\mathrm{Riem}(\Sigma)/\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma)$ (eq. 4). — [Giulini 2009 §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- **Bundle structure and vertical directions.** "The basic geometric idea is now to regard Riem(Σ) as principal fibre bundle with structure group $\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma)$ and quotient $\mathcal S_F(\Sigma)$" (eq. 6). Vertical vectors are $V_\xi(h)=-\mathcal L_\xi h$ (eq. 7), and the kernel of $\xi\mapsto V_\xi(h)$ "consists of the finite-dimensional subspace of Killing fields". — [Giulini 2009 §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- **Horizontal (best-matching) subspace.** "it is natural to try to define a bundle connection on Riem(Σ) by taking the horizontal subspace $\mathrm{Hor}^\lambda_h$ … to be the $G_\lambda$-orthogonal complement to $\mathrm{Vert}_h$". A tangent vector $k$ is orthogonal to all $\mathcal L_\xi h$ iff $(O_\lambda k)_a:=-\nabla^b(k_{ab}-\lambda h_{ab}h^{cd}k_{cd})=0$ (eq. 13). The horizontal projection $k\mapsto k-V_\xi$ requires solving $D_\lambda\xi:=(\delta d+2(1-\lambda)d\delta-2\,\mathrm{Ric})\xi=O_hk$. But "orthogonality does not imply transversality if the metric is indefinite, as for λ = 1 [the GR value]". — [Giulini 2009 §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- **Mapping class group.** Because Riem is contractible, $\pi_n(\mathrm{Riem}/\mathrm{Diff}_F)\cong\pi_{n-1}(\mathrm{Diff}_F)$ (eq. 36). For $n=1$ this gives $\pi_0\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma):=\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma)/\mathrm{Diff}^0_F(\Sigma)=:\mathrm{MCG}_F(\Sigma)$ (eq. 37), "the quotient group of components". — [Giulini 2009 §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- **Constraints only see the identity component.** The diffeomorphism constraint forces a canonical shift $\Theta=d\theta$ to annihilate vertical vectors, "so that θ is constant on each connected component of the $\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma)$ orbit … But unless the $\mathrm{Diff}_F(\Sigma)$ orbits in Riem(Σ) are connected, this does not mean that θ is the pull back of a function on Superspace … Hence there is an analogue of the Bohm-Aharonov-like ambiguity". — [Giulini 2009 §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- **Statistics sectors = permutations of identical pieces of space.** The quantum sectors "might, e.g., correspond to various statistics (in the presence of diffeomorphic primes)". For a connected sum of two identical primes, the "two 'statistics sectors' correspond to the irreducible representations of the permutation subgroup (here just given by the $\mathbb Z_2$ subgroup generated by E)". — [Giulini 2009 §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- **Spin ½ from large diffeomorphisms.** Friedman and Sorkin (1980) found that for "spinorial" 3-manifolds a $2\pi$ rotation is not in the identity component, so "pure (i.e. without matter) quantum gravity should already contain states with half-integer angular momenta". — [Giulini 2009 §5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923); [Friedman & Sorkin, PRL 44:1100 (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.44.1100). For a spin-statistics theorem for topological geons, see [Dowker & Sorkin, CQG 15:1153 (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/15/5/007).
- DeWitt's canonical theory, the configuration space of 3-geometries and the DeWitt supermetric: *Phys. Rev.* 160(5) (1967) 1113–1148, DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.160.1113 (erratum 171:1834, 1968, per Giulini's references). — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.160.1113)
- Kendall's shape space: D. G. Kendall, "Shape manifolds, Procrustean metrics, and complex projective spaces", *Bull. LMS* 16(2) (1984) 81–121, DOI 10.1112/blms/16.2.81. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/16.2.81)

### Inferences
- **Dictionary.**

  | Kendall / Barbour particles | Wheeler–DeWitt / Barbour geometrodynamics |
  |---|---|
  | Pre-shape space $\mathbb R^{3N}$ (labelled points) | Riem(Σ) (metrics on a labelled manifold) |
  | Similarity group Sim(3) (finite-dim.) | Diff(Σ) (×conformal for shape dynamics) |
  | Shape space $\mathbb R^{3N}/\mathrm{Sim}$ | Superspace Riem/Diff (conformal superspace) |
  | Singular strata: collinear/coincident configurations with stabilisers | Singular strata: metrics with Killing vectors |
  | Procrustes horizontality: $\sum m_a\, q_a\times\dot q_a=0$, $\sum m_a\dot q_a=0$ | DeWitt horizontality: $\nabla^b(k_{ab}-h_{ab}\,\mathrm{tr}k)=0$, i.e. momentum constraint |
  | Labels physical; permutation $S_N$ *not* quotiented (Barbour) | Point labels gauge; identity component quotiented by the constraints |
  | Discrete: reflections, label permutations (if particles identical) | Discrete: large diffeos, $\mathrm{MCG}_F(\Sigma)=\pi_1(\mathcal S_F)$ |
- For identical particles, quotienting by $S_N$ makes the configuration space multiply connected and gives boson/fermion (and anyon in 2D) sectors. That is the standard Leinaas–Myrheim picture (from memory, not verified here). Giulini's "statistics sectors" for diffeomorphic primes are the exact GR analogue, so large diffeomorphisms play the role of discrete relabellings.
- This matches the project's Procrustes finding. Continuous relabellings (rotations, small diffeos) are removed smoothly by infinitesimal best matching, a connection. Discrete ones (reflections, permutations, large diffeos) cannot be generated infinitesimally, and choosing among them is where the fit jumps. Compare the 12-candidate jump locus on the triangle shape sphere in `correspondence_soft_alignment.md` and `shape_sphere_bridge.md`.
- Giulini's horizontality equation $D_\lambda\xi=O_hk$ is the GR "best-matching equation": linear and elliptic for $\lambda<1/3$, but with possible non-uniqueness for GR's indefinite $\lambda=1$. It is the counterpart of solving for the optimal infinitesimal rotation via the inertia tensor in the particle case (Barbour–Bertotti), where uniqueness can fail when the inertia tensor degenerates.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Wheeler 1968 ("Superspace and the nature of quantum geometrodynamics", Battelle Rencontres) or Fischer 1970 ("The theory of superspace"). Coinage and page data are from memory.
- I did not find a paper that explicitly calls superspace "the GR analogue of Kendall's shape space". E. Anderson's papers (cited in sibling notes) are the likeliest place, but this was not verified here.
- Whether physical states should be invariant under large diffeomorphisms (θ-sectors) is an open interpretive choice in canonical quantum gravity; Giulini presents it as an ambiguity.

## Q7. Connections as gauge fields: the Levi-Civita connection vs. the "mechanical"/best-matching connection on configuration space

### Takeaway
The two connections play parallel roles at different levels. The Levi-Civita connection lives on the tangent/frame bundle of space(time). It removes the dependence on coordinate labels when vectors at neighbouring *points* are compared, and its curvature is the holonomy of loops in space. The best-matching (mechanical) connection lives on the configuration-space bundle $Q\to Q/G$ (Riem → superspace, or $\mathbb R^{3N}$ → shape space). It removes the dependence on frame or point-labels when *configurations at neighbouring instants* are compared, and its curvature is the holonomy of loops in shape space or superspace (the falling cat). Gryb states explicitly that the best-matching correction is "the covariant derivative … with connection $\dot\omega$", the pull-back of a principal-bundle connection, and that the corrected coordinates come from an "open-path holonomy".

### Cited Findings
- Gryb: "$D_\omega q^a=\dot q^a+\dot\omega^\alpha t_\alpha{}^a{}_b q^b$ is the covariant derivative of q with connection $\dot\omega$ along a trial curve γ in A. Indeed, it can be thought of as the pullback onto γ of a connection on the principal G-bundle A over R" [R = reduced configuration space]. — [Gryb 2010, arXiv:1003.1973, eq. 12](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)
- Gryb: "the corrected coordinates are obtained by subtracting the action of the open–path holonomy of the lapse [sic; the path-ordered exponential of $N^\alpha$] (thought of as the pullback of the connection over A onto the classical path) on the $q^b$'s. This subtracts all vertical motion of the q's along the fiber bundle", with $\bar q^a=P\exp\{\int N^\alpha t_\alpha d\lambda\}^a{}_b q^b$ (eq. 43). — [Gryb 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)
- For gravity: best matching under $\bar g_{ab}=\exp\{\mathcal L_\xi\}g_{ab}$ is "equivalent to introducing the gauge covariant derivative $D_\xi g_{ab}=\dot g_{ab}+\mathcal L_{\dot\xi}g_{ab}=\dot g_{ab}+\dot\xi_{(a;b)}$ … semi-colons represent covariant differentiation on the tangent bundle of Σ using a metric compatible connection" (eqs. 65–66). — [Gryb 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)
- Gryb's Jacobi geodesics on configuration space use the Levi-Civita connection of the kinetic metric, "$\Gamma^a_{bc}=\tfrac12g^{ad}(g_{db,c}+g_{dc,b}-g_{bc,d})$ is the Levi-Civita connection", alongside the gauge connection $\dot\omega$. So both kinds of connection appear in one formalism. — [Gryb 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1973)
- Earlier gauge formulation: Gryb, "Implementing Mach's principle using gauge theory", *Phys. Rev. D* 80 (2009) 024018, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.80.024018, arXiv:0901.2362. — [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.2362)
- Littlejohn & Reinsch, "Gauge fields in the separation of rotations and internal motions in the n-body problem", *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 69(1) (1997) 213–276, DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.69.213. This is the standard treatment of the rotational (mechanical) connection on $n$-body configuration space, whose curvature gives the falling-cat holonomy. Details are in sibling note `shape_sphere_bridge.md`. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.69.213)
- Giulini's horizontal subspace on Riem (Q6) is the field-theory version of the mechanical connection: horizontal = $G_\lambda$-orthogonal to the orbit directions $\mathcal L_\xi h$. — [Giulini 2009 §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3923)
- Holonomy of the Levi-Civita connection measures curvature: "If you know the holonomy of every possible loop, that turns out to be equivalent to knowing the metric". — [Carroll ch. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9712019)

### Inferences
- **Side-by-side.**

  | | Levi-Civita / Christoffel (Einstein) | Best-matching connection (Barbour) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Compared objects | vectors at $x$ and $x+dx$ | configurations at $\lambda$ and $\lambda+d\lambda$ |
  | Label freedom | $x\to x'(x)$ | particles: frame $g(\lambda)\in\mathrm{Euc}(3)$; GR: $x\to x'(x,\lambda)$ |
  | Why the naive derivative fails | $\partial_\mu V^\nu$ gains a $\partial^2x'/\partial x\partial x$ term | $\partial_\lambda g_{ij}$ gains $-\xi_{(i;j)}$; $\dot q$ gains $\dot\omega\times q$ |
  | Compensator | $\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}$ (inhomogeneous) | $\dot\omega^\alpha$ or $\dot\xi^i$ (= shift $N^i$), shifting by $\partial x/\partial\lambda$ |
  | Corrected derivative | $\nabla_\mu V^\nu=\partial_\mu V^\nu+\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda$ | $D g_{ij}=\dot g_{ij}-\mathcal L_{\dot\xi}g_{ij}$ (sign by convention) |
  | How the compensator is fixed | kinematically: metric compatibility + no torsion ⇒ Christoffel formula | dynamically: extremise action over $\xi$ ⇒ momentum constraint (horizontality) |
  | Locally removable? | $\Gamma(p)=0$ in normal coordinates (equivalence principle) | $\dot\xi=0$ along a curve in the best-matched gauge (distinguished frame) |
  | Invariant obstruction | Riemann curvature = holonomy around loops in space(time) | bundle curvature = holonomy around loops in shape space/superspace (falling cat) |
  | Bundle | frame bundle $GL(n)$/$SO(n)$ over the manifold | $\mathrm{Diff}_F\to\mathrm{Riem}\to$ superspace; $\mathrm{Euc}(3)\to\mathbb R^{3N}\to$ shape space |
- One difference matters. The Levi-Civita connection is fixed by the metric alone (no extra variational principle). The best-matching connection is induced by the kinetic (DeWitt or mass) metric through orthogonality to the gauge orbits, and its value along a path is fixed by extremising the action, i.e. by solving the constraint. Einstein's $\Gamma$ is "the gravitational field". Barbour's $\dot\xi$ is "the inertial-frame/equilocality field". Both are what local inertial structure looks like once the background has been removed.
- Einstein's reading of Γ as the field and Barbour's reading of best matching as what defines inertial frames are the same idea at two levels. In both, "inertia" is a connection, gauge-dependent pointwise and invariant only through its holonomy.
