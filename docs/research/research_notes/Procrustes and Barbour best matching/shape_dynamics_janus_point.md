## Q1. Field theory: how does best matching under 3-diffeomorphisms give the momentum constraint, and how does conformal best matching connect to York's CMC method?

### Takeaway
Best matching of 3-geometries means correcting the metric velocity by a Lie derivative, $Dg_{ij}=dg_{ij}+\mathcal L_{d\xi}g_{ij}$, and then varying the auxiliary vector $d\xi$. That variation gives exactly the ADM momentum (diffeomorphism) constraint $\nabla_j p^{ij}\approx0$. The *local* square root of the Baierlein–Sharp–Wheeler (BSW)-type action gives the Hamiltonian constraint, and only the GR action propagates it consistently (Barbour, Foster & Ó Murchadha 2002). Adding best matching with respect to volume-preserving conformal transformations gives the conformal constraint $p-\langle p\rangle\sqrt g\approx0$, i.e. a constant-mean-curvature (CMC) slicing. With it come all of York's equations for the conformal initial-value problem (Anderson, Barbour, Foster, Kelleher & Ó Murchadha 2005). On this reading York's conformal degrees of freedom follow from a symmetry principle, not from a gauge choice.

### Cited Findings
- **Barbour & Bertotti 1982 (background).** Best matching originates in "Mach's principle and the structure of dynamical theories", *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* 382 (1783), 295–306 (1982), DOI 10.1098/rspa.1982.0102. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1982.0102)
- **BFÓ 2002 ("Relativity without relativity", arXiv Dec 2000; CQG 19, 3217–3248, 2002).** The authors derive GR and the gauge principle "presupposing neither spacetime nor the relativity principle". 3-coordinate invariance is "the only postulated symmetry, and it leads to the standard momentum constraint". The Lagrangian uses a "'local' square root of an expression quadratic in the velocities". This produces quadratic constraints that are in general not propagated, so such actions are "internally inconsistent". "Only one action of this form is well behaved: the Baierlein-Sharp-Wheeler reparametrisation-invariant action for GR". On this account spacetime symmetry is "emergent", a "'hidden' symmetry". — [arXiv:gr-qc/0012089](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089)
- **BFÓ 2002, matter couplings.** Constraint propagation also acts as a selection mechanism. A scalar field "must have the same characteristic speed as gravity. Thus Einstein causality emerges". Any 3-vector field must satisfy Einstein causality, the equivalence principle and the Gauss constraint, which recovers massless Maxwell theory. — [arXiv:gr-qc/0012089](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0012089)
- **The mechanism in equations (Mercati's tutorial, §A.3/§7 on the BSW action).** The best-matching differential is $Dg_{ij}=dg_{ij}+\mathcal L_{d\xi}g_{ij}=dg_{ij}+\nabla_i d\xi_j+\nabla_j d\xi_i$. The local "differential of the instant" is
  $$d\chi=\sqrt{\tfrac12\,(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})\,Dg_{ij}Dg_{kl}\,/\,(R-2\Lambda)},$$
  with momenta $p^{ij}=\frac{\sqrt g}{2\,d\chi}(g^{ik}g^{jl}-g^{ij}g^{kl})Dg_{kl}$. Because of the local square root, these satisfy at each point the primary constraint $\mathcal H=\frac{1}{\sqrt g}\big(p^{ij}p_{ij}-\tfrac12p^2\big)-\sqrt g\,(R-2\Lambda)\approx0$. "The best-matching condition gives the diffeomorphism constraint" ($\nabla_jp^{ij}\approx0$). Mercati notes that putting the integral inside the square root, as in the particle Jacobi action, "would spoil the locality", and that "this requirement of locality of the square root has very far-reaching consequences". — [Mercati, A Shape Dynamics Tutorial, arXiv:1409.0105 (v2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **ABFÓ 2003 ("Scale-invariant gravity: geometrodynamics", CQG 20, 1571, 2003).** This paper builds "conformal gravity" by "extension of the method of best matching w.r.t scaling", without Weyl's compensating field. In spatially compact GR, infinitely many shape degrees of freedom interact with "a single volume degree of freedom". In conformal gravity "the volume is no longer a dynamical variable". The authors note that its "cosmology and quantization will be completely different" from GR's. — [arXiv:gr-qc/0211022](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211022). The companion particle paper is Barbour, "Scale-invariant gravity: particle dynamics", CQG 20, 1543–1570 (2003). — [arXiv:gr-qc/0211021](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0211021)
- **Why a global volume is retained (tutorial).** Once $\nabla_jp^{ij}\approx0$ is imposed, full conformal best matching "introduces a new constraint $p\approx$ const", which the evolution does not propagate. The working theory therefore uses *volume-preserving* conformal transformations (VPCT), with the conformal constraint $C=p-\langle p\rangle\sqrt g\approx0$. This is first-class with the diffeomorphism constraint. — [arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **ABFKÓ 2005 ("The physical gravitational degrees of freedom", CQG 22, 1795–1802, 2005).** The authors derive GR (compact, without boundary) "as a theory of evolving 3-dimensional conformal Riemannian geometries" from two principles: "1) time is derived from change; 2) motion and size are relative". They obtain "GR in the CMC gauge, in its Hamiltonian 3+1 reformulation" and "all the equations used in York's conformal technique for solving the initial-value problem". They conclude that York's independent degrees of freedom "do not arise from a gauge fixing but from hitherto unrecognized fundamental symmetry principles". — [arXiv:gr-qc/0407104](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0407104)
- **York's method in brief (tutorial §8).** The extrinsic curvature splits as $K^{ij}=K^{ij}_{TT}+\tfrac13\tau g^{ij}$ with $\tau$ spatially constant. $\tau$ "grows monotonically whenever York's method can be applied, and can be used as a time parameter… referred to as the York time". Under conformal rescaling, $K^{ij}=\phi^{-10}\bar K^{ij}_{TT}+\tfrac13\phi^{-4}\tau\bar g^{ij}$. The diffeomorphism constraint then decouples, and the Hamiltonian constraint becomes the Lichnerowicz–York equation for $\phi$, tutorial eq. (197). In the standard normalisation this reads $8\bar\Delta\phi-\bar R\phi+\bar A_{ij}\bar A^{ij}\phi^{-7}-\tfrac23\tau^2\phi^5=0$. It has a unique solution on compact or asymptotically flat manifolds "regardless of the conformal class". The caveat: "York's method can only be applied to CMC-foliable spacetimes". — [arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)

### Inferences
- The structure is Procrustes-like. In both the particle case (Barbour–Bertotti) and the field case, the action is minimised over a group orbit: Euclidean motions (and scale) for particles, $\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma)$ and then VPCT for metrics. The resulting stationarity conditions are linear constraints on the momenta: $\mathbf L=0$, $\mathbf P=0$ ($D=0$ if scale is best-matched) for particles, and $\nabla_jp^{ij}=0$ ($p-\langle p\rangle\sqrt g=0$) for fields.
- The "relativity without relativity" result is a uniqueness claim about local-square-root actions on superspace. The light-cone universality and the Gauss constraint are consequences of constraint propagation, not postulates.

### Gaps
- I did not reread the full text of BFÓ 2002 or ABFKÓ 2005. The equations above are taken from Mercati's tutorial, which reproduces them, and from the abstracts. The exact equation numbering in the CQG papers is not checked.
- The tutorial's definition of $\langle p\rangle$ was garbled in extraction. I give the standard reading (the volume average of $p=g_{ij}p^{ij}$), which should be checked against Gomes–Gryb–Koslowski 2011.

## Q2. Shape dynamics proper: what is the duality with GR, and where do the two theories differ (black holes, singularities)?

### Takeaway
Shape dynamics (SD), in the form of Gomes, Gryb & Koslowski (2011), trades GR's refoliation invariance (the local Hamiltonian constraint) for local spatial (volume-preserving) conformal invariance. A larger "Linking Theory" contains both. Gauge-fixing it one way gives GR in CMC gauge; the other way gives SD with a single global Hamiltonian. The two theories agree wherever spacetime is CMC-foliable, and they can differ elsewhere. Gomes's Birkhoff theorem gives an isotropic **wormhole** with no singularity instead of maximally extended Schwarzschild. Rotating (Gomes–Herczeg) and other solutions (Herczeg) show "parity horizons". Mercati's later analysis questions whether these asymptotically flat solutions are physically realised in a closed SD universe.

### Cited Findings
- **GGK 2011 (arXiv Oct 2010; CQG 28, 045005, 2011).** "Gravity admits a dual description in terms of a theory where local size is irrelevant." The dual theory is invariant under "foliation preserving 3-diffeomorphisms and 3D conformal transformations that preserve the 3-volume". "Locally, this symmetry is identical to that of Horava-Lifshitz gravity in the high energy limit but our theory is equivalent to Einstein gravity." GR solutions "in a gauge where the spatial hypersurfaces have constant mean extrinsic curvature, can be mapped to solutions of a particular gauge fixing of the dual theory". The authors give "a general geometric picture… that allows us to trade foliation invariance for conformal invariance". — [arXiv:1010.2481](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.2481)
- **Symmetry trading, phase-space picture (Mercati tutorial).** The Hamiltonian and conformal constraints are "good gauge-fixings for each other and are both first-class with respect to the diffeomorphism constraint". The Hamiltonian constraint "is completely gauge-fixed by the conformal constraint except for a single residual global constraint". That global constraint generates time evolution. It is $H_{\rm global}=\int d^3x\,N_{\rm CMC}(x)\,\mathcal H(x)$, where $N_{\rm CMC}$ solves an elliptic (lapse-fixing) equation. — [arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **Linking Theory (tutorial §12, eq. 231).** Phase space is extended by a conformal factor $\phi$ and its momentum $\pi$, with constraints including $Q=\pi-4(p-\langle p\rangle\sqrt g)\approx0$. "This theory is called the Linking Theory, and it leads to SD as the following gauge-fixing: $\pi\approx0$… A different gauge-fixing, namely $\phi\approx0$, gives instead a first-class system (General Relativity)." — [arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **FAQ (Gomes & Koslowski, arXiv Nov 2012; Found. Phys. 43(12), 1428–1458, 2013).** "Barbour's interpretation of Mach's principle led him to postulate that gravity should be formulated as a dynamical theory of spatial conformal geometry." New SD, "unlike earlier proposals by Barbour and his collaborators, implements local spatial conformal invariance as a gauge symmetry that replaces refoliation invariance". The paper covers Poincaré invariance, CMC gauge, earlier SD and York's method. It uses free electrodynamics and "its dual shift symmetric formulation" as a toy model of symmetry trading. — [arXiv:1211.5878](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5878)
- **Problem of time (BKM 2014, CQG 31, 155001).** SD "trades spacetime refoliation invariance for three-dimensional conformal invariance". Its "logical completion… gives a dimensionless description of gravitational dynamics", in which "the classical problem of time is completely solved". — [arXiv:1302.6264](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6264); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/31/15/155001)
- **Black holes: Gomes's Birkhoff theorem (arXiv May 2013; CQG 31, 085008, 2014).** "There are solutions of Shape Dynamics that have no counterpart in General relativity, just as there are solutions of GR that are not completely foliable by global constant mean curvature slicings (such as the Schwarzschild spacetime)." For a spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat ansatz, "the solution obtained is uniquely the isotropic wormhole solution, in which no singularity is present, as opposed to maximally extended Schwarzschild. This provides an explicit example of the breaking of the duality between General relativity and Shape Dynamics." — [arXiv:1305.0310](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0310)
- **Rotating case (Gomes & Herczeg, CQG 31, 175014, 2014).** The paper gives a procedure for "(locally) mapping stationary, axisymmetric general relativity solutions onto their shape dynamic counterparts". The rotating SD black hole "is also free of physical singularities, it does not form a space-time at the horizon, and it possesses an inversion symmetry about the horizon which leads us to interpret the solution as a wormhole". — [arXiv:1310.6095](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.6095)
- **Parity horizons (Herczeg, CQG 33, 225002, 2016).** "The event horizons of the known asymptotically flat black hole solutions of shape dynamics are parity horizons". Observer-dependent horizons and Cauchy horizons become parity horizons too, which "suggests a general chronology protection mechanism in shape dynamics that prevents the formation of closed time-like curves". — [arXiv:1508.06704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06704)
- **Caveats from within the programme (Mercati tutorial v2).** In SD, spherical symmetry gives "a one-parameter family of solutions which depend on the expansion of matter at infinity. Only one of those solutions is Lorentz-invariant at the boundary, and it coincides with the 'wormhole' found by Gomes". Outside a parameter interval the others "do not possess a 'throat' anymore". "Noncompact manifolds cannot be proper solutions of SD: they can at most be approximations". For thin-shell collapse in a closed universe, "there is no evidence that the solution [Gomes's wormhole] forms". Still, "the ADM description in a CMC foliation breaks down at some point during the gravitational collapse… but the shape degrees of freedom seem unaffected by this breakdown, and can be continued smoothly". — [arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105)
- **Singularity continuation as a further GR/SD difference (tutorial summary of Budd–Koslowski and KMS).** "At the singularity it is only the spatial volume and its conjugate momentum, the York time, which are singular… the shape degrees of freedom are not singular." Continuity of shape variables joins two GR solutions, so "each half is an acceptable solution of GR… but the whole solution cannot" be described as a spacetime. — [arXiv:1409.0105](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0105). See also Q5 (Koslowski–Mercati–Sloan 2018).
- **Related SD results (records only).** Gomes, "Poincaré invariance and asymptotic flatness in Shape Dynamics", PRD 88, 024047 (2013) — [arXiv:1212.1755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1755). Gomes, Gryb, Koslowski & Mercati, "The gravity/CFT correspondence", EPJC 73, 2275 (2013) — [arXiv:1105.0938](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0938) (Crossref gives vol. 73; the arXiv journal-ref says "75", which is wrong). Gomes, Gryb, Koslowski, Mercati & Smolin, "A shape dynamical approach to holographic renormalization", EPJC 75, 3 (2015) — [arXiv:1305.6315](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.6315). Koslowski, "Shape dynamics and effective field theory", IJMPA 28, 1330017 (2013) — [arXiv:1305.1487](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.1487). Barbour, Lostaglio & Mercati, "Scale anomaly as the origin of time", GRG 45, 911–938 (2013) — [arXiv:1301.6173](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.6173).
- **Book (2018).** Mercati, *Shape Dynamics* (Oxford University Press, 2018; online 19 Apr 2018), ISBN 9780198789475, DOI 10.1093/oso/9780198789475.001.0001. — [Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198789475.001.0001). The subtitle "Relativity and Relationalism" is from memory; Crossref lists only "Shape Dynamics".

### Inferences
- The SD–GR "duality" is exact only on the overlap of CMC-foliable GR solutions with SD solutions. The differences concentrate where CMC slicing fails or where the volume/York-time pair degenerates: black-hole interiors and big-bang/crunch singularities.
- Whether SD's black-hole "wormholes" are physical is open within the programme itself. Mercati's own collapse model found no evidence that they form in a closed universe.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 paper by Gomes or Herczeg continuing SD black holes. Gomes's recent arXiv listing (2023–2026) is on gauge theory, the hole argument and reference frames. Whether SD black-hole work continues elsewhere (e.g. under the PSD label) is unknown.
- Quantum SD: no definitive quantisation was found in the sources checked. The tutorial says work "has been (mostly) limited to the classical theory".

## Q3. N-body results: exact definitions ($\ell_{\rm rms}$, $\ell_{\rm mhl}$, $C_S$), Lagrange–Jacobi, dilatational momentum $D$, Janus point, and the 3-body shape sphere

### Takeaway
For the Newtonian N-body problem with $E=0$ (more generally $E\ge0$) and $\mathbf P=\mathbf L=0$, the Lagrange–Jacobi identity $\ddot I_{\rm cm}=4E-2V_{\rm New}>0$ makes the dilatational momentum $D=\tfrac12\dot I_{\rm cm}$ strictly monotonic. Hence $I_{\rm cm}$ is U-shaped with a unique minimum where $D=0$: the **Janus point**. The scale-invariant shape complexity $C_S=\ell_{\rm rms}/\ell_{\rm mhl}=-V_{\rm New}\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}}/m_{\rm tot}^{5/2}$ is also, up to sign, the **shape potential** that drives the dynamics on shape space. After scale is removed, scale acts as **friction**, so $C_S$ grows secularly, with fluctuations between rising bounds, in both directions from the Janus point ("one past, two futures"). On the 3-body shape sphere, $C_S$ is minimal (the shape potential is maximal) at the **equilateral triangle** (Lagrange) for all masses. The collinear **Euler** configurations are saddles. The three **binary collisions** on the equator are infinitely deep wells. The central configurations are exactly the stationary points.

### Cited Findings
**Definitions (PRL 2014 and the 2016 reply, verbatim structure):**
- Energy and potential: $E_{\rm tot}=\sum_a \frac{\mathbf p_a\cdot\mathbf p_a}{2m_a}+V_{\rm New}$, $V_{\rm New}=-\sum_{a<b}\frac{m_am_b}{r_{ab}}$ (PRL eq. 1). — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Dynamical similarity: for a potential homogeneous of degree $k$, $\mathbf r_a\to\alpha\mathbf r_a$, $t\to\alpha^{1-k/2}t$ maps solutions to solutions (PRL eq. 2); for $k=-1$ this gives Kepler's third law. The reply states it for Newton gravity as $t\to\lambda t$, $\mathbf r_i\to\lambda^{2/3}\mathbf r_i$, $\mathbf p_i\to\lambda^{-1/3}\mathbf p_i$. Such solutions "coincide when projected to unparametrized curves in shape space". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917); [arXiv:1604.03956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03956)
- Centre-of-mass moment of inertia: $I_{\rm cm}=\sum_a m_a\|\mathbf r_a-\mathbf r_{\rm cm}\|^2=\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}}\sum_{a<b}m_am_br_{ab}^2$ (PRL eq. 3). The reply writes this as $I_{\rm cm}=m_{\rm tot}\,\ell_{\rm rms}^2$ (reply eq. 2). — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917); [arXiv:1604.03956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03956)
- Root-mean-square length: $\ell_{\rm rms}:=\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}}\sqrt{\sum_{a<b}m_am_br_{ab}^2}=\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}/m_{\rm tot}}$ (PRL eq. 8). It "is dominated by the largest $r_{ab}$ and measures the overall size of the system". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Mean harmonic length: $\frac{1}{\ell_{\rm mhl}}:=\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}^2}\sum_{a<b}\frac{m_am_b}{r_{ab}}=\frac{|V_{\rm New}|}{m_{\rm tot}^2}$ (PRL eq. 9; reply eq. 3: $-V_{\rm New}=m_{\rm tot}^2\ell_{\rm mhl}^{-1}$). It is "dominated by the smallest $r_{ab}$". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917); [arXiv:1604.03956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03956)
- Shape complexity: $C_S:=\ell_{\rm rms}/\ell_{\rm mhl}$. The reply's eq. (1) gives $C=-\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}^{5/2}}\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}}\,V_{\rm New}$. $C_S$ "is a good measure of non-uniformity or clustering". $\ell_{\rm rms}$ "changes little if two particles approach each other", while $\ell_{\rm mhl}$ "tends to zero" under clustering. "$C_S$ has minima and saddle points, but no maxima: it is unbounded above." — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917); [arXiv:1604.03956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03956)
- Shape potential: $V_S=\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}}\,V_{\rm New}$ (negative definite). "Its factor $\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}}$ removes the scale dependence from $V_{\rm New}$, so forces derived from $V_S$ can only change the shape of the system, not its size." The authors find it "remarkable that the simplest obvious measure of clustering, or complexity, of a system of mass-weighted points is simultaneously the function that determines the objective behaviour of a universe subject to Newtonian gravity". — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- Lagrange–Jacobi relation: $\ddot I_{\rm cm}=4E_{\rm cm}-2(2+k)V_k$ for a potential homogeneous of degree $k$ (PRL eq. 4). For Newton gravity ($k=-1$), $\ddot I_{\rm cm}=4E-2V_{\rm New}$. "For the Newton potential … and $E_{\rm cm}\ge0$ it follows that $\ddot I_{\rm cm}>0$", so $I_{\rm cm}(t)$ "is concave upwards". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Dilatational momentum: $D:=\sum_a\mathbf r^{\rm cm}_a\cdot\mathbf p^{\rm cm}_a$ (PRL eq. 5), which is half of $\dot I_{\rm cm}$. "The monotonicity of $D$ implies that $I_{\rm cm}$ is U-shaped, with a unique minimum… At that point, $D=0$." "Since $D$ is monotonic, it can be used as a physical time variable $\tau$." — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Exceptions (PRL footnote 3): some solutions reach total collision ($I_{\rm cm}=0$, $D=0$) or parabolic escape ($I_{\rm cm}\to\infty$, $D\to\infty$). These "form a measure-zero set with respect to the canonical Liouville measure on shape phase space". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)

**Dynamics on shape space (scale as friction):**
- Shape-space Hamiltonian in $\tau=D$: $H(\tau)=\ln\!\big(\sum_a\boldsymbol\pi^a\cdot\boldsymbol\pi^a+\tau^2\big)-\ln\!\big(I_{\rm cm}^{1/2}|V_{\rm New}|\big)$ (PRL eq. 6). Shape coordinates are $\boldsymbol\sigma_a=\sqrt{m_a/I_{\rm cm}}\,\mathbf r^{\rm cm}_a$, and shape momenta are $\boldsymbol\pi^a=\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}/m_a}\,\mathbf p^a-D\boldsymbol\sigma_a$ (eq. 7). — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Set $\lambda=\log\tau$ and $\boldsymbol\omega^a=\boldsymbol\pi^a/D$. The Hamiltonian becomes autonomous, $H_0=\log\!\big(\sum_a\boldsymbol\omega^a\cdot\boldsymbol\omega^a+1\big)-\log C_S$ (eq. 10). The equations of motion are $d\boldsymbol\sigma_a/d\lambda=\partial H_0/\partial\boldsymbol\omega^a$ and $d\boldsymbol\omega^a/d\lambda=-\partial H_0/\partial\boldsymbol\sigma_a-\boldsymbol\omega^a$ (eq. 11), with "a non-canonical friction term $-\boldsymbol\omega^a$". The friction, together with "the fact that the potential $-\ln C_S$ has no local minima (only infinitely deep potential wells)", explains "why $C_S$ grows secularly either side of a unique minimum". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Arrow of time: "a dynamically-enforced scenario with one past (the minimum of $C_S$, which occurs near $\tau=0$) and two futures". Numerics with N = 1000 show "clear linear growth of $C_S$ on either side of the central 'turning point'". Using Marchal–Saari asymptotics, subsystems develop asymptotically conserved $E_J$, $J_J$ and $X_J/t$, and an information measure $N(Y,t)=\log_{10}(Y(\infty)/\Delta Y(t))$ grows monotonically ("records"). Isolated Kepler pairs act as emergent rods, clocks and inertial frames ("spontaneous geometrogenesis"). — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Scope disclaimers (PRL): "we make no claim to explain all the various arrows of time". The N-body model is "not… a phenomenological model for cosmology; rather it serves as a toy model". In SD the role of $I_{\rm cm}$ is played by the CMC volume $V$, and York time is "monotonic like $D$". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- Uniformity at minimal complexity: Battye et al.'s numerics (cited in the PRL) "indicate that the minima of $C_S$ for $N\approx10^3$–$10^4$ correspond to extraordinarily uniform (super-Poissonian) shapes". — [arXiv:1409.0917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0917)
- "A gravitational origin of the arrows of time" (arXiv Oct 2013, unpublished per arXiv). The claim is proved "for arrows of complexity and information in the Newtonian N-body problem. Except for a set of measure zero, all of its solutions for non-negative energy divide at a uniquely defined point into two halves". Analogous complexity arrows are proved "in the vacuum Bianchi IX model", but "in the absence of non-trivial solutions with matter we cannot prove that arrows of dynamical information will arise in GR". — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)

**3-body shape sphere (arXiv:1310.5167, §§2–3, Fig. 4):**
- Construction: quotient the 9-D configuration space of three particles by the similarity group (translations, rotations, scalings), following Montgomery. The result, "to which the 3-body collision (not a shape) does not belong, is topologically a sphere with three piercings". In Montgomery-type coordinates $\mathbf w$, the sphere before scaling out has $\|\mathbf w\|^2=I_{\rm cm}^2/4$ (eq. 11). — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- Landmarks: "The equator corresponds to collinear configurations; at three points on it there are two-body coincidences, where $V_S$ is singular with infinitely deep wells. Between them… there are the Euler configurations, saddle points of $V_S$." Points at the same longitude and opposite latitude are mirror images. "For all values of the mass ratios, the absolute maximum of $V_S$ is at the equilateral triangle." For equal masses the Euler points are at azimuths $\pi,\ \pi/3,\ -\pi/3$. — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- So **complexity is minimal at the equilateral triangle** (max $V_S$ = min $C_S$, "a result due to Lagrange"). "All non-maximal stationary points are saddles. In the 3-body problem, they are collinear." For 4 bodies, "the maximum is at the regular tetrahedron, also for all mass values". — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- Central configurations: "The usual definition… is that the resultant force exerted on each point by all the others is exactly in the direction of their common centre of mass. This is equivalent to such configurations being stationary points of $V_S$." J. Barkley found many numerically; "for N = 16, Barkley readily found more than 70 000 in the equal-mass case". For N ≳ 10, shape space "resembles a fairly gently undulating plateau" riddled with deep wells. — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- Big bang analogue: "the 'Big Bang', modelled in the 3-body problem by triple coincidence… The only candidate to replace it in S is the equilateral triangle, the most uniform shape." — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- Measure-zero solutions on the sphere: they "always tend asymptotically to homothetic motion (the shape freezes); the final shape can only be a central configuration… all the stationary points of $V_S$ are unstable equilibria: the Euler configurations are saddles, the equilateral triangle a maximum". The initial conditions "must be doubly fine-tuned". Generic $E=0$ solutions are "drawn inescapably ever deeper into the potential wells" (Kepler pair plus escaping third body). — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- $E>0$ versus $E=0$: $E>0$ adds "a time-dependent effective potential to $V_S$", which "flattens the total potential and allows the solution curve… to asymptote… to points away from central configurations. Any scalene end shape of the triangle is possible." With $E=0$, the topography of $V_S$ "completely determines" the asymptotics. — [arXiv:1310.5167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5167)
- Total collisions on shape space (Mercati & Reichert 2021, *Symmetry* 13, 1712). For $E=0$, total collisions are "the points of central configuration and zero shape momenta". "Even on shape space where scale is not part of the description, the equations of motion diverge at (and only at) the points of total collision"; the singularity is "essential… no way to evolve the solutions through these points". — [arXiv:2109.14959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14959)

**Entaxy, mid-point data, typicality (BKM 2015, unpublished per arXiv):**
- Two entropy-type concepts are proposed: **entaxy**, which "is scale-invariant and decreases as the observable universe evolves", and the sum of ordinary entropies of branch systems, which increases. "It is only possible to specify unbiased solution-determining data at the Janus point." These "mid-point data" live on a space ($PT^*S$) with "a natural bounded measure", so the authors can "develop a rational theory of the typicality of universes". The Janus point is described as a point "of maximum disorder". — [arXiv:1507.06498](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06498)
- Free inertial particles are "the simplest [systems] with a Janus point". — [arXiv:1507.06498](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06498)

### Inferences
- $C_S$ and $-V_S$ are the same function up to the constant $m_{\rm tot}^{5/2}$. The quantity that measures structure is also the potential on shape space, which makes the arrow dynamical rather than statistical (Lazarovici & Reichert make the same point; see Q4).
- For triangle Procrustes work in this repository: the shape sphere of Fig. 4 in arXiv:1310.5167 is the triangle shape sphere of Kendall/Montgomery (shapes modulo translation, rotation and scale, with reflections at opposite latitudes). On the Barbour landscape, the poles (equilateral, two orientations) are global complexity minima, the equator is the collinear (degenerate) set, and the binary collisions are singular points on the equator. That fits the repository's recent observation that fits are sensitive on flat (equatorial) and isosceles (meridional) loci.

## Q4. The debate: Zeh (2016), BKM's reply, Carlip's Viewpoint, the Lazarovici–Reichert review, and philosophical assessments

### Takeaway
Zeh (2016) argued that the Janus-point arrow is "trivial" in conventional terms. On his view the double arrow follows from an improbable condition placed at the start of the calculation, and scale cannot be meaningless. BKM replied that the Janus point exists in *every* solution apart from a measure-zero set, as a theorem of Newton's laws. They argued that statistical intuitions built for *confined* systems do not carry over to *unconfined* ones, and that any entropy-type quantity for the whole universe must *decrease* away from the Janus point. Carlip (2014) praised the result as "an intriguing new viewpoint" but judged that it probably does not "yet" solve the arrow-of-time problem. Lazarovici & Reichert (2020) credit the relational theory with a normalisable measure, under which homogeneous Janus-point states are typical. They criticise the non-canonical choice of measure and the "ad hoc" entaxy, and they show that Boltzmann entropy relies on absolute scale.

## Q5. Later developments (2016–2026) and the state of the programme as of October 2026

### Takeaway
After 2016 the programme split into four strands:
1. **Singularity continuation through the big bang.** Koslowski–Mercati–Sloan 2018, then Mercati 2019, Sloan 2019, Mercati–Sloan 2022 (black-hole interior), Hoffmann–Sloan 2024 and Adamo–Mercati 2024.
2. **Dynamical similarity and contact geometry.** Sloan 2018, then Bell & Sloan 2025–2026 on field theories and GR.
3. **Pure Shape Dynamics (PSD).** Koslowski, Naranjo & Vassallo 2022. In 2025 PSD was extended to full GR, with the claim that Janus-point evolution through the big bang is generic for the inhomogeneous description applied to Bianchi IX.
4. **Barbour's own line.** *The Janus Point* (2020), "Complexity and Its Creation" (2024), and a 2026 series with M. Lourenço and F. Lobo that renames the complexity function the **variety V** and studies central configurations and morphology.

### Inferences
- **State as of October 2026.** The Newtonian theorem (Janus point plus complexity growth for $E\ge0$, $\mathbf L=0$) is not contested as mathematics. What is contested is its interpretation as *the* arrow of time, and whether the typicality measure is canonical. The GR extension has moved from "complexity arrows in vacuum Bianchi IX" (2013) to "Janus-point evolution through the big bang" in PSD-GR (2025). A general result for inhomogeneous GR with realistic matter, deriving thermodynamic, radiative and quantum arrows, is still missing from the sources found.
- Terminology has drifted. "Shape complexity $C_S$" (2014) became "complexity C" (2016) and then "variety V" (2026, Lourenço–Barbour–Lobo). Mathematically it is the same scale-invariant $\propto -V_{\rm New}\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}}$; the 2026 abstract defines V from $\sqrt{I_{\rm cm}}$ and the Newton potential. Barbour & Smolin used "variety" earlier, in a different (discrete, non-gravitational) sense; that is from memory and unverified this session.
- The programme's centre of gravity has moved. Active physics strands are PSD (Koslowski, Naranjo, Farokhi), contact-geometric dynamical similarity (Sloan, Bell), and Barbour with Lourenço and Lobo (Lisbon). Philosophy is carried by Gryb, Friederich and Vassallo. Original SD figures (Gomes, Mercati, Gryb as physicist) have largely moved to other topics.
