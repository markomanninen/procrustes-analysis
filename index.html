Thin lines are the twelve pairings, blue for turns and green for mirror images. The thick line is the one shown. A red tick marks a change of pairing. Wherever two thin lines cross at the bottom, the best fit is a different triangle on either side, and a jump there is not a bug but the answer changing.

Where the fit jumps: the shape sphere

Every triangle is one point on this sphere, whatever its position, turn and size. The two poles are the equilateral triangles, one for each direction of travel. The equator is the triangles flattened to a line. The height of a point is (4/√3)·area / Σ|p − K|². For any pairing, |s|² = ‖z‖²‖u‖²·(1 + nABC · nDEF′)/2, so the best pairing is simply the image of DEF nearest to ABC.

The twelve dots are DEF as each pairing sees it. Relabelling turns the point 120° about the poles or half a turn about an axis in the equator, and a mirror image (marked *) reflects it through the equator. Each dot owns the part of the sphere nearest to it, and the orange part holds ABC. Two pairings tie on the great circle halfway between their dots.

With mirror images allowed those circles never move, whatever DEF is. They are the equator, where a triangle is flat, and three circles through the poles, where it is isosceles with its apex at A, B or C. So the fit jumps exactly when ABC or DEF passes through a flat or an isosceles triangle. Without mirror images the twelve become six, the circles move with DEF, and the jumps are harder to predict.

All pairings, best first

For a pairing, s = Σ z̄ᵢ·u is summed over the centred vertices, with z from ABC and u from DEF. Turning DEF by −arg s minimises the squared distances, and the least error over all pairings is the fit. A mirror pairing replaces u by its conjugate ū before summing.

The GeoGebra construction’s six sums

As in the file. Each sum is turned into θ = atan(y/x) and the one with the smallest |θ| is used. Two things differ from least squares. atan forgets whether s points left or right, so when Re s < 0 the turn is half a turn off. And the smallest turn is not the best fit. The sixth sum pairs u₁ twice, where u₂ was meant.

Why triangles jump, and what stops it

atan instead of atan2

atan(y/x) gives the same angle for s and −s. When Re s < 0 the triangle is turned the wrong way round, and as s crosses the imaginary axis the turn leaps by 180°.

Use −arg s, that is −atan2(y, x). Try “DEF is turned half a turn”.

The best pairing changes

Twelve pairings compete. Where two of them are equally good, the best fit is one triangle on one side and another on the other. The jump is real, and no formula removes it.

Keep the current pairing until another is clearly better, and move to the new fit along a path instead of in one frame. Try “Nearly equilateral”.

A mirror image cannot be turned into

No turn in the plane takes a triangle to its mirror image, so a fit that switches between turning and mirroring has no in-between to show. Swapping vertex labels does not help, since it reverses the order of travel but leaves the shape as it was.

Every mirror fit z ↦ a·z̄ is one reflection about the line at angle arg(a)/2. Show it as a half turn in space about that line: the triangle flattens onto the line and comes back reversed.
